CREW

What the crew do. Scrapper Bots dig tunnels into the pile, haul plush back to the bin and sell it. They grow as they work: more plush per trip, wider tunnels, faster hands.

Bins. A bot unloads at the SORT bin unless you pick a Depot Beacon for it (the Bin button on its row, or aim at it or select it and press ;). Every bin pays the same, a far one is only slower, and a bin that is gone or has no power sends the bot back to the SORT bin. A bot that cannot reach its bin on the battery it has goes to a Charging Station first.

Batteries. Every bot runs on a battery. Below 25% it walks to the nearest Charging Station that has charge and a Power Cable from a live grid (like every machine it draws 1 kW; an unwired one charges nobody). Feed a station Common to Epic plush. With no working station the bot waits at the bin and trickles up very slowly, so build and wire one.

Fuel. Generators and Charging Stations hold 50 plush. With Keep machines fueled on, bots top them up before they unload (even a machine on a catwalk or another floor), an idle bot digs fuel for a machine under 40% full, and when nobody is idle the nearest digging bot is pulled off its dig for one under 15%. Each bot has its own Fuel duty switch on its row. Details in the first card below.

Up and down. Bots walk the ramps, stairs, plates and catwalks you build, climb ladders, call and ride an elevator that has power (they wait at its panel) and go through a door that has power. They never step off an edge on purpose, and a lift or door with no power, or a key lock, is a way they will not take.

Keys. T send the whole crew digging the way you face. Y call the whole crew home. V this panel. Or in the world: aim at a bot and press E to pick it, then aim at a target and press E (generator, charger, sorter, vault, belt, floor, bin, your feet to be followed, or your cart). Esc lets it go.

What the status words mean
  • Digging: at the face, taking plush. Heading out: walking to the face.
  • Hauling plush back: full, walking to the bin (or its drop-off). Unloading: selling at the bin.
  • Walking to / Recharging at a Charging Station: low battery, topping up from the station.
  • Waiting for a Charging Station: out of power with no station that has charge and a cable to a live grid. It trickles up at the bin.
  • Fueling Generator A: handing plush to that machine. Scooping fuel for Generator A: digging plush the machine will take.
  • Following you, Waiting at the bin, Taking plush to its drop-off, Blocked: nothing more to dig that way.
  • Taking the ramp, Taking the stairs, Climbing (a ladder), Waiting for the lift, Riding the lift, Waiting for the door: the bot is going up or down your build on foot. No way up: it found no way there (a floor nothing leads to, a lift or a door with no power, a key lock), so it dropped the errand and tries again in a minute.
  • Called to Generator A: the machine needs fuel and the bot is on the route up to it. Going to charge: low battery, on the way to the nearest open Charging Station it can reach. Heading back down: the errand is over (or you gave an order) and the bot walks back to the ground before it does anything else. Turn on the debug lines with g.botnav.debugRoutes(true) to see the routes.

HOW TO PLAY

The goal and the loop

The warehouse is full of millions of plush, and exactly one of them matters: Il Rotto Supremo. The Dossier computer shows what it looks like, and convincing fakes are mixed in. Find it to win. Or give up and dig out of the exit, 4.9 km east.

  1. Grab plush from the pile and carry it to the SORT bin. It pays you in Fluff (◈).
  2. Spend Fluff at the desk (E or Tab: upgrades) and the crafting bench (E: things you set down with B).
  3. Dig deeper for rarer plush and old clues, then automate: belts, power, bots.

The warehouse is lit from 07:00 to 19:00 and dark after closing. A game minute is two real seconds.

Moving and hands

  • WASD walk, Shift sprint, Space jump, C crouch, F flashlight, K medkit.
  • Left click grabs the plush you look at, instantly. Hold it to keep grabbing until your hands (or cart) are full.
  • Throw with a click while you hold plush, or with Z. With empty hands you throw from your cart when it is within 5 m. Keep the button down for rapid fire. Plush thrown at the bin are sucked in.
  • Walk near the SORT bin and it pulls in what you carry and pays you. Long-Range Suction widens that pull.
  • I opens the inventory. Put tools and building items on the 9-slot hotbar, take one out with 1-9, put it away with Q. An empty slot is your bare hands. The hammer in slot 1 (or X) takes down anything you built and gives it back.

The scoop and vacuum dials

Dials are round gauges either side of the hotbar. Two of them are yours to set.

  • SCOOP (needs Scoop Hands): how many extra plush a grab takes beside the one you aim at. It starts at 3 and moves 3 at a time, from 0 (just the one you aim at) up to the most your hands allow. Press - or =, or click the dial's minus and plus buttons.
  • VACUUM (needs the Plush Vacuum): a click inhales plush in a cone for a couple of seconds. The dial sets how hard, in steps of 10 percent from 0 to 100. It starts at 30. Press Alt+- or Alt+=, or use its buttons. A lower setting narrows the cone and reaches less deep, so you can open a tunnel without bringing the roof down. At 0 the vacuum is off and a click is a plain grab.

Both settings are your own and are saved with the shift. The other dials: left, health, bag, cart, air, dust and power; right, the sensors you own, depth, how far you are from the bay, the survey and the clock. A ring turns amber when something needs care and red and pulsing when it is urgent. Each dial carries its full reading as a tooltip. The compass stays at the top of the screen.

Carts

The Carts upgrade lets you craft a Wheelbarrow, Hand Cart, Pallet Cart, Trolley or Flatbed (24, 60, 150, 400 and 1000 plush). U rolls it out; it follows you and carries plush up to its capacity, and you can throw from it. Park it near the bin or a sorter and it unloads itself. A full cart is hauled to the bin by a free crew bot.

Bins and bin assignment

A Depot Beacon is a second place to sell, set far out so you never walk back. Every bin pays the same. Aim at a bot, your cart, a truck, digger, borer, claw rig, mech, rail station or the last piece of a belt line (or select a bot) and press ; to pick the bin it sells at: Auto, the SORT bin or a beacon. Shift+; copies the choice and E on another machine pastes it. A bin that is gone or has no power sends the load to the SORT bin.

Belts

  • Unlock Conveyor Belts at the desk. Hold B and sweep the mouse to lay a line. With a belt out, you can also press, drag and let go to draw it like a cord: the bends are put in for you, and it is as long as the pieces you hold. Or click to anchor a route, aim to see the whole curved route with its length and cost, and click again to lay it all. Q or right click cancels.
  • Curve pieces are quarter arcs and the plush ride the curve. Chevron arrows on the belt show which way it carries. R turns the next piece or flips a ramp.
  • Intake: a belt close to you pulls plush off your hands and then your cart, 2 a second within 2 m. The Belt Intake upgrade raises that to 256 a second within 6 m. ` switches it off and on.
  • Upgrades: Belt Motors and Belt Overdrive speed belts up. Higher marks (Mk2 to Mk6) unlock in the Machines tab; set one over a belt, or drag with B over a line, to upgrade it in place. Lifts, undergrounds, mergers and splitters follow. A line is only as fast as its slowest piece.
  • Belts are machines: a belt line needs one Power Cable on any one tile (see Power). Without it, the belt only crawls.

The vacuum hose

The Vacuum Hose comes with the Plush Vacuum. It is one powered tube with a single flared mouth at its open start: the mouth pulls loose plush (spilled, thrown, or fallen in a slide) off the floor within 3.5 m, up to 4 a second, and the plush ride the hose at twice belt speed. Lay it like a belt: hold B, drag a cord, or click a route. It bends in smooth curves, and it has no splitters or gates.

Gold ends

When the last piece of a belt or hose route is close enough to feed a bin (the SORT bin, a Depot Beacon, or the bin you assigned), it glows gold, the route preview turns gold, and the hint says to stop there.

Power travels only through cables

  • Generators burn Common to Epic plush: at the base 8 kW a Common lasts 4.5 minutes, an Uncommon 12, a Rare 30 and an Epic 75 (bigger plants burn faster). Legendary and Mythic plush are too valuable to burn. A generator holds 50 plush, and your bots keep it topped up. A small Portable Generator comes with Power Grid; Turbine Upgrades bring bigger turbines and plants.
  • Craft a Power Cable, take it out, click a generator, then click a Power Pole. A generator powers nothing alone. The pole is a hub with no power of its own: dead (dark lamp) until a cable joins it to a generator, live (lit lamp) once it is. Then run a cable from the pole to the next pole or to machines.
  • Every machine needs its own cable: belts, sorters, mechs, fans, claw rigs, doors, lifts, lights, rail stations, scanners and the Charging Station. A machine standing right beside a live pole still sits dark. One cable on any tile powers a whole belt line. The info panel says Not wired until you do.
  • A cable spans 14 m (Grid Range makes it longer, to 30 m). The end under your crosshair glows gold or green, red when it is too far or the sockets are full. Click empty air or press Q to cancel; click the same pair again, or hammer the wire, to remove it.
  • Two generators wired together add their power (or wire both into one pole). M shows supply, demand and storage for the grid you stand by. Batteries, switches and breakers are in the bench's Power tab.

Light and sound

  • Your helmet lamp (F) is always there; Headlamp upgrades reach further. Work Lanterns light a tunnel for good and need no power. Road Flares burn for four minutes and Glow Sticks for ten. Hanging Lanterns clip under a frame's top beam and hang on cables from a pole or generator (one 8 kW generator runs five); they dim in a brownout and are dark with no cable.
  • A shaft dug up and out of a tunnel lets daylight down while the hall is lit.
  • Sounds come from where they happen. Full volume within 4 m, then they fade, and plush between you and the source muffle them. A rig selling deep in a tunnel is silent up in the hall, and the coin of a sale rings at the bin it was sold at. Your own actions stay at full volume.

Bots

  • Hire a Scrapper Bot at the terminal, craft more at the bench (Robots tab: each needs a free bunk). Open the crew panel (V): send the crew digging (T) or home (Y), or aim at a bot, press E, then aim at a target and press E again.
  • Battery: under 25% a bot walks to the nearest Charging Station that has charge. The station is a machine like any other: it draws 1 kW and works only with a cable from a live grid. An unwired one charges nobody and bots skip it. Feed it Common to Epic plush.
  • Fuel duty: with Keep machines fueled on, bots top up generators and Charging Stations before they unload, and an idle bot digs fuel for a machine that runs low. Each bot has its own Fuel duty switch.
  • Up and down: bots walk the ramps, stairs, plates and catwalks you build, climb ladders, ride an elevator that has power and go through a door that has power. A machine that needs a bot calls it along a route up, and the route brings it back down. A lift or door with no power, or a key lock, is a way they will not take: they say No way up and try again later.

Digging and staying alive

  • Dig tunnels by grabbing plush straight ahead of you. A tunnel stands for about 7 m on its own; the deeper and heavier the pile above, the shorter. Past that the roof creaks and falls.
  • Frames are hollow 4x4x4 cubes (2.4 m) that hold the roof around them. Dig the section out first; a frame never digs for you. Aim next to a frame and the next cube snaps on any side. Struts and jacks are quick props.
  • Dust builds in tunnels, and the air goes stale with depth. A respirator, vent fans and Support Fans help.
  • Buried or trapped? You have an air countdown. R or right click punches plush out of the way (hold Space to punch up). Run out of air or health and you die, then wake at the nearest depot (the bay floor if you have built none) with your hands empty. H held recalls you to a depot.

Climbing, slides and avalanches

  • Do not climb the piles. The higher you go, the likelier the plush under your boots lets go. Height, a steep face, a heavy load and repeated climbing all raise the odds. A Slope Probe warns UNSTABLE SLOPE.
  • From about 8 m up, a footing that gives way is a soft slide: the plush flow downhill like a sheet and carry you with them. It does not hit you and it sets you down where it stops. Above 19 m on a steep slope it can be a slab avalanche, a big sheet that lets go from the top down.
  • Climbing Gear and Rope Anchors cut the risk (stake an anchor and everything within 6 m is roped in), but a slide that does happen still buries you. A slide can also bury a friend or a bot standing below it.
  • Buried by a slide: how deep depends on how much landed where you stopped. Legs or waist: walk out the way you want to go, about a second. Chest: punch your way out with R or right click, a few seconds. Under the pile: the air countdown runs, so punch up with Space. The Emergency Air Tank adds air.

Night Shift

At 19:00 the warehouse closes and the lights go out until 07:00. The red button on the stand in the middle of the hall (press E on it, or buy Night Shift at the terminal) keeps the lights on after closing, for the whole team, for good. It costs ◈ 100M.

Care packages

Every 3 game days, and at milestones (Fluff earned from 1,000 up, your first Rare, Epic and Legendary, your first generator, 50 / 100 / 200 m deep, each step of the Plushdex, the first upgrade of each branch), a small courier drone flies in and winches a crate down near the SORT bin. A chime and a toast tell you, and the compass marks the crate CARE (GOLD for the rare gold ones that hold more). Press E on it or walk into it: you get three to five things you can use, picked from what you have unlocked. Whatever does not fit in your pack stays in the crate. Deep underground (more than 40 m from the bin) the crate waits at the bin. The achievements screen (J) has the log of delivered, waiting and upcoming packages.

Notes and the Field Journal

The old crews left paper behind: worker's notes, shelving logs, memos, map scraps, manifests, ledgers, torn photographs and coded messages. E opens remains and supply caches. Notes also come from the barricade at the mouth of an old working, depot beacons set far out, rare plush tags and finished contracts. Many carry a clue about where The One is, always true and narrower the further out you find it. L opens the Field Journal: its Notes tab lists every note and pieces the clues together into a bearing, a distance and a height. Bots and machines that dig beside remains or a cache flag it and stop short; the Bot Scholar upgrade makes bots carry the notes home.

Late game

Put Detector Gates on your lines: they beep green for ordinary plush and sound a red alarm, and stop the belt, if The One rolls through. Anything that reaches the bin without being scanned is sold, and The One cannot be bought back. Build Excavators, Bulldozers and Bucket-Wheel Excavators to move whole faces, and Haul Trucks to carry their hoppers to the bin. A Vehicle Scanner checks every truck load on its way to the bin. A Mine Rail shuttle rushes you home with Backspace. Chalkboards in the hall have the numbers.

Controls

Playing together

Choose Play Together. The host starts or continues a shift and gets a 4 digit code; the friend types it in. One shared warehouse and one shared company: the world, your Fluff (the points), upgrades, crafted items, machines, belts, power and bots are shared, and both of you can use anything. Your hands, your stats and your achievements are your own; there is no separate bag. Only the host keeps the save. Enter chats. Both of you need the same version of the game.

Saving

The game saves by itself about every half minute and when you leave, and Save in the pause menu does it on demand. Continue on the title screen picks it up. Abandon shift (pause menu) and New Shift start a new warehouse and throw the old progress away, and the game asks first. A new version of the game can also wipe every saved game once, on its first load, when the game has changed too much to carry saves over.

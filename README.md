# Rot Factory

A needle-in-a-haystack game in a liminal warehouse packed with millions of brainrot plush.
Find Il Rotto Supremo (576 species and four convincing fakes stand in the way) or dig your way to the EXIT, 4.9 km east.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:5273
npm run build
node tools/pace-sim.mjs   # rough pacing model (exit ~380 h, the plush ~560 h for a greedy player; --no-rail and --all-lines show the shuttle and the unlock lines)
```

## Keys (laptop friendly, the mouse only looks)
- **Left click** grabs what you are looking at (hold it to keep grabbing), click again with plush in your hands to throw one. **F** flashlight, **Z** throw, **P** punch the plush in front of you to dig out of a hole (or hold **Space** when trapped). Walk near the SORT bin and it sucks your plush in.
- **E** use (desk = upgrades, bench = crafting, kiosk = dossier, depots, machines).
- **B** set down the build item you hold (green outline shows where). Hold **B** to lay belts/bulkheads. **1-9**, **[ ]** or the wheel pick an item, **Q** stow, **R** flips ramps, **X** takes something down (you get the item back).
- **Tab** upgrades, **V** crew, **N** plushdex, **L** journal, **J** achievements, **T/Y** send crew digging / home, **Esc** pause, **F3** fps, hold **H** to recall, **Backspace** rushes you home along a Mine Rail.

## Play with a friend
Open **Play Together**: the friend can join from the title screen, but hosting needs a running game, so the host starts or continues a shift and opens it from the pause menu. The host gets a 4 digit code, the friend types it in. No account needed. The host's game runs everything; the guest sees and controls the same warehouse. Shared: the world, collapses and falling plush, money, upgrades, gear, crafted items, belts, sorters, mechs, generators, crew bots, the cart, contracts, time of day. Personal: your hands, stats and achievements. A guest does not overwrite their own save. Press **Enter** to chat. Works best in Chrome, and both of you need the same version of the file.

To send the game itself: `npm run build` makes `dist/index.html`, a single file that opens by double-click.

## Systems
- Lazy chunked 6 km hall (`world.js`), 576 species, old workings with remains and notes (`remains.js`).
- Roof support/collapse rules, slope slides, aftershocks, dust and lungs (`dust.js`).
- Crafting table, belts, sorters, vaults, mechs, generators/poles/fans (`crafting.js`, `logistics.js`, `power.js`), depots with clues.
- **Power Cables** (`cables.js`): craft one once you own Power Grid, take it out, click a generator, pole or any machine that uses power, and a wire follows your crosshair; click a second object to attach it. A machine wired to a node joins that grid even beyond pole reach, and a cable between two nodes merges their grids. Every machine that declares a power demand in a `catalog_*.js` (Rail Stations, the Leveling Pad, doors, lifts, jump pads, lights, signs, arches ...) is a consumer of the grid solver and takes a cable; the hover text says `Powered by cable from ...`, and the Load Meter lists the loads by kind. Max length 25 m (plus whatever Grid Range adds to the pole link), shown in red as `too far`. Click empty air or press Q/Esc to cancel; click the same pair again, hammer the wire or take either end down to remove it (the cable comes back). Green wire is live, orange weak, red dead. Cables save with the game, the host simulates them and a guest draws the host's list. Tests: `__selftest('cables.')` and `__selftest('mp.cables.')`.
- **Power parts** (`powerparts.js`, `power.js`): craft them at the bench once you own Power Grid, set them down with B and wire them with Power Cables (a generator takes 2 cables, a pole 6, a switch or battery 2, a breaker 4, any machine 1; Grid Range adds 2 to a pole). A **Power Switch** joins the two grids it is wired to when it is on (E toggles it) and never cuts a grid that a pole or another cable also joins. A **Breaker Box** trips its whole grid when demand stays over the generators' rated output for 3 seconds: fix the load, then press E on it (Remote Reset does it for you). **Priority Switches** (Priority Power upgrade) shed load on an overload, the highest group number first; E opens a panel with all of them. **Power Storage** (three marks) charges from surplus and covers a shortfall. A **Load Meter** shows supply, demand, storage and a minute of history; **M** shows the same for the grid you stand nearest to.
- **Generator ladder and Hanging Lanterns** (`powerparts.js`, `power.js`, `hanglamp.js`, `catalog_power.js`, tests `__selftest('gen.')`, `__selftest('hlamp.')` and the `mp.` ones): next to the 8 kW Generator (unchanged) there is a **Portable Generator** (2 kW, a small hopper, comes with Power Grid) and, by upgrade, the **Turbine Generator** (1.8M, 6 times the output, 3 cables), **Power Plant** (14M, 30 times, 4 cables), **Grid Power Station** (95M, 150 times, 6 cables) and **Titan Plant** (650M, 800 times, 8 cables), each with a bigger fuel hopper (2, 4, 8, 16 times). Every rung burns plush by the same rules, energy per plush divided by its output, so a plant gets through fuel faster and wants a belt; Turbine Upgrades, Fusion Cores and Dyson Cores scale all of them. Aim at one to see what is burning, the rate and how long the hopper lasts. The **Hanging Lantern** (bench, once you own Power Grid) clips under the top beam of a support frame (aim at a frame, B; four to a frame, the side you face first), draws 1.6 kW so one 8 kW Generator runs five, and lights the plush around it. Wire it with the Power Cable: click the generator, then the lantern, then click that lantern and the next, and so on; a brownout dims the whole line, and E switches a lantern off.
- **Belt marks, lifts, undergrounds and the line planner** (`beltdata.js`, `catalog_belts.js`, `beltparts.js`, `beltplan.js`, belt code in `logistics.js`): six marks of belt. Mk2 to Mk6 move 1.5, 2.25, 3.4, 5 and 7.5 times as fast as Mk1 on top of every belt upgrade (about 282, 424, 635, 960, 1,412 and 2,118 plush per minute at base belt speed; the hover text prints the live number for your upgrades and names the slowest tile of the line, because a line is only as fast as its slowest piece), cost 3, 12, 60, 300, 1,500 and 8,000 per tile at the bench (times 3) and draw 0.03, 0.05, 0.09, 0.16, 0.30 and 0.55 kW. They unlock in the Machines tab: Mk2 (150k, needs Belt Motors 3), Mk3 (1.2M, Belt Motors 6), Mk4 (6M), Mk5 (30M) and Mk6 (150M) after Belt Overdrive 1, 2 and 3. Rails are tinted by mark. Set a higher mark over a belt (or hold **B** and drag over a line) to upgrade it in place and get the old belt back. **Belt Lifts** (24k, Belt Motors 2) stand up to 24 cells tall (down as well: **R** flips it), take one piece per cell, are set to a height with **,** and **.**, and need one **Lift Frame** beside them for each started 8 cells over 8 or they sway and stand still; the shaft is reserved and the hammer hands back every piece. **Underground belts** (60k, Belt Motors 3) are an entry and an exit in line, up to 4 cells apart at Mk1 and 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14 for the higher marks; plush pass under belts, frames and plush pile without carving anything, and the hover text says what lies between. The **Line Planner** (press **.** with a belt in hand): **B** sets the start (or aim at the open end of a line, a sorter output, a mech), aim the end (it snaps to the bin, a sorter or a vault) and **B** lays up to 64 tiles at once, buying what you do not hold at the bench price. **R** or **,** pick the shape (so does the wheel once the start is set): horizontal first, vertical first, around and over (follows the floor, ramps a step up or down), under (underground pairs through whatever is in the way). Corners are quarter arcs and the plush ride the curve. Everything saves, the host simulates (a guest asks for lifts, ends, upgrades and planned lines with commands the host re-checks) and both screens show the same thing. Tests: `__selftest('belts.')` and `__selftest('mp.belts.')`.
- **Mergers and ruled splitters** (`splitrules.js`, `splitparts.js`, `splitmesh.js`, `splitpanel.js`, `catalog_belts.js`, belt code in `logistics.js`, tests `__selftest('split.')` and `__selftest('mp.split.')`): four belt pieces that sit on a belt tile and run at its mark. A **Belt Merger** (Belt Mergers, 450k, needs Belt Splitters; 180 at the bench, 0.2 kW) takes plush from its back and both sides in turn, so one busy line cannot starve the others; its output is exactly one belt of its mark. A **Priority Merger** (2.5M, 1,200, 0.3 kW) ranks its three inputs: the best lane goes first and a lane below it only gets the gaps the better ones leave, so a rare lane keeps flowing ahead of bulk (E sets the order). A **Smart Splitter** (2M, needs Belt Motors 4; 12,000, 1.5 kW) has one rule on each output: a rarity range, one species, shiny, The One, Any, None, Anything else, or Overflow, which only gets what the other outputs refuse because they are full. A plush goes to an output that names it, else to an Any output, else to the Anything else output; a plush nothing takes waits and the line backs up. It can also fill its best output first instead of dealing in turn. A **Programmable Splitter** (40M, needs Smart Splitters; 180,000, 3 kW) takes up to eight rules on each output and a default output for what nothing claims. Aim at a belt and press **B** to turn it into one (the belt keeps its mark and its plush, and the splitter or Smart Splitter it replaces comes back; a Smart Splitter turned into a Programmable one keeps its rules), or set one on the floor. **E** opens the rules panel of a splitter or the lane order of a Priority Merger; **Shift+E** copies the settings and **E** on another of the same kind pastes them, and a guest's edits go through the host. Without power they deal out in turn like a plain splitter or merger. The cones on the plate show each output's rule (and each input's rank), and the hover text lists every rule, what stands at each output and the rate.
- **Earth movers** (`earth.js`): Excavator, Bulldozer, Bucket-Wheel Excavator and Haul Truck, the big late-game diggers and haulers (details below).
- **Furnish** (`furnish.js`, tests `__selftest('furnish.')` and `__selftest('mp.furnish.')`): storage, signs and lights, unlocked in the terminal (Signs and Boards, Storage Furniture, Hall Lighting, Floodlights, Plush Silos, Dimensional Depot). Craft one, take it out and aim with **B**. A **Locker** (wall, 24 loose items) and a **Parts Crate** (floor, 100 stacks of building material) open with **E** and move items in and out. A **Plush Silo** (2,000 plush) fills from any belt that ends at it and feeds the belt on the face you looked at, filtered to one species if you like. An **Output Vault** is a Vault Crate that feeds the oldest plush onto the belt it faces. The **Dimensional Depot** (80M, each one dearer) pools every vault and silo within 36 m, lists them on **E** and pulls a chosen species onto a belt. **Signs** take up to 32 characters, three sizes, an icon and a colour; the **Direction Sign** points an arrow; the **Depth Sign** prints how deep you are and the cheapest frame rated for it. Lights draw from the grid and dim in a brownout: the **Ceiling Lamp** (0.5 kW, switches on under a roof or a frame), the aimable **Floodlight** (6 kW, 25 m), the **Strip Light** (0.15 kW per 2.4 m) and the **Warning Beacon**, which flashes on a gate alarm or a tripped breaker. Shift+E copies a sign style or a silo filter onto another. The host runs everything; a guest edits through the same panel.
- Contracts, crew bots that grow (`crew.js`), grid surges, volatile Razzo plush.
- **Build shell** (`build.js`, unlocked by Foundation Pads, Ramps and Stairs, Leveling Pad in the terminal): **Floor Pads** are flat 4 x 4 cell floors (2.4 m, one cell deep) in every frame material, solid world cells that never fall, take no roof load and hold belts, poles, frames and trucks. Aim at the floor or at a pad and press **B**: pads snap edge to edge to each other and to frames, stack by aiming at the middle of one, and need clear ground plus 4 cells of headroom (dig first). hold **B** (or left click) on a pad, drag along a line and let go to zoop up to 10 in one go for one cost (hold Shift to drag a block up to 5 x 5), or set the count with **- and =** (Shift: the width) and just press B; **R** turns it, **Shift+R** nudges it a cell, **Ctrl** locks it to the world grid. **X** takes one piece down, **Shift+X** the whole zoop, and **E** on a pad puts up or takes down a rail on the edge you aim at. **Catwalks** (1 x 4 thin plates with rails, belts and poles but no trucks), **Wall Sections** (4 x 4 of bulkhead panels, same price as 16 Bulkhead Panels), **Ramps** (one cell of rise over two), **Truck Ramps** (one over three, shallow enough for a Haul Truck) and **Stairs** (two cells over four) attach to the side or top edge of a pad you aim at. The **Leveling Pad** machine (15 kW) digs out a box of plush in front of it and floors it with pads, paying for each pad as it lays it. Everything is host simulated and the same on both screens.
- **Transit** (`transit.js`, `transitmesh.js`, `catalog_transit.js`, unlocked in the terminal by Powered Doors 4.5M, Blast Doors 18M, Elevators 9M, Deep Hoistways 60M and Jump Pads 7.5M; tests `__selftest('transit.')`, `__selftest('elev.')`, `__selftest('mp.transit.')` and `__selftest('mp.elev.')`): **Doors** fill a 4 x 4 gap in a wall (aim at a Wall Section and the door takes its place). Closed they are bulkhead cells that anchor the roof beside them, open they free the way. The leaf slides up in 0.8 s and draws 0.8 kW only while it moves. With no power a door stays where it is and E turns a slow hand crank, so nobody is trapped, and it never closes on a person, a cart or plush. Lock it to power or to a **Door Key** (kept in your pack, never used up), or let the sensor open it for whoever walks up. The **Blast Door** (1.6 s, 2.4 kW, sensor off) seals bad tunnels. The **Elevator** (Elevators 9M, Deep Hoistways 60M) is a big box cab, 4 x 4 cells and 4 high, that you set into a dug opening high up the pile (on the ground, on a pad, or at the mouth of a shaft that is already dug). You dig a hollow 4 x 4 shaft straight down from it and shore it with frames like any tunnel: it never digs, load tracing and stale air apply, and a stretch that runs further from a frame than the tunnel rule allows is refused with the depth where it is not shored. Once the tube is dug the rails run out down it by themselves (up to 24 m below the cab, 64 m with Deep Hoistways, which is the whole 43 m hall), and the cab stops at the home row, at the bottom, and at every landing, a side tunnel or the edge of a pad that opens into the shaft, each with its own call panel (E on it calls the cab). It carries you, a friend, your cart and loose plush at 3 m/s and draws 6 kW only while it moves or the rails run out; E in the cab (look up, look down or level) picks where to go. With no power the cab turns by hand at a quarter speed. It never runs through plush, a wall or a machine in the shaft: when a collapse cuts the shaft it stops at the last clear landing and says where, and the rails run on by themselves once you dig it out. The hover readout shows the reach, the stops, why the shaft ends where it does, the load on the busiest frame and the air at that distance. The **Jump Pad** throws you at 12 m/s at 0 to 90 degrees in 5 degree steps (- and = set the angle, R turns the heading, E on a placed pad steepens it), draws 0.1 kW standing by and 6 kW while it charges, fires 5 times and then recharges for 22 s. The path is drawn while you place it and while you aim at it. A fall onto a **Cushion Pad**, or onto a Jump Pad or the plush pile after a launch, never hurts; a hard floor still does. In a game with a friend the host moves every door, cab and pad and both screens stand on the same floor.
- **Mine Rail shuttle** (`rail.js`, `catalog_rail.js`, unlocked by Mine Rail Shuttle in the terminal, 7M; tests `__selftest('rail.')` and `__selftest('mp.rail.')`): rush back to base from the work face. **Mine Rail** (one 0.6 m cell, 450 at the bench) goes down on the tunnel floor like a belt: hold **B** and walk to lay a line. Pieces join the pieces beside them, bend at corners, branch into junctions and climb or drop one cell at a time (45 degrees); a piece needs a floor and 3 cells of headroom. A **Rail Station** (1.2M) goes on a piece of track: BASE when it is within 45 m of the bin (carts that stop within 7 m of the bin sell their load), FACE beyond it (**E** switches). A **Rail Cart** (2.7M, two seats, 120 plush) goes on the track: **E** with plush in your hands loads them, **E** with empty hands sits you in it, and the camera rides along. **Backspace** is the one key: in a cart it rushes you home along the track (and back out to the face when you are already at the base), beside a cart it climbs in and goes, anywhere within 40 m of the track it calls the nearest free cart and carries you home when it arrives. **E** or **Space** hops off (a fast cart brakes to a stop first). A Rail Station is a 3 kW consumer of the power grid (a pole or generator in reach, or a Power Cable): a line runs at **8 m/s** at full power, slower in a brownout (8 m/s times the grid's satisfaction) and at a hand-crank **2 m/s** with no power; the speed shows in the cart and in the hover text. Carts never overlap: a cart brakes to a halt **1.5 cells** behind the cart ahead (standing, slower or coming the other way) and goes on when it clears, and at a junction the cart nearer to it goes first (ties by cart id) while the other waits a headway before it; riders stay seated and safe, and the hover text says `Waiting for the cart ahead.` Carts that hold each other up (nose to nose, or a ring of carts each waiting for the next) sort it out after 8 s: the one with the highest id backs out and takes another way round, or with no other way it stops and says `Blocked by a cart coming the other way.` (**Backspace** sends it again). A cut or blocked track (hammer a piece out, plush falling into the tunnel) stops the cart where it is, a cart whose track is pulled out from under it derails (**E** sets it back), and the way home says so when it is cut. Carts and track save, the host simulates, a guest rides with synced positions through rows and asks with a `rail` command, and two players can ride one cart or two on the same line.
- **Two tunnel classes: frame cubes and giant arches, the Portal, haul roads and truck docks** (`arches.js`, `portal.js`, `haul.js`, `catalog_haul.js`, the arch branch of `loadtrace.js`, tests `__selftest('arch.')`, `portal.`, `haul.`, `cavein.`, `mp.arch.`, `mp.portal.` and `mp.haul.`). A **normal tunnel** is a run of 4 x 4 x 4 frame cubes: 3 x 3 cells clear between the pillars and under the beams, enough for the Mine Rail cart and its shuttle (with a rider), belts, belt lifts, Support Fans and you. A **giant tunnel** is a run of **arches**: modules 4 cells deep like a cube and 6, 8 or 12 cells wide, in any frame material you own. The **Haul Arch** (6 wide, 5 high, clear 5 x 4 cells) takes one truck lane, an Excavator or a Bulldozer; the **Wide Arch** (8 x 6, clear 7 x 5) two lanes or a Bucket-Wheel Excavator; the **Cathedral Arch** (12 x 8, clear 11 x 7) anything. Wide Arches (2.5M, needs Steel Frames) unlock the first two, Cathedral Arches (60M, needs Concrete Lining, and a Haul Truck for its bench rows) the third. At the bench an arch costs its frame's price times 4, 9 or 22, times 3, and uses stock of its material first at the frame's 30% discount. Dig the section out first (an arch never digs), aim at the tunnel floor and press **B**; the next one snaps a whole module along it. An arch is a real support: it anchors the roof around its centre (reach 4.65, 6.2 and 9.3 m, or the frame's reach if that is more), carries the same load model as a frame and is rated shallower the wider it is (85%, 70% and 55% of the frame's depth, so a Steel Wide Arch is rated to 266 m and a Concrete Wide Arch to 560 m); every tier of every span is at 100% load at its rating, an arch that would buckle where you aim is refused with the load it would carry, and the hammer shows a placed one's live load. A Support Fan clamps under an arch like a frame. A **Haul Truck** needs 5 x 4 cells clear (its 3 x 3 cells plus a cell of margin), so it drives through arches and never through cubes: its router searches the floor cells and says what blocks it ("route blocked by the Steel frame at (-7, 1): too narrow (3 x 3 cells clear, a Haul Truck needs 5 x 4)"). In a tunnel the truck drives on the tunnel floor, not over the top of the pile. The **Portal** (Portal Driver, 45M, needs Wide Arches): a giant arch set at the mouth of the pile (open on one side, the pile wall on the other) starts a powered auto-driver (150, 260 or 480 kW by span) that bores the tunnel to the arch's size one slab at a time (it sells what it cuts like a borer) and, every 4 slabs, sets the next arch behind it, paying Fluff for the cheapest tier you own that the load tracing says will hold with the next section cut as well. Where none holds it halts with "the mountain presses N%" and goes on by itself when you add a stronger tier or supports; it chokes on stale air (hang a Support Fan under the arches), stops at a wall or machine in its way, cuts out what fell into an open section, saves and loads mid bore, and never cuts The One's cell unless a powered Vehicle Scanner is on the line from its mouth to the bin (then it hands The One to the scanner: the alarm, E takes it). **E** on a Portal parks or starts it. **Haul Roads** (1.5M, needs the Haul Truck): 2.4 m road plates (24 at the bench) laid edge to edge by holding **B** and walking; a truck on one drives 1.4 times as fast and the router prefers roads. **Truck Docks** (Truck Docks, 6M; 220,000 at the bench): a 4 x 6 cell pad where a parked truck charges its battery at 25 kW; with a belt (or a sorter or vault) at a short end it is a place trucks unload onto the belt, at the belt's speed and never The One, instead of the bin. Trucks now carry a battery (120,000 kJ, +50% per Dispatch Radio level; driving draws 40 kW, the yard charges it at 25 kW, a trip it cannot finish waits to charge); one that runs dry stops where it is and a **Charge Pack** (3,000 at the bench, E on the truck) puts 30% back. Everything saves, the host simulates and a guest draws the same arches, Portal progress (a 0.5 s row), roads, docks and trucks, and asks through commands the host rebuilds from a few integers.
- **Cave-in tuning for big tunnels** (wave 6, `world.js`): the search for the nearest anchor gave up after a fixed 500 cells, so a wide room ran out of cells long before a narrow tunnel did (a 24 wide tunnel fell at 16 cells with Pile Tamping at the top level where a 2 wide one stood 25). The allowance now grows with the reach it is asked for (500 plus 70 per cell of safe length): the same safe length at every width, a lined cube tunnel and a lined arch tunnel of every span stand at the spacing their supports cover (cubes every 8 cells, arches every 12) from the bay to 500 m and past, and the same tunnels dug without supports still come down. The numbers and screenshots are in `DESIGN_SATISFACTORY.md` (section 13).
- Adaptive resolution keeps the frame rate near 60.

## Notes
- **Detector gates**: build one early and attach it to a belt. Everything passing through is scanned: green beep normally, red alarm and a held item when the rare plush is on board. Crew bots check in at the nearest gate before unloading.
- **Detector Arch** (`src/detector.js`, `src/catalog_detector.js`, tests `__selftest('detector.')` and `__selftest('mp.detector.')`): a walk-through arch you carry plush through. Unlock **Detector Arch** (120k, needs the Detector Gate), craft one at the bench (12,000) or the **Giant Detector Arch** (unlock 3M, bench 220,000: 4.8 m by 4.2 m, a lit crown panel that shows its target, a 6 m crowd plaza, a louder buzz with a low thunk, clears carts, bots and Haul Trucks). Set it down with **B** (it faces the way you look, so you walk through it; near a frame it lines up with the frame's 4 x 4 section). Nothing you carry matches its target: a soft negative buzz, only if you carry something (an empty bag gets a quiet tick). A match: a two note da-ding, green and gold lamps and a MATCH toast. **E** on it opens the target panel: **The One** (a match is the win, same flow as the other detectors), **a species** (picked from your Plushdex and your contract species), **a rarity** (and better, or that rarity only), **new to the dex** (a species you have handled once or never) or **shiny**. Volume and a Quiet switch (no buzz, the da-ding stays) sit in the same panel; **Shift+E** copies the settings to another arch. It scans you without power (a battery buffer); the lamps and crown need a pole or generator in reach (1.5 kW, giant 4 kW). Carts within 4 m (giant 6 m) count. Each player's own crossing is detected on their own screen; the other screen shows the same lamps and plays the same cue, scaled by distance (full inside 8 m, silent at 60 m). Belt gates and crew bots are unchanged (arch bot check-in comes in a later pass).
- **Vehicle Scanner** (`src/vehiclescan.js`, `src/catalog_scan.js`, tests `__selftest('vscan.')` and `__selftest('mp.vscan.')`): heavy equipment never leaves with The One. Unlock **Vehicle Scanner** (12M, needs the Giant Detector Arch), craft one at the bench (6M, 35% more for each one you run, 14 kW): a steel arch 7.2 m wide and 6 m tall whose footings are sunk 0.6 m into the floor, big enough for every truck and digger. Set it across the truck road with **B** (it faces the way you look) near a pole or generator. Every Haul Truck routes through the nearest powered scanner on its way from the digger to the bin, stops under it for a moment and is scanned: a clean load gets a soft tick and drives on. A load that holds The One is dumped on the ground at the arch (the whole load, poured out in about four seconds), the scanner keeps The One on a pedestal and the alarm runs like a detector gate: red lamps, a beacon and a red column, the klaxon, a `!!` on the compass. Every truck routed through it waits in line until you press **E** on the scanner (taking The One is the win). Without a powered scanner on its road a truck that has The One aboard refuses to leave and says so (a toast and its hover text); **E** on the truck takes The One out. Hover text shows power, loads scanned and the last scan. The host simulates; a guest sees the same alarm, lamps and trucks and presses **E** through a command the host checks (the friend must stand there).
- **Physics**: loose plush use fixed-step contact physics with friction, spin and air drag, so piles hold shallow slopes and slide on steep ones.
- **F** toggles your helmet flashlight.
- **Trapped?** A pulsing 60 second air countdown starts. **P** punches ahead, hold **Space** to punch straight up. Run out and you pass out at a depot. The Emergency Air Tank upgrade adds 30 s per level.
- Creaking roofs now shed plush, and cave-ins bring down the pile above.
- **Dynamite**: cheap early upgrade. Craft at the bench, aim and press B. 4 second fuse, small blast (bigger than nothing, smaller than a Charge).
- **Joining**: host gets a 4 digit code, the friend types it under Join a friend (uses the free PeerJS broker to meet, then a direct connection). Long copy/paste codes remain under the details drop-down as a fallback. If the two builds differ, both players get a warning to hard refresh (Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+R).
- **Health and death**: a health bar (bottom left) and an air bar that appears when you are buried. Falling plush, long falls and close blasts hurt. Run out of air and you suffocate. Dying is not the end: you wake up on the sorting bay floor and what you carried spills where it happened.
- **Avalanches are real**: cave-in debris can land on you and bury you. Punch out (R, right click, P, or hold Space).
- **Controls**: left click grabs. R or right click punch. F flashlight (O also works). Throwing Arm now has six levels.
- **Building materials**: frames are made from Lumber, then Steel, Concrete, Rebar and so on. Buy stock at the bench (cheaper in bulk), or find it in caches and old workings. Short on stock, the shortfall is bought at full price.

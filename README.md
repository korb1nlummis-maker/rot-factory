# Rot Factory

A needle-in-a-haystack game in a liminal warehouse packed with millions of brainrot plush.
Find Il Rotto Supremo (576 species and four convincing fakes stand in the way) or dig your way to the EXIT, 4.9 km east.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:5273
npm run build
node tools/pace-sim.mjs   # rough pacing model (exit ~380 h, the plush ~560 h for a greedy player; --no-rail and --all-lines show the shuttle and the unlock lines)
```

## Keys (laptop friendly, the mouse only looks)
- **Left click** grabs what you are looking at (hold it to keep grabbing), click again with plush in your hands to throw one. **F** flashlight, **Z** throw, **P** punch the plush in front of you to dig out of a hole (or hold **Space** when trapped). Walk near the SORT bin and it sucks your plush in.
- **E** use (desk = upgrades, bench = crafting, kiosk = dossier, depots, machines).
- **-** and **=** set how many plush a grab scoops (the SCOOP dial); **[** and **]** set how hard the vacuum pulls with bare hands (the VACUUM dial; with a tool out they step the hotbar, as do the wheel and the number keys while the vacuum is in hand). Both dials have minus and plus buttons you can click.
- **B** set down the build item you hold (green outline shows where). Hold **B** to lay belts, hose pieces and bulkheads (sweep the mouse and the cells in between are laid too, so a curve stays one connected line). With a belt or a Vacuum Hose out, **left click** is the laying mode: click to anchor a route (at the open end of a line if you aim at one), aim to see the whole curved route with its length and cost (it turns gold when its end feeds a bin), click again to lay all of it, **Q** or right click to cancel. Or **press, drag and let go** to draw it out like a cord: the bends are put in for you, the cord is as long as the pieces you hold (the preview stops where they run out), and the next press at its open end carries on and turns the way you aim. **1-9**, **[ ]** or the wheel pick an item, **Q** stow, **R** flips ramps (and turns the next belt or hose piece a quarter turn), **X** takes something down (you get the item back).
- Belt intake has no key: once you own Conveyor Belts, belts close to you always pull plush off your hands first, then your cart (2 a second within 2 m, much more with the Belt Intake upgrade).
- **B** with bare hands, a cable, a cart or a supply item in hand (anything that is not a building item) opens the bins panel for what you aim at: pick the bin a bot, your cart, a machine or the end of a belt sells at (**Shift+;** copies it).
- **Tab** upgrades, **V** crew, **N** plushdex, **L** journal, **J** achievements, **T/Y** send crew digging / home, **Esc** pause, **F3** fps (in co-op also the net numbers: KB and messages a second by type in and out, the biggest message, round trip, what the channel still holds and the quality level), hold **H** to recall, **Backspace** rushes you home along a Mine Rail.

## Play with a friend
Open **Play Together**: the friend can join from the title screen, but hosting needs a running game, so the host starts or continues a shift and opens it from the pause menu. The host gets a 4 digit code, the friend types it in. No account needed. The host's game runs everything; the guest sees and controls the same warehouse. Shared: the world, collapses and falling plush, money (Fluff), upgrades and unlocks, gear levels, belts, sorters, mechs, generators, every machine, crew bots, contracts, time of day. Personal: your own pack (the things you craft or find, your hotbar, the tool in your hand and its dials, your hands and your cart; see Per-player inventories below), your stats, Plushdex and achievements. A guest does not overwrite their own save. Press **Enter** to chat. Works best in Chrome, and both of you need the same version of the file.

To send the game itself: `npm run build` makes `dist/index.html`, a single file that opens by double-click.

## Systems
- Lazy chunked 6 km hall (`world.js`), 576 species, old workings with remains and notes (`remains.js`).
- Roof support/collapse rules, slope slides, aftershocks, dust and lungs (`dust.js`).
- Crafting table, belts, sorters, vaults, mechs, generators/poles/fans (`crafting.js`, `logistics.js`, `power.js`), depots with clues.
- **The Crafting Table browser** (`bench.js`, `bench.css`, rows from `crafting.js` and every `catalog_*.js`, tests `__selftest('bench.')` and `__selftest('mp.bench.')`): E at the bench opens a tab strip (ALL, Supports and Frames, Building, Belts and Logistics, Power, Machines and Vehicles, Robots, Tools and Gear, Carts and Transit, Furnish, Locked) with counts (ready/total), a search box (names and text, every word must match, just start typing or press `/`), a grid of compact cards and a detail pane with the description, how to use, what it needs and the batch buttons. Arrows move, Enter crafts 1, Shift+Enter crafts a batch, `[` `]` change tab, Tab moves between controls; the last tab is remembered. Rows you cannot craft yet are greyed with what unlocks them (found by trying every upgrade level against the recipe lists, so a new catalog row needs no list here, only a kind in `KIND_CAT` of `bench.js`; a test fails until it is filed). **Scrapper Bots are crafted there**: the Scrapper Bot upgrade hatches the first bot, More Scrappers and the Bot Foundry add bunks (`T.crewMax`), and each bot fills a bunk for half of what that upgrade level cost, so the price rises with every bot owned (`botPrice` in `crafting.js`; a guest sends the normal `craft` command and the host spawns).
- **Power travels only through cables** (`cables.js`, `power.js`, `gridinfo.js`, tests `__selftest('wire.')`, `__selftest('cables.')`, `__selftest('mp.wire.')` and the audit `__selftest('wires.audit.')`): there is no range link, no plug-in reach and no faint blue link line. Craft a Power Cable once you own Power Grid, take it out, click a generator, pole or any machine that uses power, and a wire follows your crosshair; click a second object to attach it. The end under your crosshair glows gold (a valid first end), green (a valid second end) or red (too far, or no free socket) and the hint counts the cables you have left. **Generators** power nothing until they are wired. A **Power Pole** is a hub with no power of its own (ten sockets): its lamp is dark and it reads DEAD until a cable path joins it to a generator (or a charged battery), then it is LIVE and you run more wire from it to machines or to other poles. The normal build is generator, cable, pole, cables to machines. **Every machine needs its own cable** to a node (generator, pole, battery or breaker): the claw rig, the mech, the borer, fans, sorters, lights, doors, lifts, rail stations, arches, scanners, the Charging Station (1 kW) and the rest all sit dark next to a live pole until a cable runs to them, and their readout says `Not wired: run a cable to it`. The one exception that keeps belts practical: a **connected belt line** (belt tiles that hand plush to one another: ramps, lifts, underground tiles, hoses, splitters and mergers included, `beltLine` in `cables.js`) is ONE load and ONE cable endpoint. A cable on any tile powers the whole line, the line draws the sum of its tiles, a line takes one cable, and the readout says `Line of N tiles powered through a cable at tile X` or `Not wired`. **Two generators add together**: a cable between two generators (or both into one pole) merges them into one grid whose supply is the sum, for every rung, battery and breaker; the readout on a generator or pole shows how many generators are wired together, the supply, the demand and each generator's share (a generator burns its own fuel at its own output whenever it has fuel, in auto mode, whatever the grid wants, and the grid sums what burns; the share is the part of its output the demand takes). Sockets: generator 4 (Portable 2, Turbine 4, Plant 5, Station 6, Titan 8), pole 10, battery 4, breaker 6, switch 2, hanging lantern 2 (a string of lanterns hangs on one node), any other machine 1. A cable spans 14 m; Grid Range (+4 m a level) and Superconducting Poles (+8 m a level) only lengthen it (`T.cableLen`, shown red as `too far`). Each node and each cable end shows a small socket with a lamp (green live, orange weak, red dead); a machine with no cable yet shows a gold socket lamp near you. Click empty air or press Q/Esc to cancel; click the same pair again, hammer the wire or take either end down to remove it (the cable comes back). Green wire is live, orange weak (it throws sparks), red dead. The first generator you place says it needs a wire. Cables save with the game (an old save whose grids relied on range simply loads unwired), the host simulates them and solves the grids, a guest draws the host's list and sends a `cable` command the host checks (real ids, a place within reach of one end, length, sockets, belt line, cable stock). Every catalog machine that declares a power demand is a consumer of the grid solver, and the Load Meter lists the loads by kind.
- **Power parts** (`powerparts.js`, `power.js`): craft them at the bench once you own Power Grid, set them down with B and wire them with Power Cables (a generator takes 4 cables, a pole 10, a switch 2, a battery 4, a breaker 6, any machine 1). A **Power Switch** joins the two grids it is wired to when it is on (E toggles it) and never cuts a grid that another cable path also joins. A **Breaker Box** trips its whole grid when demand stays over the generators' rated output for 3 seconds: fix the load, then press E on it (Remote Reset does it for you). **Priority Switches** (Priority Power upgrade) shed load on an overload, the highest group number first; E opens a panel with all of them. **Power Storage** (three marks) charges from surplus and covers a shortfall. A **Load Meter** shows supply, demand, storage and a minute of history; **M** shows the same for the grid you stand nearest to.
- **Generator ladder and Hanging Lanterns** (`powerparts.js`, `power.js`, `hanglamp.js`, `catalog_power.js`, tests `__selftest('gen.')`, `__selftest('hlamp.')` and the `mp.` ones): next to the 8 kW Generator (unchanged) there is a **Portable Generator** (2 kW, holds 50 plush like the Generator, comes with Power Grid) and, by upgrade, the **Turbine Generator** (1.8M, 6 times the output, 4 cables), **Power Plant** (14M, 30 times, 5 cables), **Grid Power Station** (95M, 150 times, 6 cables) and **Titan Plant** (650M, 800 times, 8 cables), each with a bigger fuel hopper (2, 4, 8, 16 times). Every rung burns plush by the same rules, energy per plush divided by its output, so a plant gets through fuel faster and wants a belt; Turbine Upgrades, Fusion Cores and Dyson Cores scale all of them. Aim at one to see what is burning, the rate and how long the hopper lasts. The **Hanging Lantern** (bench, once you own Power Grid) clips under the top beam of a support frame (aim at a frame, B; four to a frame, the side you face first), draws 1.6 kW so one 8 kW Generator runs five, and lights the plush around it. Wire it with the Power Cable: click a pole or generator, then the lantern, then click that lantern and the next, and so on (the string must end on a node); a brownout dims the whole string, and E switches a lantern off.
- **Belt marks, lifts, undergrounds and the line planner** (`beltdata.js`, `catalog_belts.js`, `beltparts.js`, `beltplan.js`, belt code in `logistics.js`): six marks of belt. Mk2 to Mk6 move 1.5, 2.25, 3.4, 5 and 7.5 times as fast as Mk1 on top of every belt upgrade (about 282, 424, 635, 960, 1,412 and 2,118 plush per minute at base belt speed; the hover text prints the live number for your upgrades and names the slowest tile of the line, because a line is only as fast as its slowest piece), cost 3, 12, 60, 300, 1,500 and 8,000 per tile at the bench (times 3) and draw 0.03, 0.05, 0.09, 0.16, 0.30 and 0.55 kW. They unlock in the Machines tab: Mk2 (150k, needs Belt Motors 3), Mk3 (1.2M, Belt Motors 6), Mk4 (6M), Mk5 (30M) and Mk6 (150M) after Belt Overdrive 1, 2 and 3. Rails are tinted by mark. Set a higher mark over a belt (or hold **B** and drag over a line) to upgrade it in place and get the old belt back. **Belt Lifts** (24k, Belt Motors 2) stand up to 24 cells tall (down as well: **R** flips it), take one piece per cell, are set to a height with **,** and **.**, and need one **Lift Frame** beside them for each started 8 cells over 8 or they sway and stand still; the shaft is reserved and the hammer hands back every piece. **Underground belts** (60k, Belt Motors 3) are an entry and an exit in line, up to 4 cells apart at Mk1 and 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14 for the higher marks; plush pass under belts, frames and plush pile without carving anything, and the hover text says what lies between. The **Line Planner** (press **.** with a belt or a hose in hand): **B** sets the start (or aim at the open end of a line, a sorter output, a mech), aim the end (it snaps to the bin, a sorter or a vault) and **B** lays up to 64 tiles at once, buying what you do not hold at the bench price. **R** or **,** pick the shape (so does the wheel once the start is set): horizontal first, vertical first, around and over (follows the floor, ramps a step up or down), under (underground pairs through whatever is in the way). The **laying mode** is the same router with the build gun's feel (`beltplan.js`, tests `belts.route-` and `hose.route.`): with a belt or a hose out, **left click** anchors a route (aim at the open end of a line and it carries on in that line's direction; aim at a sorter output or behind a mech and it starts there), the preview draws the whole route with quarter arcs at the corners and its length, rate and cost in the hint (the last piece turns gold when it is in suck range of a bin, and the hint says to stop there), another click lays all of it and carries on from its new end (it stops by itself at a bin, a sorter, a vault or a generator), and the route picks its own shape (the L that does not turn back on the line, else the other L, else around) until you press **R**. **Q**, right click or another tool puts it down. Corners are quarter arcs and the plush ride the curve. Everything saves, the host simulates (a guest asks for lifts, ends, upgrades and planned lines with commands the host re-checks) and both screens show the same thing. Tests: `__selftest('belts.')` and `__selftest('mp.belts.')`.
- **Mergers and ruled splitters** (`splitrules.js`, `splitparts.js`, `splitmesh.js`, `splitpanel.js`, `catalog_belts.js`, belt code in `logistics.js`, tests `__selftest('split.')` and `__selftest('mp.split.')`): four belt pieces that sit on a belt tile and run at its mark. A **Belt Merger** (Belt Mergers, 450k, needs Belt Splitters; 180 at the bench, 0.2 kW) takes plush from its back and both sides in turn, so one busy line cannot starve the others; its output is exactly one belt of its mark. A **Priority Merger** (2.5M, 1,200, 0.3 kW) ranks its three inputs: the best lane goes first and a lane below it only gets the gaps the better ones leave, so a rare lane keeps flowing ahead of bulk (E sets the order). A **Smart Splitter** (2M, needs Belt Motors 4; 12,000, 1.5 kW) has one rule on each output: a rarity range, one species, shiny, The One, Any, None, Anything else, or Overflow, which only gets what the other outputs refuse because they are full. A plush goes to an output that names it, else to an Any output, else to the Anything else output; a plush nothing takes waits and the line backs up. It can also fill its best output first instead of dealing in turn. A **Programmable Splitter** (40M, needs Smart Splitters; 180,000, 3 kW) takes up to eight rules on each output and a default output for what nothing claims. Aim at a belt and press **B** to turn it into one (the belt keeps its mark and its plush, and the splitter or Smart Splitter it replaces comes back; a Smart Splitter turned into a Programmable one keeps its rules), or set one on the floor. **E** opens the rules panel of a splitter or the lane order of a Priority Merger; **Shift+E** copies the settings and **E** on another of the same kind pastes them, and a guest's edits go through the host. Without power they deal out in turn like a plain splitter or merger. The cones on the plate show each output's rule (and each input's rank), and the hover text lists every rule, what stands at each output and the rate.
- **Scoop dial** (`src/dials.js`, `src/game.js` `scoopNow`/`adjustScoop`, tests `__selftest('hands.scoop-')` and `__selftest('vacdial.scoop-')`): with Scoop Hands the SCOOP dial by the hotbar shows how many plush a grab takes besides the one you aim at, out of the most your hands allow (Scoop Hands 1 / 3 / 6 / 12, with Bucket Hands 28 / 52 / 102 / 212). It starts at 3. Its **minus and plus buttons** (click, or tap on a phone) or the **-** and **=** keys change it 3 at a time, down to 0 and up to the most; a button greys out at either end. At 0 a grab takes exactly the one plush you aim at. Turn it down to dig a tunnel without bringing the roof down. The setting is saved with the shift.
- **Vacuum dial** (`src/dials.js`, `ui.setVacuum`, `src/game.js` `vacPct`/`vacNow`/`adjustVac`/`runVacuum`, tests `__selftest('vacdial.')` and `__selftest('mp.vacdial.')`, in `src/tests/vacuum_dial.js`): with the Plush Vacuum the VACUUM dial sits beside the SCOOP dial and shows the suction in use, in plush a second, out of the most you own (3 / 5 / 8 / 12 / 18 with the Plush Vacuum, 118 with the top Cyclone Vacuum). Its **minus and plus buttons** or **[** and **]** (bare hands and the vacuum owned; with a tool out they step the hotbar, and the bare - and = keys are the scoop) change it 10 percent of the most at a time, from 0 to 100, and a small vacuum skips the steps that would not change its rate. It starts at **30 percent** (never under 1 plush a second), so a fresh Cyclone is not a firehose. The setting also scales the cone: its half angle is the setting times the full cone (45 degrees for the top Cyclone, never under 12), and under 100 percent one aim only digs a **suction depth** past the first plush on your aim line (the nearest plush in the cone when nothing is within 0.45 m of the line, say when you aim beside a pile; 0.3 m + 6 m x setting cubed, measured again when you move the aim or every 0.3 s: 30 percent takes the face layer, 60 percent about 1.6 m, 100 percent has no limit), so a gentle setting cannot bore through a tunnel roof in one breath. At 0 the vacuum is off and a click is a plain grab (scoop included), and turning the dial to 0 in the middle of a burst ends the burst at once so holding the grab works by hand. The scoop applies to every plush the vacuum pulls, so both dials multiply. Both settings live in the player's own state: saved with the shift, a guest keeps its own (a guest starts each visit at the defaults) and nothing about a dial change goes over the wire. The test digs a straight 4 wide, 4 high tunnel 20 m forward under 6 cells of cover with the real grab or vacuum, the real stability and island rules and a frame cube half way, at top upgrades: at 30 percent with the scoop at 3 it loses exactly the roof the hand loses and finishes in half the time, at 100 percent it loses several times as much and 100 percent with the scoop at 212 takes the roof away.
- **Earth movers** (`earth.js`): Excavator, Bulldozer, Bucket-Wheel Excavator and Haul Truck, the big late-game diggers and haulers (details below).
- **Belt Intake** (`beltintake.js`, hooks in `game.js` and `catalog_belts.js`, tests `__selftest('intake.')` and `__selftest('mp.intake.')`): a plain belt or ramp tile close to you pulls plush off your hands and then off your cart, the way a bin does. Conveyor Belts already give you Intake Mk1 (2 plush a second within 2.0 m); the **Belt Intake** upgrade (machines, 7 levels, 25K to 390M) raises it to Mk8: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 then 256 plush a second and 2.0, 2.6, 3.2, 3.8, 4.3, 4.9, 5.5 then 6.0 m. The rate is yours in total (a token bucket with a quarter second burst), never per tile, so standing among many belts adds nothing; hands go first, then the cart (parked or following, measured from the cart; a cart you sent to a particular bin keeps its load for that bin). The nearest tile that can still take a plush gets it, so a full belt backs up and the pull stops (a fast intake needs a fast line). Lifts, undergrounds, splitters, mergers, gates and hoses are not pulled onto, belts must be within 1.8 m of your height, and The One and every special id are never pulled. A bin closer than the belt still wins; a belt that is closer holds the bin back while it can take plush. A build item in your hands stops the hands being drained (the cart still feeds); there is no on/off switch (an old saved `S.beltIntakeOff` is ignored and deleted). The readout of a belt says "Pulls up to N plush per second from your hands and cart within R m", and each pull sends a small flier from your hands or cart, a spark and a soft sound (rate limited). Co-op: the host pulls from its hands and cart and from the friend's cart; the friend's own hands are on their screen, so their game finds the belt and sends the old `feed` command per plush with `pull:1`, and the host re-checks the position (range plus 1.2 m), the species, the rate (the friend's own bucket, shared with their cart) and hands back what it refuses. Hand drops onto belts keep working at up to 10 a second.
- **Furnish** (`furnish.js`, tests `__selftest('furnish.')` and `__selftest('mp.furnish.')`): storage, signs and lights, unlocked in the terminal (Signs and Boards, Storage Furniture, Hall Lighting, Floodlights, Plush Silos, Dimensional Depot). Craft one, take it out and aim with **B**. A **Locker** (wall, 24 loose items) and a **Parts Crate** (floor, 100 stacks of building material) open with **E** and move items in and out. A **Plush Silo** (2,000 plush) fills from any belt that ends at it and feeds the belt on the face you looked at, filtered to one species if you like. An **Output Vault** is a Vault Crate that feeds the oldest plush onto the belt it faces. The **Dimensional Depot** (80M, each one dearer) pools every vault and silo within 36 m, lists them on **E** and pulls a chosen species onto a belt. **Signs** take up to 32 characters, three sizes, an icon and a colour; the **Direction Sign** points an arrow; the **Depth Sign** prints how deep you are and the cheapest frame rated for it. Lights draw from the grid and dim in a brownout: the **Ceiling Lamp** (0.5 kW, switches on under a roof or a frame), the aimable **Floodlight** (6 kW, 25 m), the **Strip Light** (0.15 kW per 2.4 m) and the **Warning Beacon**, which flashes on a gate alarm or a tripped breaker. Shift+E copies a sign style or a silo filter onto another. The host runs everything; a guest edits through the same panel.
- **Co-op traffic control** (`netperf.js`, `netgame.js`, `net.js`, hooks in `game.js` and `world.js`; tests `__selftest('netperf.')` and `__selftest('mp.netperf.')`): everything either screen sends goes through a send pipe that watches how much the data channel still holds (`bufferedAmount`; PeerJS lets it grow to 8 MB, which is what froze both players when a link could not carry the stream). Above 128 KB the visual messages (`pos`, `dyn`, `bodies`, `time`, `shared`: the newest replaces the older, `fl`, `creak`, `slide`, `dynb`, a sale sound: dropped) wait or go, and the reliable ones (`cells`, `sale`, `ent+`, `cmd`, `give` ...) wait in order in a queue and are never dropped. Each visual type has a bytes a second budget (bodies 90 KB, dyn 60 KB, flights 24 KB ...). A level (fine, slow, bad) rises at once and falls back after 1.5 s of a clear link; while it is up the host sends fewer bodies (220 down to 110 and 55), fewer flights, positions, rows and creaks less often, and cell edits at half and a quarter the rate, and a small "Connection slow" badge shows. A ping every 2 s measures the round trip (it counts too: over 1.2 s is slow). The host's bodies are whole numbers (a body is about 50 bytes, not 75), the nearest first, far ones every second to fourth time, with the ids in range every half second so the guest drops what is gone; cell edits go out at most 20 messages a second at up to 8,000 cells a second (a cave-in of 22,500 cells is three seconds of steady traffic, not one 370 KB second), identical edits of one cell are merged; upgrades, gear and items are sent only when they change (and every 3 s); a big factory's belts go in several `dyn`/`dynb` messages and its belt power rows only when they change; the late joiner's world goes out in slices of at most 48 KB over several frames, and never faster than the link takes it. The receiving side (`Inbox`) handles messages at once for 3 ms a frame, then queues them: newer `pos`, `bodies` and `dyn` replace older ones that are never even parsed, queued flights are capped, big cell and entity lists are applied a slice a frame within 3 ms, and a hidden tab or a loading world still drains. **F3** shows per type KB and messages a second in and out, the biggest message, the tick times of sending, receiving, rows and bodies, the buffered bytes, the queue, the inbox and the level. Audit of that work (`audit_lag_*.js`, prefix `lag.`): when more bodies are loose than one message holds, the nearest 60% go every time and the rest of the message goes to the bodies that have waited longest, so every body reaches the guest within a few messages (the guest used to see only the nearest 220 of an avalanche); a friend's position that is not a number, or whose jump after a stall would be a speed over 20 m/s, no longer reaches the plush collisions (a stall used to hit plush at 80 m/s and hurt the friend with the 'hit' it sent back); a forged `spawn` (no species, NaN, over 120 m/s) and a forged cell that is no species (the renderer threw on it every frame) are refused, the host ignores types only a host sends, and the host answers a `take` of a body it no longer has with `nope` so the plush goes back out of the friend's hand; the host looks through a long cell queue for repeated edits only when it has doubled (it was a full scan twenty times a second: 4 ms a frame at 80,000 cells); an inbox holding more than 96 MB of unhandled text closes the link.
- **World sound has a place and fades with distance** (`spatial.js`, `worldsound.js`, `sound.at(x, y, z, class)` in `audio.js`, tests `__selftest('sound.')` and `__selftest('audit_sound.')`): every sound that comes from somewhere in the world is played from there: the coin of a sale rings at the bin it was sold at (a claw rig, belt, cart, bot, truck or rail cart selling deep in a tunnel is silent up in the hall), and so do machine hums, doors, lifts, scanners and detector arches, gate alarms, creaks, slides, falling debris, supports giving way, blasts, fuses, collapses, your bots' chirps and your friend's steps, throws, builds and belt lines (the host hears them from where they happen). Full volume inside 4 m, then a smooth rolloff to silence at a maximum per class (18 m for coins and small pops, 30 m for machines, doors and scanners, 40 to 60 m for arches, alarms, creaks and slides, 90 m for blasts, rumbles and collapses), a stereo pan from the way you face, a low-pass that closes with distance and with every metre of plush between the source and you (a coarse walk along the line, cached for a second, never builds a far chunk of the world; a blast carries through the ground far better than a chirp), and a dip when one of you is under the pile and the other in the hall. A voice limiter (per class, per 8 m square, per 0.25 s, and in total) keeps fifty rigs selling at once from blasting; a coin nobody can hear never holds the coin cooldown (a far depot cannot silence the bin next to you), a voice lets go of its nodes after 8 s, and a source with no place is not heard at all. Your own actions (grab, throw, buy, panels, the coin of plush you threw or carried into a bin yourself) stay at full volume, and so do the announcements that are not a place (the hall's PA chime at opening and closing time, the grid surge rumble, the stings for finding or losing The One). In multiplayer each screen works out the falloff from its own player: the host's `sale` message now carries the bin's position (and `sstrain` the support's) and `boom`, `creak`, `slide`, `sfail`, `swarn` and `razzo` already did, so a guest never plays a sound for a source past its maximum. The machine hum is the sum of the belts, burning generators and running fans around you, faded the same way, by the distance in 3D (`logi.humAt`: a machine 40 m up the pile does not hum in the bay). `__selftest('sound.')` keeps a table of every direct `sound.x()` call classified as own or UI, so a new call that is not in it (or a world sound that skips `sound.at`) fails.
- **Lantern light** (`lanternlight.js`, `shaders.js`, `hanglamp.js`, `machines.js`, tests `__selftest('lantern.')` and `__selftest('audit_lantern.')`): a lantern, a flare, a glow stick and a hanging lantern are small warm lamps, not glowing orbs. - **The slide** (`wedge.js` and `burial.js`, tests `__selftest('wedge.')` and `__selftest('mp.wedge.')`): high on the pile (above ~8 m) the footing gives way, with no hit and no punch. A creak and a sink of the plush under your boots (0.4 s moving, 0.8 s standing still), then the top layer of 2 or 3 columns under your feet (3 to 12 cells) breaks away and **a wedge grows from there**: the moving plush free the surface plush beside and below them (entrainment). A column inside the spread angle (20 to 35 degrees each side of the downhill line, wider on a steeper face, from higher up and with a heavier load) lets go when the plush moving beside it carry enough energy for the depth of the cell: the cohesion of the pile rises with depth (a cell d deep needs `5 + 14 d^2` of speed squared), so the flow strips 1 to 3 cells and cannot dig deeper. Each cell that joins costs energy from the slide's budget and shares the momentum of the bodies that freed it. Every moving body loses speed to friction that grows with the plush round it and the pile already landed under it, so the flow slows, freezes into cells and stacks up from the bottom; the body cap (1,500), the time cap (12 to 20 s) and the travel bound are only a safety net. The energy (height, steepness, load, gear) sizes the slide: small about 40 to 80 plush, medium 150 to 400, big 450 to 900. The wedge is at least 4 times (medium) and 6 times (big) as wide at the bottom as at the apex. The odds come from `game.climbRisk`: `min(0.6, (h - 6) / 45) x (1 - 0.25 per Climbing Gear level) x (1 + 0.03 per carried plush)`, scaled by shoes, springs and repeated stress; Rope Anchors hold the slope; gear also shortens the carry.

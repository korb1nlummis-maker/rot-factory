## 1. What Satisfactory does (new facts only)

Mining (wiki): Miner Mk1/2/3 make 60 / 120 / 240 items per min at 100% clock and draw 5 / 15 / 45 MW. A node purity multiplies output: impure 0.5, normal 1.0, pure 2.0 (hand mining gives 1 / 2 / 3 items). One miner per node, one belt output, all three footprints are identical (6 x 14 x 18 m), the Mk2 and Mk3 recipes consume the earlier miner so upgrades feel earned. Resource wells use a central pressurizer with satellite extractors around it, each marked on the map with hollow (free) or filled (claimed) dots. Miners cannot be blueprinted because they must snap to a node.
Clock speed (wiki): any value from 1% to 250% in 0.0001 steps. Each Power Shard raises the cap by 50%, max 3 shards (250%). Production scales linearly, power scales with exponent 1.321928: 50% costs 40%, 150% costs 170.9%, 200% costs 250%, 250% costs 335.8%. Generators scale linearly. Below the 0.1 MW idle floor a building still draws 0.1 MW. Shards come from slugs (blue 1, yellow 2, purple 5 shards, about 1,242 slugs in the world), later synthesized from endgame items. Overclocking always costs more power than adding a machine, so shards are a space and node limiter, not an efficiency tool.
Build gun (wiki): range 100 m. Modes: build, dismantle (F, full refund, mass select up to 50 with filter), customizer (paint swatches, tier 2), zoop (up to 10, one axis), nudge (arrows 1 m, Ctrl 0.5 m, PgUp/PgDn vertical, lock with H), quick switch (E cycles variants such as foundation size), eyedropper copy (middle click), hotbar search (N). Snapping guidelines: solid color for side alignment, gradient for input/output. Ctrl aligns to the 8 m world grid (1 m vertical), foundations rotate 45 degrees (Ctrl: 5 degree fine when stacked). Foundations in 1/2/4 m thickness cost the same.
Blueprint Designer (wiki): Mk1 32 m cube (tier 4), Mk2 40 m (tier 6), Mk3 48 m (tier 9). The Control Bench names, describes, categorizes and gives an icon to a blueprint, and an attached storage box receives dismantled materials and supplies them while placing. Extractors, the HUB and the Space Elevator cannot be blueprinted.
Containers (wiki): 24 slots (10 plates, 10 rods) and Industrial 48 slots with two ports, both output LIFO, belt tier sets speed, they do not balance across ports.
AWESOME Sink (wiki): 30 MW working, 0 idle, one input up to 1,200/min (Mk6 belt), cannot overclock, points persist if the sink is removed, coupon n costs 500 for the first three then `250 * (ceil(n/3) - 1)^2 + 1000` points, up to coupon 3,000. Shards, slugs, drives and consumables cannot be sunk (they jam the input).
Drones (wiki): Drone Port 100 MW constant, 24 x 24 x 15 m, 36 slots (18 out, 18 in), one drone per home port, fuel is batteries or packaged fuels, tier 8.
Trains and tubes (wiki): train station 50 MW constant, platforms in one straight flat line, 27.08 s load/unload cycle that pauses all belts on the platform, rail doubles as a power line. Truck station 20 MW while docking, 0.1 standby, 8 s dock, fills only the fuel a recorded round trip needs, vehicles carry up to 25 stacks. Hypertube about 50 km/h unboosted (a Mk5 belt), boosters above 500 km/h, segment max 100 m, tubes pass through terrain, each entrance needs power.
Progression (wiki): tiers 0 to 9, tier 0 is six sequential HUB upgrades, tiers 1 and 2 open together, later tiers need Space Elevator deliveries. The MAM runs nine research trees triggered by items in your inventory (not the depot), 3 s per node, Hard Drives take 10 min and grant a random alternate recipe (107 alts, 118 drives), several MAMs share one progress.

What makes it satisfying (design reading): every machine states its rate and a status lamp; a belt's tier is a number you can read and beat; nodes are a finite, mapped set so expansion is a choice; clock and shards are a late "squeeze" knob; blueprints turn a good layout into a stamp; foundations make every build look planned; the sink gives surplus a purpose and a long coupon curve; milestones deliver real quantities of what you already automate.

- Spec 1 mapped: belts tiers, mergers, smart splitters, power switches and storage, pads and zoop, ramps, lockers/silos/depot, signs and lights, doors/lifts/jump pads, haul roads, docks, arches, detector arch, blueprint copy (P1), pipette, copy settings, shift milestones (P2), drones (P2), trains (skipped).

## 4. Specs, prioritized

### 4.1 Vein nodes and Vein Taps (P1)

Satisfactory: fixed, mapped nodes with purity, one miner per node, Mk1 to Mk3 by recipe chain, output on one belt.
Rot Factory mapping: rich veins already exist but only as a drop-rate bonus for diggers. Make them extraction nodes.
- Node identity: `veinNodes(i0,k0)` in new `src/veinnodes.js` scans `veinAt` on a 16 cell (9.6 m) lattice per 64 cell block and keeps local maxima above `VEIN_T`. A node is `{id:'vn:'+i+':'+j+':'+k, i,j,k, peak, grade}` (pure data, deterministic from the world seed, nothing saved except claims). Grade by `peak`: impure 0.80 to 0.87, normal 0.87 to 0.94, pure above 0.94 (thresholds tuned by `tools/vein-grade.mjs`, target share 50 / 35 / 15 of nodes; the script prints the real shares over a 2 km sample so the numbers can be fixed without code review). Multiplier `GRADE_MUL = [0.5, 1, 2]`. Grade also sets rare share: 5% to 17% scaled by peak, pure never above 17%.
- Survey: Vein Assay level 1 meter already exists. Add Survey Stake (item, price 90, stack) that plants a marker on the nearest node within 3 m and prints grade (hollow ring unclaimed, filled claimed, like the wiki's well icons). Assay level 3 lists nodes within 150 m with grade glyph and depth in `info.js`. A claimed node shows the tap's `st` lamp.
- Vein Tap ents `{type:'tap', mk:1..3, node:'vn:...', i,j,k, dir, clock, shards, out:{i,j,k}, buf:[sp], st}` in `game.machines.items`. Placement: aim at a node center inside a dug cavity (the same 4 x 4 x 4 dig rule as frames; the tap sits on a PAD cell at the node's j, so decks from 4.4 apply). One tap per node. A tap is a vertical collar plus a bore head drawn with the borer's teeth mesh. It never removes cells (flag `T.tapRemovesCells = false`; the pile is effectively infinite and a conserved version would exhaust nodes within minutes). It samples species from the vein distribution at its depth with `rare share` above and never returns The One, a decoy or Razzo.
- Rates at 100% clock: Mk1 1.0 plush/s, Mk2 2.0, Mk3 4.0 (60 / 120 / 240 per min, matching the wiki), times grade. Pure Mk3 is 8/s, 480 per min, more than a Mk2 belt (423) so the player must run Mk3 belts (spec 1 4.1); at 250% clock it is 1,200/min and needs a Mk6 belt. Power kW: 6 / 18 / 54 at 100% (earth movers draw 30 to 80, so taps sit in the machine range); `DEMAND.tap` is per mk, scaled by the clock curve (4.2). Standby 0.02 kW.
- Prices before K: Mk1 craft 180,000, Mk2 1.4M, Mk3 9M. Mk2 and Mk3 consume the earlier tap when crafted (refund the old on upgrade in place, like belts). Unlocks: `veinTap` 2.2M (needs Vein Assay 2 and Borer Fleet 1), `veinTap2` 14M (needs Hydraulics at top), `veinTap3` 70M (needs Superconducting Poles 1). `tapMax` count upgrade: 6, 12, 24, 48 taps for 3M, 10M, 40M, 120M.
- Output: `out` is the belt tile in front (spec 1 belts). `buf` holds 20 plush; when full the tap shows `st:'blocked'` (amber lamp). `info.js` prints "Pure vein, Mk3 at 150%: 720/min, belt Mk3 carries 635, line limited by this belt".
- Blueprints exclude taps (they snap to a node), a ghost tap shows the node ring.
- Load tracing: a tap collar is a support-like ent with `kind:'tapcollar'` using the Frame capacity of the best owned frame (reuse the earth mover canopy code in earth.js, called through one exported function `canopyCap`), halts with `Halted: the mountain presses N%` like diggers. Not a load source.
- Files: new `src/veinnodes.js`, `src/taps.js`, `src/machkit.js` (4.3), `tools/vein-grade.mjs`, `catalog_mine.js`, tests `src/tests/vein_*.js`, `tap_*.js`.
- Tests: `vein.nodes-deterministic-per-seed`, `vein.grade-shares-near-50-35-15`, `vein.node-never-in-open-air`, `tap.rate-matches-printed-per-min`, `tap.pure-doubles-impure-quadruples`, `tap.one-tap-per-node`, `tap.never-returns-one-or-decoy`, `tap.blocks-when-out-full-and-lamp-amber`, `tap.halts-when-canopy-would-buckle`, `tap.mk2-upgrade-refunds-mk1`, `tap.saves-and-loads-node-claim`, `mp.tap.row-and-ents`.
- MP: ents sync via `ent+`; `buf` and `st` are transient and travel in the `taps` xrow (id, st, rate). Guests never roll species.

### 4.2 Clock dial and Overclock Shards (P1)

Satisfactory: per machine clock 1% to 250%, 3 shards unlock 250%, power exponent 1.3219, non renewable shards.
Rot Factory mapping: a dial on every powered production machine: tap, rig, mech, borer, excavator, dozer, bucket wheel, sorter, generator output. Not on belts, doors, lights, the sink (wiki: sink cannot overclock).
- Fields `clock` (0.1 to 2.5, step 0.05, default 1) and `shards` (0 to 3). Cap = `1 + 0.5 * shards`. cfg keys `clock`, `shards` (the shards value is bounded by the player's inventory count and checked by `check`, which refunds on lowering). Dial UI: E opens the panel (spec 1 modal hook) with a slider, shard slots and the live numbers: rate per min, kW, kW per plush and "vs adding another machine".
- Rate: every `T.*Rate` consumer divides its action time by `clock`. Implement as `clockOf(ent)` from new `src/clock.js`; each machine's update uses one call (`dt * clockOf(e)`), so existing upgrades (global multipliers) stay multiplicative. Power: `kw = base * clock^1.321928`, floor 0.02 kW. Table printed in the panel: 50% 40%, 100% 100%, 150% 171%, 200% 250%, 250% 336% (matches the wiki). Generators: output linear in clock, burn linear (a clocked generator burns plush faster; fuel hold from spec 1 4.3f still applies).
- Heat: no heat system. Instead a clocked machine above 200% draws `grid noise`: its kW counts 10% more toward the breaker trip check (spec 1 4.3b) so overclocked fleets can trip the grid. Cheap and gives batteries and priority a reason.
- Shard sources: (1) Sink Shop (4.8) 1 coupon each; (2) Spark Cells: glowing cells inside Old Workings caches (id 4096) and remains, deterministic roll about one per 600 m of depth along the hall, 1 or 2 shards, flagged `spark` in the cache table of `remains.js`; (3) bench `shard` synthesis, price `400,000 * (1 + n/20)` where n is shards crafted so far in this save, unlocked by `shardSynth` 9M late (wiki: shards are non renewable until tier 9). A fleet of 300 machines at 3 shards each (900 shards) costs about 4.1B through the bench alone, and the shop and caches offset about a third, which suits 100M+ endgames and makes shards a choice not a default.
- Safety: `stripEnt` keeps `clock` and `shards` (config). A machine whose shards fall below what its clock needs is clamped on load and toasts once.
- Files: new `src/clock.js`, `machines.js` (the three update functions and `DEMAND` scale call), `earth.js` (diggers, one call each), `power.js` (consumer demand multiplies by `clockPower(ent)`; owned here), `catalog_clock.js`, `ui.js` only through the modal hook, tests.
- Tests: `clock.power-curve-matches-table`, `clock.rate-linear`, `clock.shards-cap-per-50pct`, `clock.lowering-shards-refunds`, `clock.generator-output-linear`, `clock.floor-draw`, `clock.overclock-adds-to-breaker-trip-check`, `clock.old-save-defaults`, `mp.clock.guest-dial-via-cfg`.

### 4.4 Decks, thickness and load tracing (P0, supersedes spec 1 4.4 load rules)

Satisfactory: foundations are the grid; 1/2/4 m thick cost the same. Rot Factory spec 1 4.4 states pads take no load. That is correct for floors and wrong for pads that roof a gap (a pad bridging a shaft, a floor between two tunnel levels, a platform built in mid pile). This section replaces the sentence "pads under a roof do nothing for support".
- Thickness: pad ents get `th` in {1, 2, 4} cells (0.6, 1.2, 2.4 m) and keep the same price at each size (like the wiki). Cell kind stays PAD (4095); the ent stores the vertical extent and `deck` flag when any cell of it has a gap below. Thick pads carve more salvage on placement (more plush returned) so thickness is a real choice; a thin pad over open air is the only one allowed to hang (`th 1`, over a dug shaft up to 4 cells wide).
- Deck rules in `src/deck.js`, hooked through `loadtrace.js` (one function `deckInfo(w,i,j,k)` called from `loadOn`, owned by Wave 10 only):
  1. Share cap: a roof cell that is a deck cell uses `DECK_SHARE[th] = {1:3, 2:4, 4:6}` instead of `MAX_SHARE=2` in `Math.min(cap, n)`. A thick deck lets six supports share one cell's weight.
  2. Beam reach: for deck cells `inside()` uses radius `s.r + 0.6 * th * C` (4 cells thick adds 1.44 m), so one frame covers a wider deck without moving it. Calibration (`CAL`) is unchanged because standard tunnels have no deck.
  3. Edge anchor: a pad cell counts in `world.anchored` for roof cells at the same row (spec 1 already adds it) and now also anchors the deck's own neighbors within `th + 1` cells, so a thick deck is a rigid ledge.
  4. Mid pile carry: a support whose foot cells (4 pillars of a frame, 1 of a strut) rest on a deck cell with a gap below is an upper support. Its load `L` is not on the pile column below (the void), so `updateLoads` runs a top down pass (supports sorted by y descending, depth of transfer 4): each deck cell under a foot gets `extra = XFER * L / feet` added to its roof weight for the lower supports. `XFER = 0.6` (to calibrate in test). If no lower support covers it, the deck cell is simply a heavy roof and may buckle the deck: failing decks are removed in 1 cell steps from the loaded edge, lost to the player (not refunded), and the roof releases through the existing collapse code. A buckled upper frame dumps its share onto neighbors as today.
  5. Preview: placing a frame or pad over a void shows both the upper frame's predicted ratio and the deck's lower supports' ratio (call `loadOn(w, s, others)` twice).
- Why this fits the wiki's idea: Satisfactory platforms are what you stand a machine on; here a platform in mid pile can be what a machine and a second tunnel level stands on, but the mountain still presses on it.
- Zoop and snap are as spec 1. Thickness cycles with E (quick switch, wiki). Ctrl aligns to the 4 cell grid (spec 1). Pad groups store `grp`.
- Files: new `src/deck.js`; `loadtrace.js` (the single hook, after spec 1's arch branch), `game.js` only through `updateLoads` registering `deck.pass()` as an extension (Wave 9 adds `ext.pass('loads')`), `world.js` (anchored, one line), `build.js` (th param), `catalog_deck.js`.
- Tests: `deck.thick-pad-shares-six-supports`, `deck.beam-reach-extends-frame-cover`, `deck.upper-frame-load-reaches-lower-support`, `deck.unsupported-span-buckles-and-drops-roof`, `deck.thin-pad-over-shaft-rated-narrow`, `deck.xfer-calibrated-stacked-frames-hold-at-60pct-rated-depth`, `deck.floor-pad-unchanged-load` (a pad on solid floor adds nothing), `deck.old-supports-unchanged` (existing qa.* load tests stay green), `qa.deck-no-load-queue-drops`, `mp.deck.collapse-sync`.
- MP: decks are normal pad ents; the host runs the pass and broadcasts breaks through `sbreak` exactly like frames.

### 4.5 Terraces, switchbacks and high galleries (P0 for terrace, P1 for road)

The need: flat floors, roads and tunnels higher on the slope. High up there is less overburden (`top - j - 1` is small) so frames are cheaper and more rarely overloaded, but footing fails above about 8 m and the face slides.
- Terrace tool (unlock `terrace` 1.8M, needs Pad built once and Rope Anchors 1): plan a rectangle of 1 to 5 x 1 to 5 pad units (2.4 m each, up to 12 x 12 m) at a chosen level `j` (aimed height, wheel adjusts in 1 cell, PgUp/PgDn like the wiki's nudge). Placement does five things in one host operation, `terrace.apply(plan)`:
  1. Cut: remove every cell above the plane inside the footprint up to `H` cells (default 6, max 10), salvaged at 70% sale value (money, not carried).
  2. Pad: lay pads (thickness choice) over the plane, group id `grp`.
  3. Fill: cells below the plane that are void (downhill edge) are filled with embankment (BULK cells, price per cell).
  4. Retain: the uphill face and both flanks above the plane get a 1 cell BULK wall up to the cut height (these are anchors, spec 1 4.4), so no cliff of 4+ cells is left to slide. A `slide.hold(box, 3 s)` call suppresses slides while the edit lands.
  5. Optional `portal:{w,h,side}`: leaves a rectangular opening in the retaining wall for a tunnel mouth (spec 1 arches or frames start there).
- Safe footing: a pad cell and cells within 2 of it count as "rope anchored" in `climbRisk` (one function in `player.js`/`game.js`, owned here) so standing on a terrace never trips footing, climbing between them still can.
- Mountain Road (unlock `mtnRoad` 12M, needs Terrace and Haul Truck): picks a start terrace and an end level, runs an A* on the coarse 4 cell grid and lays ramp pads (spec 1 ramp ent) with grade 1 cell per 4 cells (trucks climb up to 1 per 3), switchbacks where the straight line is too steep, a terrace landing every 8 pad units, strip lights if owned. Cost is the sum of parts shown in the preview with the count of cells cut and filled. Roads are the lane graph of spec 1 4.7, so Haul Trucks and the router (clearance W x H) use them with no extra data.
- High gallery: nothing new to build; `info.js` and the Structural Survey add a line "overburden here 18 cells, same tunnel at floor level 52 cells" so the player sees why climbing pays.
- Costs (before K): pad prices from spec 1, wall or embankment cell `bulkPrice`, cut fee 3 per cell (offset by salvage). A 12 x 12 m steel terrace with a 6 cell cut costs about 9k to 25k; a 30 level mountain road about 400k to 2M. Thickness has no price delta (wiki rule).
- Dismantle: Shift+X on any terrace piece removes the whole `grp` and refunds pads, not the retained wall cells (they stay unless hammered), so you do not trap yourself.
- Files: new `src/terrace.js` (plan, apply, road), `build.js` (a call to place pads in a group; owned by Wave 10), `player.js`/`game.js` (`climbRisk` branch), `slide.js` (`hold` helper only), `catalog_deck.js`, tests `terrace_*.js`.
- Tests: `terrace.cut-leaves-no-cliff-over-4-cells`, `terrace.retaining-wall-anchors-neighbors`, `terrace.no-slide-during-apply`, `terrace.fill-embankment-solid`, `terrace.portal-opening-and-frame-snap`, `terrace.footing-safe-on-pad`, `terrace.road-grade-truck-climbable`, `terrace.road-router-uses-lane-graph`, `terrace.dismantle-group-keeps-wall`, `terrace.survey-shows-overburden-delta`, `mp.terrace.batch-cells-and-ents`.
- MP: one `place` command with `p` (rect, j, th, options); host computes diffs and sends cells plus pad ents in one batch so a guest never replays 2,000 cells one by one. Spectators see the tool ghost via the existing placement echo.

### 4.6 Blueprint Designer, hologram building, build modes (P1, extends spec 1 4.10)

Spec 1 has a plain blueprint copy (cap 200 ents), pipette, copy cfg and nudge. This adds the Satisfactory parts that make blueprints a loop:
- Designer Mk1/2/3 (wiki 32/40/48 m): a placeable box `type:'designer'` of 20 / 40 / 80 cells per side (12 / 24 / 48 m), unlocks `bp1` 90k (spec 1), `bp2` 8M, `bp3` 60M. Piece caps 200 / 600 / 2,000, so a hangar needs Mk2. Only pieces the player owns can be copied; excluded: taps, the sink bin (one coupon counter), the Welcome Gate, bots, trucks.
- Control Bench: name, description, category (folder), icon (from `icons.js`), saved in `S.blueprints[]` (host state, shared by all players because money and gear are shared). Fields `{id,name,cat,icon,size,ents:[{type,rel:[di,dj,dk],dir,cfg}],cells?:[...]}`. `cfg` carries sorter filters, splitter rules, clock, signs, arch targets (`copy` keys of spec 1). Wallet cost of use: sum of parts (spec 1), no surcharge.
- Storage link: a Parts Crate (spec 1 4.5) placed next to the designer or the hologram makes it the supply: placing a blueprint draws materials and items from the crate first, then wallet purchase at full price for the shortfall (Rot Factory's existing "shortfall bought at full price" rule); dismantling a pasted blueprint (Shift+X on the group) refunds into the crate. This removes per piece hotbar fiddling.
- Hologram: pasting shows the ghost with per piece color: green ok, red blocked (reason on hover: solid cells, reserved, not owned, too deep), amber if a support would run above 85%. A "Clearance" toggle draws the free volume each piece needs (excavator 7 x 5 face, truck 4 x 4, bucket wheel 5 x 6, arch `clear`) as thin boxes. Piece placement order: pads and walls first, supports, belts, machines, power, signs last, so each step passes its own check.
- Frames and digging: frames never dig (spec rule). A blueprint with supports pastes into dug space only; the hologram offers `Dig first` which queues an earth mover job over the blueprint's cleared volume (`earth.js workCells`), or the player digs by hand.

## 9. Mine Rail shuttle done: API notes

User request: a cart on a track that rushes you back to base from the work face (fans, frames, anything deep in a tunnel). This is the player facing slice of 4.10 "Shuttle Rail", scaled to the tunnel: single track, no signals, one cell pieces, carts with seats. Files: `src/rail.js` (graph, planning, carts, rows, HUD, readouts), `src/catalog_rail.js` (unlock, bench rows, TYPES), tests `rail_core.js`, `rail_ride.js`, `mp_rail.js` (helpers in `rail_lib.js`), small hooks in `game.js` (import, Backspace in `onKey`, `rail` case in `runNetCmd`, `rail` joins the hold-B paint test, the hammer pick and the readout name), `info.js` (aim pick), `controls.js` (two rows), `catalog.js` (the stub import and `PARTS`), `hall.js` (one more foot line on the TUNNEL CRAFT board), README and the GATE table in `upg_crafting.js`.
- Entities (all `game.machines.items`, tool kind = type): `rail {i, j, k}` has no x y z on purpose (the aim loops skip it; `pickRail` finds it by marching the ray and dipping to rail height inside its cell), `railstn {i, j, k, x, y, z, role 'base'|'face', name, pw}`, `railcar {x, y, z, yaw, pitch, a, b, u, cargo, n, riders, st, spd, dst, why, face, call, home, leave}` with runtime numbers in `cache` and the route in `path` (both in TRANSIENT, so never sent). `a` is the idx of the piece the cart stands on (or left), `b` the piece it is heading to, `u` 0..1 along that edge.
- Graph (`sync(g)`): pieces join when they touch on a side at the same level or one level up or down (45 degree slope). Rebuilt from a signature over `machines.items` on every call, so no add or remove path can be missed (the host hammer, a guest `ent-`, a test reset). `R.nodes`, `R.adj` (flat list key, length), `R.comp` (line id per piece), `R.comps[id] = {n, stns, pw, powered, speed}`, `R.stnAt`, `R.cars`. `bfs(g, R, from, goal)` skips pieces whose 3 cell column holds plush (`clearAt`), which is how a collapse blocks a route.
- Speed: a line runs at `POWER_SPEED` 8 m/s times the best station `pw` (a pole or generator within `poleReach + 1.5` of the station, the Leveling Pad rule: the power solver does not count catalog machines as consumers, so the 3 kW in DEMAND is declared only) and at `HAND_SPEED` 2 m/s with none. Carts accelerate 5 m/s^2 and brake along `sqrt(2 * 6 * rem)`.
- The rush key (`Backspace`, `RAIL.rushKey`): seated, it goes to the nearest base station (or, if the line has none anywhere, the piece nearest the bin; if a base station exists on a cut off line it says the track is cut), and from a base node it goes back to the face (`car.face`, the last face station it left, else the nearest, else the far end). Not seated, a cart within 4.5 m takes you in and goes; otherwise it calls the nearest free cart on the line of the piece nearest you (within 40 m) and seats you on arrival (60 s patience).
- Riding: the host sets `player.pos` from the cart every frame in its `tick` (after `player.update`, and moves the camera to match), a guest does the same with its own player; hop off is E or Space (a cart above 3 m/s brakes over its stopping distance first). Seats are `car.riders` (`'host'`, `'guest'`), a person is in one cart at a time. Carts do not collide: two on one line pass through each other.
- MP: a guest sends `cmd rail {act: 'rush'|'sit'|'leave'|'load', id, items}`; the host checks range (4.5 m of `g.remote.pos`), seats, capacity, The One and returns refused plush with `give`. Moving carts go out as `xrow railcar` rows at 10 Hz (the guest extrapolates along the heading and smooths), everything else with the 0.5 s `row` (stations carry `pw`). Placement, hammer and station settings use the existing `place`, `decon` and `cfg` commands; `conflict` re-checks item id, whole numbers and the cell.
- Closed in the gaps wave (see the section "Gaps closed" at the end of DESIGN_SATISFACTORY.md): carts follow each other (`HEADWAY` 1.5 cells) and take turns at junctions, `logi.canPlace` reads the reserved cells (`logi.cellTaken`), a station is a 3 kW consumer of the grid solver and takes a Power Cable, rail has achievements and a pace-sim model (`railLaid`, `railRides`, `railHomes`, `railMeters`, `railHauled`).

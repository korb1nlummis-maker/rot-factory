# Rot Factory: Satisfactory-inspired build spec (internal, not published)

Scope: adapt Satisfactory's best systems to Rot Factory's voxel pile, plush economy and 7 Days style load tracing. Facts about Satisfactory marked (wiki) were fetched from satisfactory.wiki.gg; the rest are from general knowledge and only used for flavor, never for numbers we depend on.

## 1. What Satisfactory does (short)

Belts (wiki): Mk1 to Mk6 move 60 / 120 / 270 / 480 / 780 / 1200 items per min. Max one belt is about 56 m, max slope 35 degrees, min bend radius 2 m. Three build modes: default curve, straight (90 degree turns only), spline. Belts pass through each other, terrain and some buildings freely. Lifts are separate vertical belts with the same tier speeds. Higher tiers are gated by the research ladder (Tier 0 to 9 in the HUB).
Splitters and mergers: 1 to 3 and 3 to 1, round robin. Smart Splitter (wiki): per output rule of Any, None, a chosen Item, Overflow (only used when the other outputs are full or have no matching rule), Any Undefined (items with no explicit rule elsewhere). Buffer of 9 items, keeps 2 back when an overflow exists. Programmable Splitter adds multiple items per output.
Power lines (wiki): hand wired. 100 m per line on a pole, 300 m on a tower, 25 m of cable costs one cable, poles have 4/7/10 connection slots by mark, most machines 1 slot, storage and lights 2. No line of sight rule (lines go through anything). Lossless, no capacity limit on a wire. Power Switch (wiki): bridges two grids, default open, A and B connectors, green on, yellow off, red unconnected, does not isolate if another path exists. Priority Power Switch (wiki): 8 priority groups, on overload the switches drop starting at group 8 until supply meets demand, an undefined group goes first, group 1 goes last, any priority switch can toggle the others. Overload without that: the whole grid trips (the fuse) until demand is fixed and the grid is reset. Power Storage (wiki): 100 MWh each, charges from surplus at up to 100 MW, discharges instantly to cover deficits, blue bar charging, orange discharging, grey idle. The power info panel shows production, consumption, capacity, max consumption and a history graph.
Building (wiki): foundations 8 m square in 1/2/4 m thickness, same cost for all, world grid snaps to 8 m (X/Z) and 1 m (Y), Ctrl aligns to the world grid, rotation in 45 degree steps (5 degree fine steps when stacking). Walls only attach to foundations or walls. Ramps join levels. Zoop places up to 10 at once. Blueprints copy a designer box of buildings and recipes. Catwalks, stairs, railings and pillars are the walkways.
Catalog and gating: storage containers (personal box, industrial container), signs with text, lights (street, wall, ceiling, floodlight), doors and walls, elevators, jump pads (wiki: tier 2, 5 MW active, 0.1 MW standby, 5 launch buffer, 0 to 90 degree angle), hypertubes, vehicles (wiki): Tractor, Truck, Explorer, Cyber Wagon burn fuel, Factory Cart runs on power, recorded path autopilot, docking stations refuel and load, drones fly between ports with fuel, trains run on power (25 to 110 MW) with stations. Everything is gated by HUB milestones, then Space Elevator phases; items cost materials made by the factory itself.

Takeaways to copy: (1) tiered everything with a visible per-tile throughput number, (2) rule based splitters with an overflow output, (3) player wired grids with switches, a trip risk, priority shedding and storage, (4) a build grid with flat foundations and snapping so factories look intentional, (5) vehicles with recorded routes plus a dock that refuels and unloads, (6) gating by what the player has already automated.
Things not to copy: no line of sight rule exists in Satisfactory, so we add none (cables stay length limited, see cables.js). Hypertubes, pipes, fluids, train signaling: out of scope.

## 2. What the repo already has (read in full or skimmed)

- Grid: 0.6 m cells, `idx(i,j,k)`, hall 16384 x 16384 x 72 cells (config.js). Special cell ids 4090 to 4099 are taken (BULK 4098 is a player built, never falling wall cell, drawn in render.js with `bulkLin`). 4094 and 4095 are free.
- Belts (logistics.js): `type:'belt'` tiles in `game.logi.tiles` (Map keyed by idx), fields `dir, rise (-1/0/1), items[{sp,vr,t}], splitter, detector, hose, pw, halt, cd (corner feeder dir)`. One speed for all belts: `T.beltSpeed` 1.6 cells/s (upgrades Belt Motors, Belt Overdrive). Item spacing 0.34, 3 items per tile max, so about 4.7 items/s per belt (about 280 per min). Splitter is a belt flagged `splitter` that alternates forward/left/right (`splitOuts`, `rr`). Sorter has `mode` and `filter` by rarity only. `accept(n,item,fromDir)` is push based, update order is Map order (side merges can starve). Gate = belt flagged `detector` with `scanItem`, `recomputeHalt` stops the whole line on The One.
- Power (power.js): WIRE-ONLY (see the section "Wire-only power" at the end). Nodes (`gen`, `pole`, `battery`, `breaker`) join a grid only through cables; consumers are powered only by a cable to a node (a belt line is one load and one endpoint). No range link, no `T.poleReach`. Supply is burning generators at `T.genOutput` (8 kW base); demand per type in `DEMAND`; `net.sat = min(1, supply/demand)` slows everything (brownout). `net.cap` sums rated generator output. Switches, storage, trips and the meter are below.
- Cables (cables.js): click source then click target, `T.cableLen` metres (14 + Grid Range + Superconducting Poles), saved, host simulated, `cables` MP message.
- MP: host simulates everything. Config edits travel as guest `cmd` messages (`place`, `decon`, `tile`, `cable`, `earth`); host broadcasts `ent+`/`ent-` with `stripEnt` (drops `items q kept stored buf path trail carry hop cargo`). Guests draw host state from compact rows (see `earthRow`).
- Tests: `src/tests/*.js`, each `export default async (ctx) => { await ctx.T('area.name', async () => true | 'reason') }`, run in the dev console with `await __selftest('prefix.')`. `tools/pace-sim.mjs` models pacing.

## 3. Cross-cutting rules (every wave follows these)

1. One new module per area, imported through one registry. Wave 0 creates `src/catalog.js` which imports `catalog_belts.js`, `catalog_power.js`, `catalog_build.js`, `catalog_furnish.js`, `catalog_transit.js`, `catalog_haul.js`, `catalog_detector.js` (empty stubs at first). Each stub exports `{ UPGRADES: [], RECIPES: (g) => [], DEMAND: {}, TYPES: {} }`. upgrades.js, crafting.js and power.js merge these once (Wave 0). Later waves only edit their own catalog file, so no two agents edit the same shared file.
2. New placeable types are entities with `{id, type, i, j, k, dir, ...fields}`. Cell footprint types use `game.logi.tiles` only when they are belt-like or need `pw`; everything else goes in `game.machines.items` like frames. Save: `S.entities` already round trips plain JSON. Never store Three objects or Maps on an ent. Transient fields are listed in `stripEnt`; add any new transient name there once in Wave 0 (`lane`, `wait`, `cache`, `hist`).
3. Cost: every new item gets a `price` in recipes (K=3 multiplier applies) and a `matN`/`mat` only for building shell pieces (see 4.3). Endgame wallets are 10M+, 100M by the Neutron frame tier, so top tier items are priced 1M to 150M and must scale with count via `earthCost`-style growth where abuse is possible.
4. Power demand goes in the area's `DEMAND` map in kW. Unpowered fallback for anything the player needs to finish a line: keep `HAND_CRANK` behavior (35% speed) for belts only; nothing else cranks.
5. MP: the host owns simulation and config. A guest changes config through one `cmd` kind `cfg` (new, handled in Wave 0 in game.js): `{id, patch:{...}}`, host validates keys against a per-type whitelist, applies, then sends `ent-` + `ent+`. Guests never simulate. Any per second HUD value (power history, truck battery) goes in a compact row broadcast every 0.5 s by the owning module, applied through `guestRow`.
6. Every wave ships tests under `src/tests/<area>_*.js` with prefix `<area>.` and one `mp.<area>.` test that applies a host ent list to a guest `Logistics`/`Machines` and compares. Run `__selftest('<area>.')`, `__selftest('mp.')`, and the full suite before finishing. Update `src/controls.js` for any new key (a test compares it to real handlers). Update `src/info.js` so aiming at each new item shows a readout.
7. Docs: add one README line per shipped system under Systems (README is published; this file is not).

## 4. Specs, prioritized

Priority key: P0 unblocks everything or fixes a real pain, P1 high value, P2 nice, P3 later.

### 4.1 Belt tiers, lifts, curves, planner (P0)

Satisfactory: six marks, per mark speed, lift as its own part, curved spline belts placed by click and drag with snapping to ports.

Rot Factory mapping:
- Data: belt tile gets `tier` (0 to 5, default 0). `TIER_MUL = [1, 1.5, 2.25, 3.4, 5, 7.5]`, tile speed = `T.beltSpeed * TIER_MUL[tier]`. Spacing stays 0.34 so printed throughput is `speed/0.34 * 60` items per min: Mk1 about 282, Mk2 423, Mk3 635, Mk4 960, Mk5 1,410, Mk6 2,120 at beltSpeed 1.6 (show the live number in `info.js`, including upgrades). A line is only as fast as its slowest tile; `info.js` shows the line bottleneck.
- Power per tile (kW): `[0.03, 0.05, 0.09, 0.16, 0.30, 0.55]` replacing the flat `DEMAND.belt`.
- Cost per tile (before K=3): `[3, 12, 60, 300, 1500, 8000]`. Unlock upgrades (absolute, needs `beltSpeed` at the listed level): Mk2 `beltMk2` 150k (beltSpeed 3), Mk3 1.2M (beltSpeed 6), Mk4 6M (Overdrive 1), Mk5 30M (Overdrive 2), Mk6 150M (Overdrive 3). Place a Mk N over a belt of lower mark to upgrade it in place (refunds the old tile), hold B drags the tier you hold.
- Lifts: tile `type:'belt', lift:{h:n}` with `rise:0`; its output is the cell `(i+DX[dir], j+h, k+DZ[dir])`, h from 2 to 24 (14.4 m, hall is 43.2 m). The column cells between are added to `world.reserved` so nothing else is built in them; plush climb at the tile's speed, 1 item per 0.34 of length like belts. Price `belt price * 2 * h`. A lift of up to 8 cells needs no support; taller than 8 cells needs a Lift Frame (a strut ent, uses `STRUT_DEPTH.strut` rating, 1 per 8 cells) or it sways and the top tile reports "unsupported". Down lifts use a negative `h`.
- Underground pair: `ug:{pair:id, span}` entry and exit tiles up to 4 cells apart (Mk-scaled: span 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14) that carry plush under belts, frames and through plush pile without carving. Both ends reserve only their own cell. Cost 40 per end. Hover shows span and the cells it crosses. This replaces Satisfactory's "belts pass through each other" which a one tile grid cannot do.
- Curves: keep the 4 direction grid. Add 45 degree looking corners only in the mesh: the corner piece (`t.cd`) becomes a quarter arc (instanced curved mesh, 3 segments) and items follow a Bezier path during that tile (`it.t` mapped through the arc). No new simulation.
- Planner (build gun): new tool `belt` mode where clicking a start cell then an end cell previews an auto routed line (straight + corners, optional rises), snapping start to a machine output or the end to a machine input or the bin. Controls: first click start, aim end, scroll or `R` cycles the route variant (horizontal first, vertical first, over, under), second click lays all tiles. Preview shows tile count, cost, throughput of the tier held, and red cells where blocked. Current hold B behavior stays as the "paint" mode. Max 64 tiles per planned line (about 38 m, close to Satisfactory's 56 m).
- Files: logistics.js (tier speed, lift, ug, curve mesh, accept rules), new `src/beltplan.js` (router, preview, snapping; no edits to logistics.js except one call), `info.js`, `catalog_belts.js`, crafting recipes (ids `belt:1` to `belt:5`, `lift`, `ug`), `controls.js`.
- Tests: `belts.tier-speed-matches-printed-throughput`, `belts.slow-tile-sets-line-rate`, `belts.upgrade-in-place-refunds`, `belts.lift-carries-plush-up-24-cells`, `belts.lift-reserves-column`, `belts.ug-pair-crosses-belt-and-frame`, `belts.planner-routes-around-wall`, `belts.planner-snaps-to-bin`, `belts.corner-arc-keeps-item-order`, `mp.belts.tier-lift-ug-sync`.
- MP: `tier`, `lift`, `ug` are plain fields on the tile, sent by `ent+`. Visual arc interpolation is local.

### 4.2 Mergers, smart splitters, priority (P0, after 4.1)

Satisfactory: merger takes 3 inputs fairly, smart splitter has per output filter rules and overflow.

Rot Factory mapping:
- Merger tile: `type:'belt', merger:true`. Pulls from its three input neighbors in round robin (`mrr`), no pushing race. 1 output. Fixes the Map order starvation described in section 2.
- Priority Merger: `merger:'prio'` with `prio:[a,b,c]` order per input (cycle with E). Pulls input 1 until empty, then 2, then 3. Use: keep a rare lane flowing ahead of bulk.
- Smart Splitter (upgrade `smartSplit`, 2M, needs Belt Motors 4): the existing `splitter` belt gets `rules:[r_fwd, r_left, r_right]`, each `{k:'any'|'none'|'overflow'|'undef'|'rarity'|'species'|'one'|'variant', v}`. `rarity` v is a minimum rarity 0 to 6 plus optional max, `species` is an id (set from the plushdex picker or by aiming a plush at it), `variant` matches shiny. Resolution per item mirrors Satisfactory: explicit matches (round robin among equal matches), then `any`, then `undef` if no output claims the species, and `overflow` only when every other eligible output is full (use `accept` returning false). Items that match nothing wait (belt backs up) unless `undef` or `overflow` exists. Buffer of 3 items (cells hold less here).
- Programmable Splitter (upgrade `progSplit`, 40M, needs Smart Splitter and a sorter at level 3): each output holds up to 8 rules, plus a "default" output.
- Sorting Box filter by rarity stays; add `species` filter mode to it so sorters can hold a chosen plush (contract helper).
- Throughput: splitter and merger speed equal the tier of the tile they sit on; merger limited to one item per 0.34 spacing so a 3 to 1 merge is exactly the output belt rate.
- Prices (before K): Merger 60, Priority Merger 400, Smart Splitter 4,000, Programmable Splitter 60,000. Power 0.2, 0.3, 1.5, 3.0 kW (they need power for the rules; unpowered they act as a plain splitter at `HAND_CRANK`).
- UI: E opens a three slot modal (forward/left/right) with rule radio plus picker. Rule glyph above each output arrow in the mesh (colored cone) so lines read at a glance.
- Files: logistics.js (`accept` order, `splitOuts`, `updateBelt` merge pull), new `src/splitrules.js` (pure rule evaluator, unit tested without three), `ui.js` modal hook through catalog, `info.js`.
- Tests: `split.rule-any-undef-overflow-order`, `split.overflow-only-when-full`, `split.rarity-range-and-species-match`, `split.unpowered-acts-plain`, `split.merger-fair-three-inputs`, `split.priority-merger-starves-low-lane-only-when-high-busy`, `mp.split.rules-sync`.

### 4.3 Power: switches, breakers, priority, storage, meter (P0 for 4.3a, P1 rest)

Wave 2 owns power.js. The manual cable tool is already built by another agent (`cables.js`: click source, click target, `connect(aId,bId)`, `game.cables.live()`). Do not rebuild wiring. Extension points only:
- Make `Cables.wireable` accept the new node types `switch`, `battery`, `breaker`, `meter` (one line in `NODE`/`CONSUMER` sets). Everything else below lives in power.js and a new `src/powerparts.js`.
- Port counts (new, enforced by `Cables.connect` through a `maxPorts(e)` helper exported from `powerparts.js`): (superseded by the wire-only rules at the end: generator 4, pole 10, switch 2, battery 4, breaker 6, machine 1, charger 1; there is no range link any more).

a) Power Switch (P0): node type `switch`, field `on` (default false like the game, wiki: default open). In `recompute`, a switch is not a normal pole; it is excluded from the pole range union and from `nodes`. It connects exactly two cable ends (A and B); if `on` it unions the two cable targets' grids. It never isolates grids that are also joined by poles or other cables (same as Satisfactory). Lamp: green on, yellow off, red when fewer than two cables. E toggles, any switch toggles by hammer click too. Price 6,000, 0.05 kW.
b) Breaker Box / fuse (P1): node type `breaker`, field `trip:{at, delay}`, `tripped`, `armed`. A grid with at least one breaker trips when `demand > cap * 1.0` for longer than `delay` seconds (default 3 s), where `cap` is rated generator output (`net.cap`, already computed; add stored discharge). While tripped the whole grid supplies 0 (`sat=0`), lamps red, a loud contactor clack and a hint "GRID TRIPPED: fix demand, then E on the breaker". Reset by E; it re-trips if demand is still over cap (a 1 s grace). Upgrade `autoReset` (Remote Reset, 900k) resets after 20 s once demand is below cap. A grid with no breaker keeps today's brownout (so nothing existing breaks). Price 25,000, `DEMAND.breaker` 0.1. Saves `tripped` so a reload does not hide a failure.
c) Priority Switch (P1): `type:'switch', prio:1..8 or 0`. Same two cable behavior as (a) but participates in shedding. Algorithm in `recompute`: compute nets; for each overloaded net (demand > supply with a breaker present, or any net with a priority switch), iterate: open the on priority switch with the highest group number (undefined group, `prio:0`, goes before 8), recompute that pair of nets, stop when supply meets demand or none are left (max 8 passes, each pass is the existing union-find, so cost stays small; cache by a dirty flag). Dropped switches show `shed:true` and an orange lamp and are reset by E once the grid is healthy, or auto at 10 s of surplus if `autoReset`. Satisfactory extras we copy: remote toggle (any priority switch's E panel lists all of them with names), names editable (sign text editor, see 4.5). Price 90,000, upgrade `prioPower` 4M (needs Switch built once, Breaker built once).
d) Power Storage (P1): node type `battery`, `charge` kJ, `cap` by mark: Mk1 36,000 kJ (50 Commons or 3 Epic), Mk2 360,000, Mk3 3,600,000. Price 40k / 400k / 4M. Charge rate limit `cap/1800` kW (full in 30 min at surplus), discharge unlimited like Satisfactory. In `recompute`: `surplus = supply - demand`; if surplus > 0 charge each battery proportionally (kW * dt kJ); if surplus < 0, discharge up to the missing kW and add it to `supply` for that tick before `sat`. Trips if empty and still overloaded. Mesh bar: blue charging, orange discharging, grey idle (matches Satisfactory). Wired by cable like any node (port count 2), also joins the grid by pole range.
e) Load Meter (P1, cheap): `type:'meter'`, a wall panel drawing 0.02 kW. Shows supply, demand, rated cap, battery %, tripped state, and a 60 s history graph from `net.hist` (ring of 120 samples at 0.5 s, transient). Aiming at any grid node shows the same one-line summary (`info.js`). Also a toggle HUD readout (K is medkits and U is the cart, so the wave picks a free key after checking `controls.js`) for the grid you stand nearest to (`Power.nearest`).
f) Gen priority and fuel hold (P2): `gen.mode:'auto'|'reserve'`. A reserve generator does not burn unless a battery is below 30% or the grid is overloaded (stops wasting fuel when batteries are full). Satisfactory burns constantly; this is a plush saving improvement.
g) Wiring along poles (P2): cable placement snaps its mid point to a pole's hook (like Satisfactory poles) and a "multi cable" mode: click a pole as source, then click several machines, one cable each, within port limits. Owned by the cable agent; list this in their backlog, do not implement here.
- Power demand of the new parts: switch 0.05, priority switch 0.08, breaker 0.1, battery 0.1, meter 0.02, smart splitter 1.5, programmable splitter 3.0 kW.
- Files: power.js (recompute, trip, shed, battery), new `powerparts.js` (types, meshes, maxPorts, PORTS table), `cables.js` (one-line sets and maxPorts call), `info.js`, `ui.js` (meter graph canvas, switch panel), `catalog_power.js`.
- Tests: `power.switch-open-isolates-two-grids`, `power.switch-does-not-isolate-if-pole-path-exists`, `power.breaker-trips-over-cap-after-delay`, `power.no-breaker-keeps-brownout`, `power.tripped-saves-and-loads`, `power.priority-sheds-highest-group-first`, `power.priority-undefined-group-goes-first`, `power.battery-charges-on-surplus-discharges-on-deficit`, `power.battery-empty-trips`, `power.reserve-gen-holds-fuel`, `power.meter-history-ring`, `mp.power.switch-breaker-battery-sync`.
- MP: host recomputes; `on`, `tripped`, `shed`, `prio`, `charge` are fields on ents. Battery charge changes every tick, so it is excluded from `ent+` churn and sent in the 0.5 s power row `{netId, supply, demand, cap, batt, tripped}`. Guests draw lamps from `pw`/`tripped` they receive.

### 4.4 Foundations, leveling pads, ramps, walls, catwalks, stairs (P1)

Satisfactory: foundations are the build grid. Here the floor is plush pile; leveling means a flat, solid, safe floor and clear walls so factories, haul roads and arches have somewhere to sit, and it ties into the roof rules (a pad is not a roof, a wall is BULK).
- Cell kinds: PAD = 4095 (concrete floor cell, never falls, drawn like BULK in render.js with a pale concrete color and a grid line), `RAMP` reuses the `ramp` belt model idea but for walking: ramps are `type:'ramp'` ents (2 x 2 cells, rise 1 cell over 2 cells, so about 30 degrees; walkable, trucks climb up to 1 cell per 3). Walls are existing BULK. All three are solid cells in `world`, so they hold the pile back and join `anchored()` checks in world.js (BULK already does; PAD counts as an anchor for neighbor roof cells at the same row).
- Pad ent: 4 x 4 cells x 1 deep (2.4 m square, the frame footprint so they snap to frames and each other), placed by `planPad` the way `planFrame` does (aim next to a pad or frame, snaps in line, beside, above or below), min clearance: the 4 x 4 x 4 volume above must be empty (dig first, same rule as frames). Material: reuse `MATERIALS` (timber pad is a plank deck, steel plate, concrete slab; price in material units like frames: `matN` 4). Crafting price per pad 18 at timber, 60 steel, 140 concrete (before K). Zoop: hold B and drag along an axis to lay up to 10 pads (or 5 x 5 with Shift), one cost line in the preview, one undo (X on any piece removes only that piece, Shift+X removes the zooped group via shared `grp` id).
- Leveling: `LevelPad` (an automated tool for late game) removes cells in a chosen box and fills the floor with PAD so players do not dig by hand: price 1.2M, runs as an earth mover job (reuse `earth.js` `workCells`, no new simulation), draws 15 kW.
- Ramp: rise 1 cell (0.6 m) over 2 cells; player step height currently needs checking (`player.js` step up); the ramp ent registers as a walkable collider with `rampY(x,z)`. Stairs: 2 cells per 1.2 m rise, 1 cell wide x 4 cells long, no collider math beyond the ramp slope (just a different mesh and railing).
- Catwalk: 1 cell wide, 4 long, rail meshes, solid floor cells of kind PAD with `thin:true` (cheap, half the price, holds belts and poles, cannot carry trucks). Railings are cosmetic ents on pad edges (`rail:[n,e,s,w]` bits).
- Snapping rules (Satisfactory style): pad grid snaps to multiples of 4 cells on both axes relative to the first pad (`gridOrigin` stored per group). `Ctrl` while placing aligns to the world grid origin (k, i multiples of 4), R rotates 90, Shift+R nudges 1 cell. Pads reject placement where `world.reserved` or an existing ent overlaps (use `footprintOverlap` from machines.js).
- Load rules: pad cells are not roof, they take no load; pads under a roof do nothing for support. Walls (BULK) already count toward anchoring; keep that behavior and add a test that a sealed pad room does not change safe length.
- Files: new `src/build.js` (pads, ramps, stairs, catwalks, zoop, groups), `plushdata.js` (PAD id, `isSpecialCell`), `render.js` (PAD color, same as BULK branch lines 281 and 410), `world.js` (one line in `anchored` and the solid test, owned by this wave only), `player.js` (ramp height query, one function), `catalog_build.js`.
- Cost tiers: timber/steel/concrete follow `FRAME_TYPES`; titan and above pads (carbon, plasma, void) at 5x each step so a 100 pad hangar at plasma costs about 14M of materials, within endgame budgets.
- Tests: `build.pad-snaps-to-frame-and-pad`, `build.zoop-10-pads-one-cost-one-undo`, `build.pad-needs-clearance`, `build.pad-holds-belt-and-pole`, `build.ramp-walkable-and-truck-slope`, `build.wall-is-bulk-and-anchors`, `build.pad-room-keeps-safe-length`, `build.ctrl-aligns-world-grid`, `mp.build.pad-ramp-sync`, plus the existing render hole test (random edits compare to fresh scan) extended with PAD cells.
- MP: pads and ramps are ents or cells; cells already sync through `diff`/`cells`, ents through `ent+`. Zoop sends one `place` per piece in a single batch message to avoid 100 round trips.

### 4.5 Storage containers, signs, lights (P1)

Vaults gain an output tile: `out:true` pulls the oldest item to the belt it faces at tier speed.

### 4.8 Big support arches for trucks and tunnels, tied to loadtrace.js (P1)

Frames are 4 x 4 cell boxes (2.4 m), too tight for two lane haul and big diggers. Add arch supports with wider spans, rated by the same load model.
- `ARCH_TYPES` in a new `src/arches.js` (not upgrades.js): `{ kind:'arch8', span:8, h:6, radius, maxDepth, cost }` cells. Sizes: `arch6` (6 x 5 cells, 3.6 x 3.0 m: truck lane + walkway), `arch8` (8 x 6, 4.8 x 3.6 m: two truck lanes or one bucket wheel), `arch12` (12 x 8, 7.2 x 4.8 m: cathedral, two wheel lanes). Material tiers reuse `FRAME_TYPES` keys; an arch of material `m` is named `<Frame name> Arch` and uses the same `MATERIALS` stock (units scale with span: 6, 12, 24 per piece vs 4 per frame).
- Load model hook: arches register in `w.supports` as `{id,x,y,z,r,cap,kind:'arch8:steel'}`. `capacityOf(kind)` in loadtrace.js gets one branch: `if (kind.includes(':')) return archCapacity(kind)` where `archCapacity` is exported from `arches.js` to avoid a circular edit. Radius `r = max(span/2 * C, frame.radius)`. Capacity formula: `BASE_LOAD * (r / 2.5) * press(maxDepth) * CAL_ARCH[kind]`, with `maxDepth = frameMaxDepth * DERATE[span]` where `DERATE = {6: 0.85, 8: 0.7, 12: 0.55}` (a wide span carries more roof so it is rated shallower than the same material as a frame). `CAL_ARCH` is measured by the same method as `CAL` (load of a lone arch in a standard tunnel of its own span at exactly its rated depth) so each tier hits 100% at its rating. Example outputs for the doc: Steel Arch8 about 266 m, Concrete Arch8 about 560 m, Rebar Arch8 about 910 m, Titanium Arch12 about 990 m.
- Spacing: sphere reach follows loadtrace's `inside()` so an arch covers `r` meters either way along the tunnel: arch8 about 6.2 m reach so one per 12 m of tunnel near the surface, closer at depth by the existing stress. `MAX_SHARE` is unchanged. The preview shows the predicted load (existing placement UI) and the roof clearance rule: all roof cells of the span must be dug (same as frames).
- Clearance contract with trucks: `arch.clear:{w,h}` in cells (inner clear size: arch6 5 x 4, arch8 7 x 5, arch12 11 x 7). `haul.js` router refuses a truck whose `W x H` exceeds the narrowest `clear` on the route and reports "route blocked by arch6 at (x,z): too narrow". Bucket wheel needs arch8 minimum (5 x 6 cells) and an Excavator fits arch6.

## 7. Wave 0 done: API notes

Wave 0 is in. Shared files now have the hooks below, so waves 1 to 8 edit only their own files. Files added: `src/catalog.js` (registry), `src/ext.js` (runtime), `src/detector.js`, `src/catalog_{belts,power,build,furnish,transit,haul,detector}.js` (empty stubs), tests `src/tests/wave0_{catalog,compat,mp}.js`.

Your catalog file (`catalog_<area>.js`) exports `UPGRADES`, `RECIPES(g)`, `DEMAND`, `TYPES`. Contract (full text at the top of `catalog.js`):
- Imports: never import `upgrades.js`, `crafting.js`, `power.js` or `game.js` at module level from a catalog file (they import the registry, so it would be a cycle). Plain data modules and lazily used helpers are fine.
- `UPGRADES`: entries merged once at the end of upgrades.js, costs are ABSOLUTE (no COST_SCALE, no `fresh` flag unless you want the "maxed it" achievement). A duplicate id is dropped and listed in `catalog.CONFLICTS` (a test fails on any conflict).
- `RECIPES(g)`: bench rows `{id, kind, icon, name, short, desc, price, batch, p?, use?, statusFn?(g,r)}`. Price gets the K=3 multiplier. `kind` is the tool kind, `p` is a parameter bag that arrives as `tool.p` in plan/build and in the guest `place` command. Crafting, hotbar, inventory and `S.items[id]` work unchanged.
- `DEMAND`: `{entType: kW}`, new keys only (power.js keys win). Wiring a type into `power.recompute` as a consumer is still the power wave's job.
- `TYPES[key]` handlers, all optional (key is the ent type, or a tool kind when the handler has `plan`; add `kinds:[...]` for extra tool kinds):
  - `cfg`: `{key: validator}` or `(ent) => {...}`, built with `V` from catalog.js (`V.bool`, `V.int(lo,hi)`, `V.num`, `V.str(max)`, `V.enum([...])`, `V.arr(max,item)`, `V.obj(shape)`); `copy: [keys]`, `group` (types with the same group paste into each other), `check(g,ent,clean,actor)` returns a refusal text, `onCfg(g,ent,clean,old)`.
  - `info(g,ent,ref)` replaces the aim readout (return null to fall back), `infoExtra(g,ent)` returns lines put in front of the built in readout. Works for belt like tiles and machines on both screens.
  - `use(g,ent)` is E on it (return true when handled). A guest must send `g.setCfg(...)` or its own `cmd`.
  - `add(machines, ent)` returns `{obj, ...}` for types that live in `game.machines.items` (default: an empty Group). Tile like types (`LOGI` set in logistics.js) still go through `logi.add`.
  - Tools: `plan(g,tool,eye,dir,yaw)` returns `{plan:{ok,why,ent},cost}`, `preview(g,tool,plan)` (default: the generic cell ghost when `plan.ent` has i,j,k), `build(g,tool,planEnt)` returns the new ent's fields incl. `type`, `conflict(g,planEnt,tool)` is the host's re-check of a guest placement. `item(ent)` is what the hammer hands back, `onRemove(g,ent)` runs after it.
  - `tick(g,dt)` / `guestTick(g,dt)` run once per frame per type (not per ent). `row(g)` (host, every 0.5 s while connected, return null to skip) and `guestRow(g,d)` carry per second HUD data as `{t:'xrow',k:type,d}`; use them for power history, truck battery and other values that change every tick, not `ent+`.

Game methods (game.js, thin wrappers over `ext.js`):
- `g.setCfg(entOrId, patch)` returns `{ok, why}`. On a host it validates against `TYPES[type].cfg` and applies, then sends `ent-` then `ent+` (so a guest redraws from `stripEnt`). On a guest it validates locally and sends `cmd cfg {id, patch}`; the host re-validates (unknown key, wrong type or range rejects the WHOLE patch, a refusal toasts the guest). Only `cfg` keys ever change through it, so every setting a guest can edit must be listed in `cfg`.
- `g.copyCfg(ent)` stores `g.cfgClip = {type, group, vals}`; `g.pasteCfg(ent)` pastes through `setCfg`. Keys: Shift+E aimed at a type with a `cfg` copies, E on a same group target pastes (a held copy takes E before the type's own use), Shift+E at nothing drops it. Plain E and every old behavior are unchanged when nothing is copied. The Sorting Box is registered as the first user (core type in catalog.js, `mode` only; `filter` follows it).
- `g.placeEntity(type, fields, opts)` is the host side entry: new id, `S.entities`, `addEntity`, `ent+`, power dirty, stats, hotbar refresh. A tool with `plan` and `build` goes through `placeCurrent` automatically (item is consumed there; a guest sends `place` with `tool.p`).

## 10. Lead notes: elevators are hoistway-driven (user design, binds Wave 5 audit and follow-ups)
- The Elevator is a big box (cab, about 4x4 cells footprint, 4 high) that you set into an opening high up the pile (a dug, supported cell or a pad edge).
- It needs a hoistway: a hollow vertical tube you dig straight down from the cab (same 4x4 section, shored with frames or cube supports like any tunnel, load tracing applies, stale air at depth applies). The elevator does NOT dig for you.
- Once the tube is dug down, the elevator extends to the bottom of the shaft by itself: the travel range is the clear length of the shaft below the cab (cap per tier, e.g. 24 / 64 / 160 / 400 m), with a stop at every opened landing (a side tunnel or pad that connects to the shaft gets a call button).
- Rules: refuses to run through an unsupported or blocked section (reports where), if the shaft is cut by a collapse the cab stops at the last clear landing and says so, carries the player, carts and plush loads, powered by a pole/cable (slow hand-crank speed without), shared by two players, same on both screens, saves/loads.
- Tests must dig a shaft, extend, ride up/down between two landings, cut the shaft, and run both multiplayer roles.

## 14. Cave-in tuning for the two tunnel classes: before and after (wave 6)

What was tuned, and why only one number changed. The settings looked at, live, with real tunnels dug in the running game (a pile block with an open face, 5 high tunnels 2 to 24 wide, cubes and arches of every span set at spacings from 4 to 32 cells, near the bay and deep at 200, 500 and 1,000 m, 30 s of the real stability loop each): the tunnel rule in `world.js` (`SAFE_LEN` 12, `OB` 14, `MIN_SAFE` 3, `depthPenalty`), `MIN_CAVITY` 14, the creak timers (`T.warn` 1.1 x 0.35 to 1.25), the support radii and the search allowance in `cavityLen`. Everything held except the allowance.
- **The bug**: `cavityLen` gave up after 500 visited cells whatever it was asked to search. The cells within `limit` steps of a roof cell grow with the width of the room (about 2 x width x reach), so a wide tunnel ran out of cells before a narrow one did. With Pile Tamping at the top level (safe length 27 cells), a 12 wide tunnel failed at 23 cells, a 16 wide one at 19 and a 24 wide one at 16, while a 2 to 8 wide tunnel stood 27: a giant arch tunnel dug by an excavator, or a hangar, would have caved in a third early for no reason the player could see.
- **The change**: the allowance is `CAVITY_CAP` 500 + `CAVITY_PER_STEP` 70 per cell of reach asked for (`world.capPerStep` overrides it for tests). Cost: at most 108 microseconds per roof cell at width 24 with the deepest tamping (14 to 28 for narrow tunnels).
- **Safe length of an unsupported tunnel** (first failing roof cell counted from the open mouth, `cavein.the-safe-length-of-an-unsupported-tunnel-is-the-same-at-every-width...`): at Pile Tamping 0 the limit is 11 cells (6.6 m) and every width from 2 to 24 fails at 11, before and after. Tamping level 4 (limit 19): before 19 at every width but 24 (16), after 19 at every width. Tamping level 8 (limit 27): before 27, 27, 27, 27 for widths 2, 4, 6, 8, then 23, 19, 16 for widths 12, 16, 24; after 27 at every width. A 40 wide hall with a bulkhead wall across it: a roof cell 20 cells past the wall is 19 from an anchor with a limit of 27 and now stands (the old allowance called it unsupported).
- **Safe unsupported length by depth** (limit `B`, cells): 11 near the bay (pile 32 rows), 8 at 200 m, 6 at 500 m, 4 at 1,000 m (the `MIN_SAFE` floor of 3 from about 1,050 m). Unchanged.
- **Lined tunnels stand** (real loop, 30 s, largest spacing in cells of 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32 that did not drop anything): cube (steel) 32 near the bay, 20 at 200 m, 24 at 500 m (titanium), 32 at 1,000 m (neutron); Haul Arch 32, 24, 24, 32; Wide Arch 32, 32, 24, 32; Cathedral Arch 32 everywhere. The permanent tests set cubes every 8 cells and arches every 12 and require no collapse and fewer than 9 loose plush in 40 s, and the same tunnels with no supports at all to come down (near the bay and at 500 m, every class).
- **Portal bore**: 40 slabs (24 m) lined every 4 slabs at spans 6, 8, 12 near the bay, at 200 m and at 600 m: 0 creaks, 0 collapses, 0 loose plush (at 1,200 m the Portal chokes on stale air first, as designed: it needs a fan under its arches).
- **Screenshots** (`docs/wave6/`): `cube-tunnel-rail-belt.jpg` (a lined 4 x 4 tunnel with a Mine Rail track and a belt), `arch-tunnel-truck.jpg` (a Concrete Wide Arch tunnel with a Haul Truck on its floor), `portal-at-work.jpg` (a Steel Wide Arch Portal with the arches it set behind the cutter and its readout), `lined-arch12-stands.jpg` (a 12 wide, 8 high Cathedral tunnel with a Steel Arch every 12 cells after 14 s of the stability loop) and `unlined-roof-creaking.jpg` (the same tunnel with no arches: ROOF CREAKING and the first plush falling at the far end).
- Tests that pin it: `cavein.*` (10 tests), the existing `tunnel.*`, `stress.*`, `mining.stress-*` and `qa.load.*` still pass with no number changed.

### 14b. Support reach and a grace before the roof lets go: before and after

The complaint: deep in a cave the roof falls so fast after digging that the player can set one support and no more; a cell or two too far and the ceiling comes down before the next one goes in. Measured first (a sealed 4 x 4 tunnel with one cube at its west end as the only anchor, the real stability loop, near the bay and 200, 500 and 1,000 m out; `support_reach.js`): a roof cell within a support's sphere gets `3 x bonus` extra cells, but a cell beyond it gets only the plain safe length (11, 8, 6, 4 cells at those depths), and a roof that went over the limit creaked for `warn x 0.35 to 1.25` = 0.4 to 1.4 s and let go. The next cube needs the four cells right after the first, so the margin to dig them was 4 to 9 columns, and an overshoot of one cell gave the player 0.4 to 1.4 s.
- **What changed** (`world.js`, three constants, no radius touched: `FRAME_TYPES.radius` feeds the load tracing, the calibration tables, the island reach and the arches, so widening it would move all of them):
  - `SUP_EXTRA = 3`: a roof whose NEAREST anchor is a support (cube, strut, jack, arch) stands 3 cells (1.8 m) further than before. The open mouth and bulkhead or pad walls do not get it (`cavityLen` returns what kind of anchor it found, and past the plain safe length only a support counts).
  - `GRACE_T = 2.5`, `GRACE_FULL = 3`, `GRACE_END = 6`: a roof that is past even that by 1 to 3 cells waits 2.5 s longer before it lets go (it creaks all the while: the usual creak, dust and ROOF CREAKING hint), 2/3 of that at 4 cells over, 1/3 at 5, none at 6 or more. A roof with no support within reach (a huge cavern, a mouth far away), a thin cap and anything 6 or more cells past a support fall as fast as before.
  - `island.js` `ISL_GRACE = 1.5`: a cut-off slab of 6 plush or more waits 1.5 s longer (2.5 to 4.5 s instead of 1 to 3 s). A stray plush or two still drops after 1 s.
  - Tests override the constants with `world.supExtra`, `world.graceT` and `world.islGrace` (0 gives the old rule), the way `capPerStep` does.
- **Columns of tunnel one cube holds past its east face, before -> after** (`cells`, x 0.6 for metres): near the bay timber 13 -> 16, steel 14 -> 17, concrete 16 -> 19, titanium 18 -> 21; 200 m 10 -> 13, 11 -> 14, 13 -> 16, 15 -> 18; 500 m 8 -> 11, 9 -> 12, 11 -> 14, 13 -> 16; 1,000 m 6 -> 9, 7 -> 10, 9 -> 12, 11 -> 14. The span one cube roofs (its 4 cells plus both sides) goes from 30 to 36 cells near the bay in timber (+20%), 22 to 28 in steel at 500 m (+27%) and 16 to 22 in timber at 1,000 m (+37%): the deeper, the more it is worth, because the plain safe length is what shrinks. The safe length with no support (open mouth, wall) is 11, 8, 6, 4 cells before and after.
- **The window to set the next support** (seconds until the first roof cell lets go after the player digs `n` columns past what the cube holds, the real loop, same tunnel; the old rule in brackets; each cell has its own random timer, so the earliest of 4 to 24 cells is what the player gets): 1 cell over 3.0 to 3.2 s (0.6 to 1.2), 2 cells 3.0 to 3.2 s (0.5 to 0.7), 3 cells 3.0 to 3.1 s (0.4 to 0.7), 4 cells 2.1 to 2.6 s (0.4 to 0.5), 5 cells 1.4 to 1.7 s (0.4 to 0.6), 6 cells 0.5 to 0.8 s (0.4 to 0.5), at the bay, 500 m and 1,000 m. The second cube (cells 4 to 7, next to the first) set 2 s after the dig saves the whole roof at 1 to 4 cells over (nothing falls in 12 s of loop); at 5 cells over the roof has already let go at 1.4 to 1.7 s, and at 6 even the second cube cannot hold the end of the tunnel. With the old rule a cube set 0.8 s after the dig was too late at every overshoot (the first cells let go at 0.4 to 0.8 s).
- **Not too much**: a 30 x 30 cavern with one cube in a corner collapses as before (old 258 roof cells missing at once, 1,485 rubble; new 194, 1,729: the rest is inside the cube's reach, which is the same size), a thin cap gets no grace, `cavein.*`, `thincap.*`, `island.*`, `stack.*`, `arch.*` and the frame, stack and arch load tracing are untouched. The only numbers that moved in older tests were `upg.mine.frame-*-reach-extends-tunnel` (the roof now stands `anchor + safe length + 3`) and three `island.` timing windows (+1.5 s), each changed with a comment.
- **Tests** (`support_reach.*`, 11 tests): the table above for 16 depth and material pairs (the old rule is measured in the same test), the safe length by depth unchanged, mouth and wall unchanged, the grace curve cell by cell, the window at 1, 2 and 3 cells over at the bay, 500 m and 1,000 m (old under 1.6 s, new 2.7 to 4.4 s, cube set at 2 s saves it), 6 cells over still collapsing even with the second cube, a 30 x 30 cavern, a thin cap and the slab delay.

## 13. Lead notes: stacked modular building inside the pile (user design, Wave 10)
- Collapse must follow the supports: digging UP above a frame's top (above the cube's roof) leaves new unsupported roof that obeys the span/depth rules and can fall; frames can be stacked on top of each other (cube above cube, existing snap 'above' rule) to carry the roof higher, and a stacked column shares load down to the floor (load tracing treats the column as one path). Digging beside a column without a frame beside it follows the normal span rules.
- Modules that fit in the cube supports (a cube is 4x4x4 cells): floor pad, ceiling plate, stairs, ramps (walk and belt ramps), landings, ladders, shaft openings for elevators and lift belts, door frames. They snap to the cube grid, count as part of its structure, and let a player walk and build on multiple levels inside the pile: factories, belt runs, elevators, ramps up to higher tunnels.
- Everything must fit the clearance contract (cube tunnels fit minecarts, belts, lifts, players; giant arches fit trucks).
- Load tracing: floors carry frames above them (deck rules from DESIGN_SATISFACTORY_2.md section on decks), a cube on a floor on a cube is a legal column; a floor over a void without support below is refused or flagged.
- Tests: dig up above a cube and see the real fall, stack two and three cubes and dig higher safely, walk stairs/ramps between levels, belt ramps and elevators through the stack, save/load, both multiplayer roles.

## 15. Wave 6 audit: what was found and fixed (arches, the Portal, haul)

The audit read the code, did not trust the report, and attacked it in a live game: 40 seeded random-edit soaks (arches of every span and tier, cubes, Portals, walls, pads, caches, collapses, the hammer, E, a real save and reload between steps, invariants checked after every step: one support per arch, no orphan shield, no two sections overlapping, `lined` never past what was bored), a Portal bored in all four ways a tunnel can run, a real `saveGame` and `startPlay(false)` in the middle of a bore (state, supports, shield and mesh came back and the bore went on), the 90 s real stability loop under a Portal at every span (0 creaks, 0 loose plush), trucks hauling end to end through a Portal-bored tunnel of every span, forged commands, damaged entities fed to every consumer, and the readouts of every state on both roles. Tests: `audit_arches_portal.js`, `audit_arches_haul.js`, `audit_arches_soak.js` (prefix `audit_arches.`) and `audit_arches_mp.js` (`mp.audit_arches.`). Each was run against the code as built first and failed; the fixes are small edits.
- **A Portal went silent for ever** on a wall, a floor pad, a supply cache or remains. `removeCell` never takes those four, so the cutter skipped the cell, counted the slab as bored, and the lining that followed found a "solid" cell in its section, cut nothing and returned every frame: the readout said "Boring the tunnel" with the cutter idle. Caches and remains are real: old workings put them on the floor row, where a Portal bores. Now a special cell in the next slab or in the open section halts it (`stuck`, with the reason in the readout and one announcement) and it goes on by itself when the cell is taken down or opened (E). A stuck lining also waits 2 s between tries (it re-weighed nine tiers every frame).
- **A dead truck never woke from a Truck Dock.** The readout told you to park a dock under it and the dock charged it, but `truckLogic` returned early for `dead` and only a Charge Pack item revived it. It now drives on once it holds 15% (`HAUL.BATT.low`, until then unused), 12 minutes from empty at 25 kW; a truck that dies within 3 m of its own yard is charged by the yard (it was only charged while `idle`) and drives on the same way. The readout no longer says a low truck "heads for a dock" (it waits at its yard until the trip is covered).
- **A guest could build a Truck Dock or a road plate with a belt.** `placeConflict` checks that a gen item makes a gen and nothing else; for the catalog tools it only asked the handler, and the road and dock handlers checked the spot, not the item or the unlock. A forged `place` with `{id: 'belt', kind: 'dock'}` built a 220,000 dock with the unlock locked. `roadConflict` and `dockConflict` now require their own item id and the unlock (`T.haulRoad`, `T.truckDock`).
- **The hammer took the arch down when you aimed at the machine standing under it.** A ray from outside meets the arch box first, and the arch pick won on distance: X on an excavator, a Vehicle Scanner or a truck parked inside an arch removed the roof support. The readout showed the arch too, and E (`ext.aimedEnt`, nearest centre) used the arch before a switch standing in it, which on a Portal parks the driver. `ARCH.holds(arch, item)` lets a machine standing in the box win in `findDeconRef`, `findInfoRef` and `aimedEnt` (a handler key `holds`); an arch with nothing in it is picked as before.
- **The choke readout differed on the guest**: "Choking on stale air (0%)" because the 0.5 s row carried no air figure. The row has an eighth number (percent, clamped, an old row without it still applies).
- **The Truck Dock sold for 219,999** (the bench rounded the price per K before multiplying, the unlock and the chalkboard say 220,000). It keeps the exact fraction like the Detector Arch does.
- **A trip was costed on the straight line** to the digger: a route that winds round a wall or down a bent tunnel is longer, and a truck with just enough for the straight line ran dry on the way (dead, needs a pack or a dock). The searched route length now counts.
- **A belt running INTO a dock made it a sink** (`outTile` ignored the belt direction its own readout talks about): trucks drove in and unloaded onto a dead end, a minute of patience a trip. A belt pointing into the pad is not an outlet.
- **A damaged arch in a save poisoned the game.** With no whole numbers for its section `add` returned early, leaving `span` as it was and `gm` set, and `boxOf` then threw in `layout`, `blockTaken` (every frame placement), the truck router and the hammer for the rest of the game. A damaged arch is now inert (no `gm`, a valid span and material) and can be hammered.
- **Small ones**: the Portal's alarm text said "A Haul Truck was carrying THE ONE" when the Portal cut it (`VS.raise` takes an optional text); a plain arch showed as `garch` on a cable readout (it uses its name); `nameOf` no longer throws on a garbage span.
- **A halted Portal flooded the guest.** `weigh` cuts the next four slabs and puts them back for each tier it tries (up to 9 x 384 cells), and every cell went to the guest's cell feed: a Portal halted by "the mountain presses" tries again every 2 s, so a guest got 19,200 numbers (3,840 cell updates, 10 s, one tier owned) and a re-mesh of the same tunnel for as long as it stayed halted. The trial cut now runs with the cell feed off (the world is the same after it; a test hashes the cells ahead).
- **The calibration test was flaky, the game was not.** `arch.calibrated-100pct-at-rated-depth-all-tiers-span-12` failed 3 runs in 15 (steel, 108% and 120% "at its rating"): when the first lane had natural old workings the test slid the arch 32 m sideways, off the circle of the rated depth, where the pile presses harder (measured: the same 348 roof cells read 0.986 on the circle and 1.05 to 1.15 off it). It now tries the next bearing instead (0 in 20 runs at span 12, 0 in 12 at 6 and 8).
- **Router cost**: the A* heuristic assumed a road (0.7 a cell) even with no road laid, so it explored far more than it needed in open ground. It uses 1 a cell unless a plate exists. Measured on a 240 m square of open floor (this machine, load 30): a 255 m diagonal 645 ms and 59,837 nodes before, 93 ms and 7,522 after; a 180 m detour round a wall 669 ms before, 400 ms after (36,687 nodes); a route from a Portal face to the bin in the real bay 3 to 6 ms. A goal that nothing can reach in a huge open hall still runs both searches to the node cap (about 1 s, measured 1,087 ms and 1,006 ms), and a truck in `blocked` looks again every 2 s: that is the one case left that can stall a frame, only in a hall far bigger than the bay.
- **Looked at and left alone**: `weigh` cuts and puts back up to 384 cells per tier each retry (the borer does the same; it sends the cells to a guest twice); the cave-in cap (`CAVITY_PER_STEP`) did not raise the failure rate of `stress.long-lined-tunnel-never-collapses` (1 of 24 runs failed with the old allowance, 1 of 32 with the new one: a random flake that exists without wave 6); a Portal draws its full kW while halted (the spec says "while it runs"; parking it is E); a cache can hand out any bench row, so a deep one can hold a Cathedral Arch (a pre-existing property of caches: frames of every tier were there too).
- **Not verified**: the one timber span 6 calibration failure (108.2%) before the fix above was most likely the same lane slide; `mp.arch.guest-place-command-is-rebuilt-by-the-host-and-forgeries-change-nothing` fails about 1 run in 10 (a belt, machine or shaft of the hall stands in the test's section for some world seeds); `levels.hands.cargo.effect` fails because the `carryMax` element is no longer in the page (the HUD rework, not wave 6).

## 16. Wave 10 done: stacked modular building inside the pile (API notes)

The lead notes (section 13, "stacked modular building") bind this wave; this is how each point was built. Files: `src/stack.js` (the logic: bays, plates, the switchback stair, ladders, door frames, what stands on what, readouts), `src/stackmesh.js` (the ladder and the guard rails round an opening), `src/catalog_stack.js` (the `stackKit` unlock, the ladder bench row and handlers), the stack part of `src/loadtrace.js` (`totalLoad`, `restOf`, `restingOn`), hooks in `src/build.js` (`hooks`, `holeAt`, `cellsOf`), `src/machines.js` (`cubeBlk`, the `blk` on a cube's support), `src/game.js` (the hammer's refusal, `failSupport` cascade, `stackHolds`, `strainOf`), `src/logistics.js` (one clause of `canPlace`), `src/transit.js` (a door aimed into a cube), `src/player.js` (`player.climb`), `src/info.js`, `src/hall.js` (the STACKED BUILDING board), `src/controls.js`, `src/achievements.js`. Tests `stack_*.js` and `mp_stack.js` (helpers in `stack_lib.js`).

- **A level.** A cube is a 4 x 4 x 4 cell bay; one level is one plate row and three clear rows (1.8 m), so a level fits exactly what the cube tunnel contract already says (`haul.js` `CUBE_CLEAR` 3 x 3: walker, Mine Rail cart with a rider, belt, belt lift). The beams of a cube are drawn, never collided with, and the real Player, a real rail cart and real plush on belts and lifts are driven through plated cubes by `stack.transit.` and `stack.plate.`. A Haul Truck is still refused by every cube tunnel, with the router's reason ("too narrow ... a Haul Truck needs 5 x 4"); arches are the truck class and are not stacked.
- **Collapse follows the supports.** Nothing new was needed in `world.js`: the tunnel rule is already 3D (`supportBonus` is a sphere round each support, `cavityLen` runs at one row), so digging up above a cube leaves roof that is anchored inside the sphere of the cubes under it and unsupported past it, with the same safe length, creak (`T.warn` x 0.35 to 1.25) and fall. `stack.load.dig-up-above-a-cube-beyond-its-reach-creaks-and-then-falls` digs it for real (steel cube: the roof is held to row 7 above the floor; a stack of two to row 11, `a-taller-stack-carries-the-roof-higher`), reads the creak timers when the first roof cell warns and runs the real stability loop to the fall. `digging-beside-a-column-follows-the-ordinary-span-rule` digs a room far from every cube at the height of the column and it comes down.
- **The column is one load path** (`loadtrace.js`). A grid cube's support carries `blk` (its cell block). `restingOn(w, s)` finds the cubes whose bottom row is the row over s's top row, `restOf(w, u)` what holds u up (ground cells, cubes by the cells they cover, cells over nothing). `totalLoad(w, s)` = what s's own sphere weighs (`loadOn`) plus, for every cube u on it, `totalLoad(u) x cells of s under u / cells of u that are held` (`STACK_XFER` 1): the whole of the upper cube's load, split between what holds it. So the cube at the bottom carries the sum of the roofs in reach of every cube of the column; a tall stack is never stronger than its weakest cube (timber under steel fails first, `the-weakest-cube-fails-first-whatever-order-the-tiers-are-in`) and never relieves the cube at the top; a cube beside the column gets none of it. `game.updateLoads`, `tickPending`, the survey and `strainOf` use `totalLoad`; `s.load` is the total ratio and `s.loadAbove` the part that came from above (the hover text says so).
- **A cube on a weaker stack is refused** (`STACK.cubeWhy`, called by `updateBuild` and by the host's `placeConflict`): the new cube's total is pushed down the cubes under it; the first one over 100% names itself ("The cube under it would carry 130% of what it can bear"). **A cube that loses what it stood on falls**: `failSupport` calls `STACK.beforeGone` / `afterGone`; a cube left with fewer than half its 16 cells held falls after a 1.3 to 2.1 s warning (`pendFail` entry with `force`, the usual creak and dust, a hint). The plates, stairs, ladders and door frames of a fallen cube are lost with it (`g.destroyEnt`, no refund, cells and reservations released). **The hammer refuses** a cube with a cube on it or with anything built into it (`STACK.deconBlock`, a hint for the host, a toast for a guest). A plate over a void is refused ("This cube hangs over a void"): a cube needs the ground or cubes under more than half of its footprint to take a plate, and the readout flags it.
- **Plates** are the ordinary pad ent (`type 'pad'`, item `pad:<material>`) with `bay` (the id of its cube), `ro` ('f' the bottom row, 'c' the top row), `op` (0 full, 1 landing: a 2 x 4 opening, 2 shaft plate: a 2 x 2 corner opening) and `od` (the opening turned 0..3 quarters). `cellsOf` skips the opening (`holeAt`), so it is a real hole in the world (walkable around, nothing to carry, a lift or a ladder goes through it). The aim (`bayAim`): the first cube the ray is inside is the bay; looking down is its floor, up its ceiling; looking up out of a cube that already has its plate into the cube stacked on it picks that cube's floor. `-` `=` pick the opening (g._sOpen), R turns it (the usual `g._bRot`). One plate per cube (two would leave 2 clear rows). `build.js` hooks: `checkBay` (the cube, the row, one plate, nothing left in the cube, not hanging), `planBayPad`, `bayPieces` (the host rebuilds a guest's plate from the cell it stands on: opening, turn, row and cube are derived and validated, never believed), `holeRails` (guard rails), `zoopKey`, `ownPlateCell`, `afterWall`, `counted`. A floor plate laid under your own feet lifts you onto it (`liftOnto`, host and guest, every frame).
- **Deck rules, as far as the stack needs them.** A plate is not a roof unless plush stands on it (`loadOn` skips a PAD cell with air over it: the floor of an upper level adds no load to the cube under it, a ceiling plate carries the pile like the roof it replaces). The weight a platform carries goes through the cube that holds it: the plate belongs to its cube, so its load path is the column. The thickness, share cap and beam reach rules of spec 2 section 4.4 (`deck.js`) are not built (there is no thick pad); a floor over a void is refused or flagged instead.
- **Escape hatch**: hold Ctrl and a pad, stair, ramp or wall aimed into a cube is the plain piece (`stack.plate.ctrl-keeps-the-plain-pad-and-stair-and-wall-in-a-cube`). The foot of the first flight needs a floor (the ground or the plate, not an opening: `the-foot-of-the-stair-needs-a-floor-not-an-opening`).

## 15. Lead notes: oriented snapping for every building part (user design, Wave 11)
- Frames (cubes), floor plates/pads, ceilings, stairs, ramps, landings, walls and catwalks must all snap together. If you turn one piece (free turn with Left/Right, any yaw), the NEXT piece must snap to it in THAT piece's own axes: flush in front/behind, left/right and above/below, at the same yaw, so a curve is a chain of turned pieces that stay joined (no more "turned frames never snap to another frame").
- Model: every placed part carries a yaw and an oriented cell-grid origin (gridOrigin + yaw). Snap candidates are computed in the target piece's local frame (module steps of 4 cells for cubes/plates, per-part sizes for stairs/ramps), then rotated to world. Turned neighbors must not cut through each other (the existing footprint overlap rule) and each placed part still needs its dug section/clearance.
- Mixed parts snap to each other: a plate to a cube floor/ceiling, stairs/ramps into a cube's opening, a cube on a turned plate, all in the turned frame. Chains can turn again at any piece (the new piece's yaw becomes the chain's next frame). Hover/ghost shows which side it snaps to; Down arrow still returns to the world grid.
- Load tracing, hammer, save/load (store yaw and local grid coordinates), multiplayer (host re-derives the snap, never trusts a guest transform), belts/rail/lifts along curved chains are within the clearance contract.
- Tests: a 90 degree curve of 8 cubes, a 45 degree chain of plates, stairs into a turned cube, a spiral of turned cubes stacked, remove-middle-piece, save/load, both multiplayer roles.
- Placement modes (user, binds Wave 11): every frame/part placement offers BOTH modes every time, switchable on the fly with one key (Up arrow toggles, shown in the hint as SNAP or FREE, and remembered while the tool is in hand): SNAP = grid/oriented-grid snapping to neighbors; FREE = the part sits exactly where you aim, any position and any yaw (Left/Right turn, Shift fine), centered on the aim point, still requires its dug section/clearance and refuses cutting through neighbors. While in FREE mode, getting within a small distance of a neighbor shows the snap ghost and a tap of Up or holding Alt snaps it to that neighbor. Down arrow returns to the world grid. Saves store mode-independent results (position, yaw).

## 17. Wave 10 audit: what was found and fixed (stacked building)

The audit read the code, did not trust the report, and attacked it in a live game: dig-up collapses over cubes of all nine tiers near the bay and over steel, titanium, concrete and timber cubes at 200 and 500 m, stacks of every height up to 17 cubes, brick walls (every row shifted two cells against the one under it), cascades, saves and loads in the middle of a fall (a real `save` and `loadWorld`), a real wandering player over three levels, the stair walked sprinting, jumping, crouching and slowed, ladders from every side, a building across the chunk seams in x, z and y, forged and garbled `place`, `decon` and `cfg` messages, and seeded random edits with the invariants checked after every step (60 seeds swept clean). Tests: `audit_stack_load.js`, `audit_stack_walk.js`, `audit_stack_soak.js` (prefix `stack.audit.`) and `audit_stack_mp.js` (`mp.stack.audit.`). Each was run against the code as built first; the fixes are small edits.
- **The load index could serve a stand-in cube.** `supportHolds(e, kind, 'kind')` (the borer and the roof bolter asking "would a cube here hold") pushes a stand-in into `w.supports` and pops it again. The next cube they set makes the list the same length again, and the index of cubes (`stackIndex` in `loadtrace.js`, `cubeAt` in `stack.js`) was keyed on the list and its length, so it kept serving the stand-in (`id: 'hyp'`): the cube under it saw "a cube above" that was not in the world, so when the lower cube fell the upper one was never told to fall. Both caches now also compare the last support in the list.
- **Setting a cube on a brick wall froze the game.** `cubeWhy` followed every path down the column; in a wall laid like bricks each cube rests on two cubes, so the paths are 2 to the power of the height: 2,888 ms for one aimed frame at 9 rows, doubling per row, and it runs every frame you aim a cube at the top (and for every guest `place`). It now pushes the new cube's load down once, highest cube first, with the sum of what reached each cube, and judges each cube once: 8 ms at 9 rows, 15 ms at 14 rows, the same answers. A 17 cube column weighs in 1.5 ms per cube (worst), the check for a cube on it 2.8 ms; a 112 cube brick wall weighs every cube in 309 ms (2.8 ms each).
- **A cube that was falling when you saved hung in the air for ever after a new session.** The fall was only in `g.pendFail` (memory). A cube left over a hole by a fallen cube under it stood there after a real quit and load, with nothing left to make it fall (the builder's own test passed only because the same page still held the old warning). `forceFall` now marks the entity (`fell`), `loadWorld` calls `resumeFalls` and the fall comes back with a fresh 1.3 to 2.1 s warning, counted once in `stackCascades`.
- **A warning of the last game could fall on the next one.** `pendFail` was never cleared when a world was built or loaded, and support ids start again at 2 in every world, so an old warning (an overloaded cube too, not only the new forced falls) could break an unrelated support of the new game. `loadWorld` now starts with an empty `pendFail`.
- **A slowed or crouching walker stuck at the top of the stair.** The collision lifts the feet a hair as you press into the edge of the plate, and the snap onto the stair tread (`walkStep`) put them back every substep. At the normal speed the push is hard enough to trip the step assist; at 75% of it (or crouching, or slowed by dust) the walker stood on the second last tread for ever, in every turn of the switchback (12 of 12 combinations). `walkStep` now treats a floor cell (a pad or a plate) beside the tread that is higher by less than 0.2 m, with three clear rows over it, as a lip you step onto when your body overlaps its edge (`lipBeside`).
- **The builder's soak miscounted a cascade.** It counted lost parts only at its own `failSupport` step, so a cascade that ended during a later step failed it on seeds 9 and 16 of the first 21 tried. It now counts every `destroyEnt` of a part (`audit_stack_soak.js` does the same).
- **Second pass (re-audit of the same tree).** Two things broke the stack tests and neither was a stack bug. (1) `game.js` lost the `totalLoad` import from `loadtrace.js` while other waves edited it, so `updateLoads` and `strainOf` threw `ReferenceError` on the first frame with a cube in the world (21 of 102 `stack.` tests failed at once): the import is back (`capacityOf, loadOn, totalLoad, WARN_AT`). (2) `stack.transit.the-contract-walkers-pass-a-plated-tunnel-...` failed whenever the Mine Rail test before it had run (7 of 8 tries): `g._rail.nodes` outlives a new world until the next rail tick, and `logi.held` reads it, so the old track's cells stood in the new yard and the router said "the pile blocks every route". The rail test now takes its track down (`X.clean()` and one `adv`). A third, in the collapse area: `qa.load.an-overloaded-roof-warns-for-3-to-5-seconds-...` failed about 30% of runs, because the warning is 3 to 5 s (`3 + Math.random() * 2`) and the test stepped 40 times 0.1 s after 0.4 s of pump (the long countdowns never ended inside it); it steps 70 times now and passes 40 of 40. After these, `stack.` is 102 of 102, `mp.stack.` 12 of 12, `haul.contract.` 26 of 26, and the groups machines, belts, power, mp, ui, info, hall, mining, qa, stress, transit, rail, haul and detector pass. The full run of 2,837 tests had 14 failures, none in the stack: the dial layout and scoop dial tests (`audit_hud.*`, `hud.every-dial-...`), `upg.craft.strut-and-jack-radius-match-text` and `addons.ui.inventory-modal-shows-every-item-and-details` (the raw materials left the pack), `content.structural-survey-load-matches-the-trace` (the `coverDepth` rule: a trench with no roof over it reads no depth, and where the slope is low there is no roof), and the known flakes (`ui.button-words-...`, `split.mesh-...`, `mp.slide.guest-near-...`, `hands.bots-haul-full-cart`, `audit.2b.merger-lane-...` and `truth.dom.windows-after-esc-...`, each passes alone).

## 18. Light through holes: daylight down a shaft dug up and out of the pile (API notes)

User request: "if you dig a hole up and out, light should come through it if there is nothing in the way". Files: `src/holelight.js` (the rule, two evaluators), `src/lightshaft.js` (the beam and the floor pool), the hole channel in `scanChunk` and the cascade in `updateChunks` of `src/render.js`, the camera light in `renderEnv` of `src/game.js`, the hole terms in `src/shaders.js`, one line in the TUNNEL CRAFT chalkboard, the how-to text and the README. Tests: `holelight.js`, `holelight_lib.js`, `holelight_legacy.js` (the remesh as it was, for the cost test and "no hole means exactly the old light") and `holelight_mp.js`.

- **What a hole is.** An air cell with nothing above it in its column, at the bottom of a well: its neighbours rise at least 3 cells over it (the rim), at least two of its four straight neighbours are walls 2 cells over it, and it is walled in on all eight sides within two cells. So a shaft one or two plush across is a hole; a steep natural slope (the pile rises about 3 cells between neighbours at its steepest), a cliff foot, a tunnel mouth or a wide pit is not, and `holelight.the-natural-pile-has-no-holes-on-any-seed` walks 3 seeds and the live world to prove the rule never fires on untouched pile (an earlier two-wall rule fired on 4% of the columns). Plate, wall section, door and plush are solid cells, so a column they close is not a hole any more; a bare frame cube is a lattice of beams and lets the light through.
- **Strength.** 1 for the first 12 cells (7.2 m) under the rim, fading (smoothstep) to 0.25 by 28 cells (16.8 m): the same 7 m and 17 m as the tunnel rule in `renderEnv`. A hole therefore acts as an entrance, and `renderEnv` also moves the tunnel entrance onto the player standing where the hole is strong (`sky > 0.6`), so the existing 7 m and 17 m glow rule applies round it.
- **Spill.** From every hole cell along covered air at the same height, eight way steps that may not cut the corner of plush, at most 6 steps, losing `(1 - d / 7)^3` (0.63, 0.36, 0.19, 0.08, 0.02, 0.003). The nearest hole cell wins, ties go to the stronger. A plush, a plate, a wall section or a closed door in the way is not air and stops it.
- **Two evaluators, one rule.** `holeAt(w, i, j, k)` answers one cell (the camera: `renderEnv` takes `max` of it and the plain roof light; inside a hole the old 1 becomes the hole's strength, so a deep shaft lights you less). `windowFor(w, i0, j0, k0, scratch)` fills the field of a whole chunk (a window of 34 x 34 columns, the levels j0 - 1 to j0 + 16) with a multi source ring search; `holelight.the-chunk-field-and-the-single-cell-light-agree-on-every-air-cell` compares them on every air cell of three scenes (plain, plugged, ragged).
- **The plush.** `scanChunk` bakes the hole light into the fraction of the flag channel (`flag + 0.97 * hole`, flag 0, 1 or 2; the shader splits it again with `floor(z + 0.01)`), and the hole cells no longer count in the plain sky of their neighbours. The shader adds `uHoleSun * wrap^2 * hx + mix(ground, uHoleHemi, hm) * 0.9 * hx` with `hx = max(hole - sky, 0)`; `uHoleSun`, `uHoleHemi` and `uHoleLv` are the hall level only, set in `renderEnv`, **not** dimmed by how deep the camera stands (`lo`), which is the point: a lit shaft in an otherwise dark tunnel. At hall level 0 (night, grid down) they are 0, so the hole gives only what the hall gives. Plush with no hole light near them keep the old loop of `scanChunk` untouched (`holelight.no-hole-means-exactly-the-old-light` compares floats against the old remesh).
- **Remesh cost.** Nothing near was ever edited (`anyEdited`, nine map reads) costs nothing. Otherwise one 34 x 34 window of column tops (about 9 us) finds the holes; only a chunk with a hole in reach runs the field search, and only plush inside the box the light can reach take a second look. `holelight.the-remesh-costs-at-most-15-percent-more-on-a-dug-area` measures it against the old scan on a dug test area (tunnel, with and without the shaft, shallow and deep remesh, 3 attempts, `window.__holeCost` has the numbers). An edit changes the light of the chunks round it as far as 6 cells, further than the two cell border `markDirty` re-meshes: a dirty chunk with a hole in reach (or that had one) sends its 26 neighbours round again once (`ch.casc`, `renderer.spill`), `holelight.an-edit-that-blocks-a-hole-re-lights-the-chunk-next-door-through-the-real-remesh` puts the shaft 3 cells from a chunk border and checks the next chunk with the real `updateChunks`.
- **The beam and the pool** (`lightshaft.js`). One soft vertical sheet per shaft (connected hole columns, eight way) that turns to face you, brightest near the opening, shown with `hall.level * (1 - 0.92 * camSky)` so it fades in the daylit hall and at night. A tunnel on the hall floor has no plush under it to light, so the light of level 0 is also drawn as a pool of quads on the floor (corner values averaged over the cells that meet there, stopping at walls and plush; pulled 32 cm toward the eye along the line of sight because the hall floor is one plane 9.8 km wide whose depth is only good to a few decimetres). Both refresh when an edit is consumed by the renderer, when you move 5 cells or every 2 s.
- **Multiplayer.** Nothing about the light is sent: it is a pure function of the cells, so the cell sync, `sendWorld` and a loaded save carry it. `mp.holelight.*` runs the real cell handler and the real late joiner list into a second world and compares the baked light cell for cell (a dig, a block, a re-dig, a host cave-in), a guest dig reaching the host's screen, and a guest's forged `bulk` command on the door that shuts the light out (refused; a plain wall cell is taken and the light finds the gap).
- **Not done / for the lead.** The hall floor mesh itself (a standard material) is not lit by the hole except through the pool at floor level; machines and bots standing in the light keep their old light; the plain roof light under a plate laid right over a tunnel (one cell of roof) is still the old 0.74 of `exp(-0.3 n)`, only the hole light stops; a bare frame cube does not shade a shaft; shafts wider than 2 cells are not holes (their plush keep the plain sky light, which is already bright near open columns). There is no cell readout in the game (hover reads machines and parts), so there is no hover text for the light.

- **Daylight audit: what was found and fixed.** (1) *Stale light under deep shafts.* The strength of a hole is measured from its rim and whether a column is a hole at all depends on column tops, so opening, capping or lowering the mouth of a shaft changes the light of every chunk level under it, but the cascade of `updateChunks` only reached the chunk levels next to the edited one, and an edit in a chunk that is not loaded (a mouth 40 m over your head, beyond the 30 m render radius; piles are up to 72 cells) had no chunk record to cascade from at all. Now `scanChunk` reports `hole` for any hole column in its window at any level (`S.holes` of `windowFor`), the cascade sends every level of the 3 x 3 stack of chunk columns, and `Renderer.farEdit` does the same for a dirty chunk that is not loaded when a hole is in its window now or a loaded chunk of its stack had hole light. An edit near a shaft re-meshes about 27 chunks (12 ms in all, spread over a few frames). Tests: `holelight.audit.a-deep-shaft-opened...`, `a-cap-laid-on-the-rim...`, `a-mouth-too-high...`, `random-edits-around-a-deep-shaft...` (compare `renderer.chunks` with a fresh scan; the plain sky of a buried plush, float 9, has always lagged by a hair under 0.01 and is let go). (2) *The tunnel glow outlived the hole.* `renderEnv` remembers where the sky was last open (`_entr`) so a tunnel stays lit 7 m from there; standing at a hole made the hole the entrance, and shutting the hole left the glow (`uGlow` 0.39, `uCamSky` 0.66 under a plate) until you walked 17 m. The entrance now carries the cell it was lit through (`_entr.hole`) and is dropped (moved far away) when `holeAt` of that cell is 0. (3) README said "deeper than 3 cells": the rule is 3 cells or more. Checked and fine: the rule against an independent reference on 3 seeds of random edits (both evaluators and the baked plush agree to 1e-6), `restoreDiff` marking (+28 ms for 300,000 cells), remesh and refresh timings (scan 0.5 ms a chunk, refresh 0.3 ms with 28 shafts, `holeAt` about 1 us), save and load in the middle of a dig, a late joiner's displayed chunks, garbage `cmd` and `cells` messages. Left as designed or out of scope: a bare frame cube (a lattice) and machines in a shaft do not block the light; a forged `cells` message from a guest is applied as sent (no range check, pre-existing: it is how guest digging reaches the host).

## 19. Bins audit: what was found and fixed

The audit read the code, did not trust the report, and attacked it in a live game: three soaks of seeded random edits (a yard with two bots, a cart, a belt line with a mech, a truck and a digger, and Depot Beacons that come and go, lose power, get renamed, get assigned and unassigned, with a real `save` and `loadWorld` and forged guest commands in the middle, invariants checked after every step), forged bot orders and names from a guest, a late joiner who is sent depots saved before bins had numbers, the controls table and the README read against the keys, and timings with 2000 machines and 3000 belt tiles. Tests: `audit_bins_save.js`, `audit_bins_flow.js`, `audit_bins_soak.js`, `audit_bins_ui.js`, `audit_bins_perf.js` (prefix `audit.bins.`) and `audit_bins_mp.js` (`mp.bins.audit-`). Each was run against the code as built first (the fixes are small edits); the soak found nothing more once these were fixed (12 seeds clean), and a test on a real grid (a burning generator, a pole, a depot) shows the depot is a bin while the generator burns, dark when it stops, and dark in a grid surge.

- **A saved game with a mech that has a bin crashed the belt system on load.** `mechMay` kept its line cache as `m._lc` (a Map holding Sets) and `m.lockT` on the mech tile itself. The tile is the entity that goes into the save, so the Map was written as `{}` and read back as a plain object: `c.get is not a function` on the first plush the mech tried to hand over, every tick, inside `Logistics.update`. The cache now lives in a `WeakMap` in `bins.js` (`mechRuntime`), so neither a save nor a guest's `ent+` carries it, and `lockT` can no longer outlive the session's clock (a stale one showed "Held:" on an idle mech after a load). `bins_lines.js` read `m.lockT`; it now reads the hover line.
- **A bot with a bin of its own sold a cart's load at its own bin.** `crew.js` only set `haulDest` from the cart when the bot had no `dest`, so a cart bound for Depot A, hauled by a bot assigned to Depot B, was sold at B. The cart's bin now wins for that load (`botBin` reads `haulDest` first), then the bot's own.
- **E did not paste a copied bin onto your own cart**, though the controls table and the report say it does (the test of that name pasted on a borer, a bot and a truck). A cart is no machine, so `ext.useKey` never sees it. `binspanel.pasteToCart` (first line of `useKey`) does it, with an empty crew too.
- **A depot saved before bins had numbers cost a scan of every entity per lookup.** `numOf` fell back to filtering `S.entities` for each `binById`, which a bot asks every tick and a belt end for every plush: 15 us per call with 2000 machines, now 0.2 us. `beaconBin` and `nextNum` number the depots first (once, in the order placed).
- **The tally of a depot taken down stayed for ever** (and kept growing from a truck on its way there), and went to every guest in each 0.5 s row. `onBeaconGone` drops it and `note` ignores a bin that is not there (the sale itself still pays).
- **`subjects()` listed every belt and mech tile**, a Detector Gate, a splitter and a lift too, which cannot take a bin (the soak tripped on it). It lists what `assignable` accepts.
- **A stale "back to the SORT bin" trip was never asked again.** `hallTrip` stayed set when a trip was cut short by an order, so the next `goHome` went to the SORT bin whatever the battery said. `goHome` clears it and asks the battery again.
- **Names.** The travel menu wrote the name into HTML unescaped (`&amp;` typed by a player showed as `&`; no markup can get in, `<` and `>` are stripped). A name of zero width characters, a right to left override or half of a surrogate pair is cleaned (the depot showed an empty name before).
- **A bin change forced a new solve of the power grid.** `applyCfg` marked the grid dirty for every patch; a bin changes nothing about what a machine draws, so a bin only patch (a paste onto 30 trucks, or a guest toggling) skips it.
- **A loaded game kept what the last one had said.** `loadWorld` now empties `_binSaid`, so a fallback message is not hushed by the old game's 90 s memory.
- **Looked at and clean:** forged `bindest`, `cfg` (bin, name, `__proto__`, floats, strings, huge numbers, other kinds of entities), `sell` with bad bins and `crew` `ctx` with a bin that is a vault, a truck, 0, -2, 1e9, NaN, an array, an object (all refused with a toast, nothing changes); the guest's rename cleaned the same on both screens; the money is the same at every bin on every path (hand sale, bot, belt end, truck, dozer chute, borer, claw, Portal, rail, cart); a truck with The One and a depot still refuses without a Vehicle Scanner on the road; a bot with The One goes to the SORT bin whatever its bin; the panels, the crew rows and the toasts read right in a headless screenshot (`docs/bins-audit/`).
- **Timings** (quiet machine, 2000 machines, 12 depots, 3000 belts): `resolve` 0.2 us, `pickHall` 0.1 us, `nearest` 6 us, `endSink` 13 us (two scans of the machine list for each plush that reaches a belt end, so 100 line ends moving 5 plush a second cost about 6 ms a second), `listBins` 21 us, hovering a depot 0.5 ms with 20,000 belts, the panel's list 6 ms with 20,000 belts once a second.
- **Not changed, for the lead:** a truck whose own bin has no road (a frame too narrow) stays blocked and says why instead of falling back to Auto (a bin out of charge range and a dark bin do fall back); a bot selected in the crew turns E on a depot into "unload there from now on" instead of the travel menu until you let it go with Esc; the hand cart forgets its bin when it is stowed and rolled out again; a grid surge makes every assigned subject say "no power" once (up to 5 toasts show); `hud.every-dial-sits-in-the-left-or-right-group...` (the scoop dial) and `upg.craft.strut-and-jack-radius-match-text` fail in this tree for reasons outside bins.

## 20. Belts and the Vacuum Hose: laying and function, studied from Satisfactory (internal)

What Satisfactory does (wiki, satisfactory.wiki.gg Conveyor Belts and the pipeline pages; the build gun flow is from play, not from the wiki, and is marked): **Produce.** One plate per roughly 0.5 m of belt, rounded up per segment (so a few long segments are cheaper than many short ones); Mk1 is Iron Plate (Tier 0 HUB), Mk2 Reinforced Iron Plate (Tier 2), Mk3 Steel Beam (Tier 4), Mk4 Encased Industrial Beam (Tier 5), Mk5 Alclad Sheet (Tier 7), Mk6 needs Ficsite Trigon and Time Crystal (Tier 9); the unlock ladder is the research ladder. **Lay.** Belts run between ports or poles; the first snap becomes the input and the second the output unless the first is a machine input; a segment is 0.5 to 56 m, minimum turn radius 2 m, slope 35 degrees at most, a belt cannot rise and turn in one piece; R cycles default, straight (90 degree turns only) and curve (like a railway) modes; poles are placed with the belt; a higher mark aimed over a belt upgrades that one segment in place and refunds the old materials; clearance errors show red (build gun, from play: click to start, the hologram follows the aim with a live length, click sets it down, right click or Q cancels). **Pipes** add the same: auto placed supports, junctions snapped to open ends, flow that backs up. **Function.** Belts never drop items, a full or rejecting end backs up the whole line, throughput is the mark's items per minute (60, 120, 270, 480, 780, 1200), splitters and mergers are 1 to 3 and 3 to 1.

What Rot Factory takes from it (this wave): the whole route is previewed before anything is paid for, with its length, its rate and its cost in the hint, and a click commits all of it (`beltplan.js` laying mode, the same router as the Line Planner); a new route starts at the open end of an existing line and goes on in that line's direction, or at a machine port (a sorter output, behind a mech), and ends snapped to a port (a sorter, vault, generator, the bin); the corners are quarter arcs, in the belt bed and in the hose tube (`hosegeo.js`); a higher mark set over a belt still upgrades it in place; the hammer and X take a piece down and hand the item back; red cells mark what blocks a route; throughput is printed in the readout (a hose runs at twice a belt); a full line backs up and nothing falls off (a line ending in the open spills, as before). What is Rot Factory's own: the **gold rule** (the last piece of any belt or hose route within suck range of a bin, a Depot Beacon or an assigned bin glows gold, the hologram turns gold and the hint says "This end feeds the bin: stop here."), the **single mouth** of a hose (one flared open start, only it sucks) and the hose's **no junction** rule (no splitters, mergers or gates converted onto it). Not taken: poles for belts (a belt here stands on the floor), Mk6 gating by a rare drop, the straight and curve build modes (the route shapes of the planner stand in for them), upgrading a stretch of an existing line in one route (set the higher mark over it, or hold B and drag).

Audit of this wave (tests `audit_hose_*.js`): a guest's rail-rule turn is now checked by the host (`Logistics.turnPrevOk`: the open end of a plain line one cell from the new piece, a quarter turn toward it; a forged turn used to spin any open belt anywhere), a guest's item must be the one that makes the machine it names, set on a real cell facing a real way, and only a ramp climbs (`placeConflict`), a forged splitter or gate request on a hose is refused like the aim refuses it, and a planned hose line that ends facing back into itself is refused by the host (`validate`). The aim a few cells past the end of a hose shows the run it will lay and no mouth (`bridgeRun`, shared by the aim and `bridgeFrom`). A guest's own pieces stand in for themselves for three seconds (`Logistics.standIn` and `look`, used only by the planning helpers and `canPlace`, never drawn, never saved, replaced by the host's piece), so a sweep, a bend and the gap fill work while the host is still answering. A splitter feeds a hose at its side outputs too (no mouth there), no gate goes on a hose, the vacuum no longer pulls plush on a left click while a belt, a hose or any tool is out, the gold ring is read again the moment a network changes (it waited up to 0.8 s), a mouth that finds nothing looks again after 0.2 s instead of scanning every loose plush every frame, and the route preview shares its geometry instead of building new pieces for every aimed cell (a geometry nobody disposes stays in the graphics card). Known and left: a hose piece set in front of a Mech Scooper or an Out Vault is drawn with a mouth (a mech does not count as a feeder); a hose at 0 held pieces cannot start a route (with none in the pack the hands are out), so the shortfall is only bought when you hold at least one.

Feel pass (tests `beltfeel_*.js`): the chevrons on a belt bed point the way plush travel and slide that way (the canvas was drawn apex first, so they pointed backward); a belt corner is a real quarter circle of curved deck and two curved rails (`beltgeo.js`, the circle of the hose bend), not three beds fanned round a cell; a ramp going down is drawn from the deck of its own level to the one below (it hung one level too high), and the plush sit on a ramp's deck and hand over at the same height; and a route can be drawn like a cord: press the mouse button where the line starts (or at its open end), aim along the way it should go, let go. The cord is as long as the pieces you hold (`cordLimit` in `beltplan.js`: the preview ends where they run out, with an amber marker on the cell it stopped short of, and nothing is bought), the bends are put in, and the next press at its open end starts the next stretch, turning the way you aim. A press that is let go on the cell it began on only anchors (click, then click lays all of it, buying what you do not hold, as before). Plush on a belt, ramp, lift, bend or hose were always small copies of their own shape and colour (the one instanced mesh of each shape, `feedDynamic`); the test keeps it so.

## 20. Belt Intake: belts pull plush off your hands and cart (API notes)
User request: the vacuum hose already sucks up free fall from an avalanche; a conveyor should instead pull from your hands or cart when you are close, X plush a second within X metres, with upgrades like Satisfactory that raise how much it pulls a second.
- **Files.** `src/beltintake.js` holds all of it (no `game.js` import, so `catalog_belts.js` can read the upgrade and the readout from it): the numbers, `INTAKE_UPGRADE`, `intakeLines`, the pull `update(g, dt)` (called in `updatePlay` just before `autoDump`), and the host side of a guest's `feed` (`hostFeed`). Hooks elsewhere are one line each: `game.js` (the import, `BI.update`, `!BI.beltHolds(...)` in `autoDump` and `autoDumpCart`, `case 'feed'` in `runNetCmd`), `catalog_belts.js` (the upgrade in `UPGRADES`, `intakeLines` in the belt's `infoExtra`), no key and no controls row. Tests: `src/tests/intake_core.js` (`intake.`) and `mp_intake.js` (`mp.intake.`).
- **Numbers.** Intake Mk1 comes free with Conveyor Belts (`T.machines` has `belt`): 2 plush a second within 2.0 m. The upgrade `beltIntake` (machines tab, needs Conveyor Belts) has 7 levels, Mk2 to Mk8: rate 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 a second, range 2.6, 3.2, 3.8, 4.3, 4.9, 5.5, 6.0 m, prices 25K, 125K, 625K, 3.1M, 15M, 78M, 390M (about 5x a level, from just after Belt Motors to past Mk6 belts at 150M). `T.intakeLevel` is the only tuning field (`intakeOf(T)` reads rate and range from the tables, so an old save and a default tuning need nothing).
- **Who gets pulled.** A tile takes plush only if it is a plain belt or ramp (`eligible`: not a lift, underground end, splitter, merger, Smart splitter, gate or hose), within the range of the source on the floor plan and within 1.8 m of the deck in height. Sources: your hands (only with bare hands or the hammer out, so building near belts never drains them), your cart (any mode, measured from the cart), and on the host the friend's cart (`S.gcart`). Hands go first. The One and every id from `SPECIAL_MIN` up are never pulled (`pullable`).
- **Rate.** One token bucket per player (`host`, `guest`), refilled at the rate with a burst of a quarter second (at least 2), shared by hands and cart (the guest's bucket is shared by its hand feeds and its cart), so many belts add nothing. The tile that takes a plush is the nearest one that `accept()`s it, which keeps the belt spacing and the 3 plush per tile cap: a full belt backs up and the pull stops, and a fast intake needs a fast line (one Mk1 tile takes about 4.7 a second at full power).
- **Bin precedence.** `nearestSink` is asked how far the bin (or a depot, or a sorter) is; if it is as close or closer, the belt does nothing and `autoDump` works as before. If the belt is closer and any tile in range has room, the belt keeps the matching `autoDump` timer (`_adT`, `_adC`, `_adCg`) held back for 0.6 s (`beltHolds`), so the bin does not take the whole load at once; a belt that is full lets the bin take over after the hold runs out.
- **Cost.** Nothing runs with empty hands, no cart load or no belts. Otherwise the belts near the source are found by reading the cells around it (or walking the tiles when there are 360 or fewer) every 0.12 s, then filtered and sorted by the live distance each frame: 0.08 ms a frame with 500 or 2,000 belt tiles while pulling, 0.01 ms far from every belt, with the same cost at both sizes.
- **Co-op.** The host pulls from its own hands and cart and the friend's cart. The friend's hands are on their screen, so the guest client finds the belt in its own copy of the line, skips one that looks full, and sends `feed` per plush with `pull:1`; the host (`hostFeed`) checks that the species is real and not special, the belt is eligible, the guest stands within the range plus 1.2 m (and 1.8 + 1.2 m in height), and the guest's bucket has a token, then calls `accept`. Whatever it refuses (and any plush a full belt turns away) goes back to the guest's hands in a `give` message, which also fixes an old loss: a hand drop onto a full belt used to vanish. Unflagged feeds (the old hand drop) keep working, within 3.0 m plus the slack and 10 a second. A `feed` to anything that is not a belt or a sorter is refused (a vault, a generator or a charger takes plush by `hand`, never by `feed`), a sorter takes one only from a guest within the autoDump reach plus the slack (what it refuses goes back), and The One is refused on every feed (it is never in anyone's hands: finding it ends the hunt on the spot). A guest position that is not a number never passes a range check (the checks are written `!(d <= reach)`). The host cannot see what the guest really holds, so a forged `feed` can still mint plush at the guest's rate, the same trust the old `sell` and `cartload` commands always had.
- **No switch.** Intake is always on once you own Conveyor Belts (the old Backquote toggle and the `intake` net command are gone; a saved `S.beltIntakeOff` is deleted on the first update and ignored). A first pull also shows a one time hint (`S.beltIntakeTold`).
- **Looks.** Each pull sends a flier from your hands or cart to the tile (at most 4 a frame, 80 alive), a closing cyan ring on the tile (one per source), a spark (every 0.25 s) and a soft sound (every 0.22 s).
- **Not done.** No dial in the belt of dials and no pause menu switch (the key and the readout are enough); no sitting rider check (a player seated in a Mine Rail cart that passes belts is drained like anyone else; turn it off with the key).

## 21. One fuel rule and bots that keep machines fueled (API notes)

Files: `src/botfuel.js` (all of it, imports only plushdata, config, power.js and powerparts.js), small hooks in `logistics.js` (`accept`), `game.js` (hands, throws, hover text, `dyn` rows, the `crew` command `fuelcfg`, the bot panel), `crew.js` (states, hooks), `ui.js` (crew panel), `powerparts.js` (the Portable's `hop` is 1). Tests: `botfuel_lib.js` (kit), `botfuel_rule.js`, `botfuel_bots.js`, `botfuel_scoop.js`, `botfuel_ctl.js`, `botfuel_open.js` (prefix `botfuel.`) and `mp_botfuel.js` (`mp.botfuel.`).
- **The rule.** Machines that eat plush: `gen` (every rung) and `charger`, nothing else (a test walks every tile type). `fuelCap(g, t)` is the hopper (a generator: `genHopper`, 50 at the start, Fuel Hoppers and the rungs raise it; the Charging Station: 50), `fuelRoom(g, t)` what is free, `fuelAccepts(g, t, item)` is room plus rarity (Common to Epic, a whole number species id below 60,000: The One, decoys, pads, caches and made-up ids are refused). `logistics.accept` calls it, so belts, sorters, hands (`useTile`), throws (`feedGensFromThrows`, `feedChargersFromThrows`), the host's `tile` command and bots all share it. The old exports `CHARGE_PER`, `CHARGER_CAP`, `CHARGER_HOPPER`, `CHARGER_MAX_RARITY` still come from `logistics.js` (they now live in `botfuel.js`).
- **Rarity.** A generator runs a plush for `ENERGY_KJ[r] / output` seconds (`BURN_SECONDS` in `power.js`: 270, 720, 1,800, 4,500 at 8 kW, so a full 8 kW hopper of Commons lasts 3 h 45 min and of Epics 2 d 15 h), unchanged by this feature. The station gives `CHARGE_PER[r]` bot batteries (0.34, 0.7, 1.5, 4). `fuelLines(g, t)` writes the two hover lines (per plush, and what a full hopper lasts), `fuelName(g, t)` the crew's name for a machine ("Generator A", the letter is the machine's rank by id among its kind; a Turbine is "Turbine Generator A"), `longTime` reads hours and days.
- **The switch.** `S.crewFuel` (`false` is off, absent is on) and `bot.fuelOff` (true is off). `FUEL.enabled(g, b)` needs both. A guest sends `cmd crew { act: 'fuelcfg', id, on }` (id 0 is the crew); `setToggle` takes only a boolean and a whole non-negative id and ignores anything else. `selftest.fresh` puts the crew switch back on.
- **Top-up.** `tryTopUp(crew, b)` runs when a bot with a load starts `goHome`, when its `return` path ends at the bin (before the gate and the sale), and every 1.5 s while it idles with a load (and no drop-off). It looks at the machines within 60 m and 2.2 m of height, takes the emptiest (counting slots other bots reserved) and, among those within 10 percentage points of the emptiest, the nearest it can reach on its battery with the walk to its bin. It reserves `min(room, plush it carries that the machine takes)` in `b.fuelJob = { id, n, k: 'give' }`, walks there (`fwalk`), hands over the cheapest plush first (`fgive`, one every 0.12 s, each through `logi.accept`) and then tops up the next machine (up to 4) or goes to its bin (`b.fuelDone` stops the loop until it unloads).
- **Scooping.** An idle bot (not told to stay, no drop-off, battery 45% or more, empty bucket) polls `tryScoop`: a generator under 40% full whose grid draws more than 0.1 kW, or a station under 40% full with less than half a charge in reserve, is `starving`. The emptiest is chosen, `crew.order` sends the bot to the nearest pile face with `b.fuelJob = { id, n: min(room, bucket), k: 'scoop' }` and `b.scoopHome`. `thinkFarm` then digs only plush `legalCell` allows (and keeps it in the bucket instead of putting it on a belt), `scoopCheck` starts the `give` walk when the bucket holds what the machine needs, `scoopFail` ends an errand that finds nothing legal (the machine is set aside for that bot for 90 s). `afterUnload` clears `scoopHome` so no dig order is left behind.
- **Order of things.** A bot under 35% battery does nothing here (`lowBattery`, `beam` and `afterUnload` still decide); `crew.clearFuel` runs on every order of the player's (`order`, `stand` which also sets `b.stay`, `follow`, `sendHome`, `command`); assigned bins are untouched (`home(b)` after the last machine).
- **Count.** Nothing is made: a plush leaves the bucket only through `logi.accept`, a scooped plush leaves the pile through `removeCell` as before and is counted by `mechDug`, a bot that is interrupted keeps what it carries and sells it. Fuel handed over is `S.stats.botFuel` (the first one toasts), never `stats.sold`.
- **Multiplayer.** The host simulates. `dyn` crew rows end with `[fuel machine id, 1 give or 2 scoop, bot switch]` (indexes 18 to 20) and the message has `cf` (0 when the crew switch is off). A guest shows the status line ("Fueling Generator A", "Scooping fuel for Generator A"), a small puff at the machine, the hopper from the tile row and the switches in the crew panel, and moves nothing. The `tile` command now cuts a list to the guest's hands (`T.carry`) and refuses rows that are not whole species ids.
- **Audit (what was found and fixed).** `audit_botfuel_flow.js`, `audit_botfuel_walls.js`, `audit_botfuel_perf.js`, `audit_botfuel_mp.js` (all under the prefix `botfuel.audit-`):
  - A scooping bot's own dig order (`b.origin`, flagged `b.scoopHome`) used to outlive the errand when it was cut short (Go home and unload, a drop-off order, a Recharge order, a low battery with a station), so the bot went back to the pile and dug on its own, with no fuel rule, trip after trip. Now `clearFuel` leaves the flag alone, only a dig order of the player's, `stand` and `follow` clear it, `resumeDig` and `thinkFarm` send a flagged bot with no errand home, and `afterUnload` drops the order.

## Wire-only power (the end of range power)

Why: poles, generators, batteries and breakers used to auto-link to each other within `T.poleLink` and every machine drew from the nearest node within `T.poleReach`, with faint blue link lines drawn, so hand laid cables were nearly optional. Now WIRES are the way power works (Satisfactory style).

Rules (one place each):
- Nodes (generators of every rung, poles, batteries, breakers; switches join two grids) join a grid ONLY through cables. `power.recompute` has no distance test left. The faint blue `LineSegments` is gone; cables.js draws the sagging wires (green live, orange weak with sparks, red dead).
- A consumer (mechs, borers, claws and rigs, fans, sorters, earth movers, lights, hanging lanterns, catalog machines, arches, doors, lifts, jump pads, rail stations, depot beacons, meters, scanners, chargers that draw power) is powered only when a cable runs from it to a node of a grid that has supply. Standing against a pole or generator gives nothing. `Cables.checkEnds` refuses machine to machine cables (except lantern to lantern: a string of lanterns hangs on one node).
- A pole has no power of its own. It is a hub, dead until a cable path links it to a generator (or a charged battery): `gridinfo.js` `isLive`, `deadReason`, the model lamp (`logistics.js`, `M.dead` when dark) and the readout `POWER POLE . LIVE/DEAD` all use the solver's `net.supply`. A machine wired to a dead pole is unpowered and the readout says why (`No power: no generator is wired to it`, `its generator is out of fuel`, `the grid is tripped`).
- The one exception, belt lines: the chain of belt tiles that hand plush to one another (`beltLine`, `nextOf` in both directions, ramps, lifts, undergrounds, hoses, splitters, mergers included) is ONE load and ONE cable endpoint. A cable ending on any tile powers every tile of the line, the line's demand is the sum of its tiles (`beltKw`), and a line takes one cable (`checkEnds`). The readout says `Line of N tiles powered through a cable at tile X` or `Not wired`. Belts that no cable reaches stay hand cranked.
- Cable length is `T.cableLen` (14 m): Grid Range adds 4 m a level, Superconducting Poles 8 m a level (`upgrades.js`). They no longer mean anything about range power. Sockets (`powerparts.js` `PORTS`, `GEN_KINDS.ports`): pole 10, generator 4 (Portable 2, Turbine 4, Plant 5, Station 6, Titan 8), breaker 6, battery 4, switch 2, hanging lantern 2, anything else 1. Plug points: `cables.socketOf` (the pole's junction box, the generator's top corner, otherwise the machine's own point) and a small socket drawn at every node and cable end; a machine with no cable shows a gold lamp within 14 m.
- Two generators add: a cable between two generators, or both into one pole, is one grid with the summed supply (`net.gens`, `net.gen`). The readout lists the generators wired together, supply, demand, each one's output and the part of it the demand takes (`carry = out x min(1, demand / gen output)`). A generator pushes nothing into a grid it is not wired to. Burn rule, unchanged: a generator burns its own fuel at its own full output whenever it has fuel (mode auto) or is needed (mode reserve), whatever the grid demands, so burn rates are per generator and independent of how many are wired together.
- Brownout, priority shedding, battery charge, auto and reserve modes, breakers and the meter are unchanged.
- Bots: `botfuel.js` asks for a generator only when its grid is in use (`net.demand`), which comes from the cables the player laid, never from a distance. Hall chalkboard `POWER NEEDS WIRES` and the first-generator hint teach it.
- Multiplayer: cables are shared ents (`cables` message), the host alone solves the grids, `packRow` now also sends the grids (and each generator's share) when a world has no power part, so a guest's readout of a pole or generator matches. The `cable` command is validated by `Cables.guestWhy` (real integer ids, the guest within 14 m of an end) and `connect` (length, sockets, belt line, stock).
- Saves: an old save whose machines were powered by range loads with its cable list intact and everything else unwired; nothing crashes (a malformed cable record is dropped).
- Cost: the solve is union-find over the nodes plus one pass over the consumers (belt lines are walked only when a cable ends on a belt). `wire.solve-cost` times 500 consumers, 60 nodes and 200 cables.
- Tests: `src/tests/wire_*.js` (prefix `wire.`) and `mp_wire.js` (prefix `mp.wire.`). The helpers of the old tests (`power_lib.js` `grid()`, `addons_lib`) lay cables instead of relying on reach.
- Audit fixes (`src/tests/audit_wires_*.js`, prefix `wires.audit.`): (1) the wire mesh drew at most 6,000 segments, 12 per cable, so past 500 cables the wires vanished; a cable now takes `segsFor(len)` segments (4 to 12) and the mesh holds 24,000. (2) `Cables.prune` and `detach` removed cables one at a time and re-solved the grid after each (300 solves for a collapse, 10 for a pole with ten cords); `removeMany` removes the lot and solves once. (3) A string of hanging lanterns hung off a closed switch lit only the first lantern; `power.js` `solve()` now carries the switch's grid down the lantern string (`lampNbr`). (4) A cable length that is not a number (an ent with no position) passed the `len > max` test and was laid for nothing; every length check is now `!(len <= max)`. (5) The cable tool only found a machine by the single point in its middle, so a door leaf, a jump pad, a truck dock slab or a Leveling Pad could not be aimed at from their edge; `Cables.shapePick` (set in `game.js`) asks the hammer's own pickers (`TRANSIT.pick`, `BUILD.pickBuilt`, `RAIL.pickRail`, `HAUL.pickFlat`, `ARCH.pick`) as well. (6) A guest may take a cable down only from within 14 m of an end (`Cables.guestMayRemove`), like laying one. (7) `checkEnds(A, B, fresh)`: the aim passes `fresh = false` so aiming a wire at a belt does not rebuild the whole belt adjacency every frame. (9) Taking a frame down gave back the fan hung on it but not the fan's cable (`dropMountedFans` now detaches with a refund; a collapse still refunds nothing). (10) Two belt lines joined after they were wired carry two cables, and a hanging lantern has a cord at each end: the line (or lantern) used to take the first cable's grid even when it was dead and the other cable ran to a live pole; `power.js` `netRank` now picks the grid with a burning generator, then one with a charged battery, and the first cable wins a tie (the line's demand is counted once). (11) `Cables.pwRows` sent every cable's power six times a second; it now sends only what changed, the whole list about every 3 s and at once after the list changes. (8) Texts that still taught range power are gone (`crafting.js` generator and pole blurbs, belt readouts `A pole near a generator makes it full speed`, the rail shuttle upgrade and station hint, the jump pad how-to, the truck dock readout); `wires.audit.no-text-teaches-range-power-any-more` scans every source file, `index.html` and `README.md` for the old phrases.

## 23. Bots that climb: multi-level navigation (user request, API notes for the call routes job that comes next)

Files: `src/botnav.js` (all of it), small hooks in `src/crew.js` (the walk, the steer, move, animate, the status words), `src/build.js` (`register` and `unregister` bump `g.botnavVer`), `src/stack.js` (the same in `addLadder` and `removeLadder`), `src/transit.js` (`people()` also lists the bots that are on a multi-level errand, and the door and lift add and remove bump the version), `src/game.js` (one more number per crew row). Tests: `src/tests/botnav_{ramp,stack,transit,misc}.js`, `mp_botnav.js`, helpers in `botnav_lib.js`. Nothing about a path is ever written onto a bot (it all lives in a WeakMap), so a save holds no path and a reloaded bot simply plans again.

- **The graph.** A node is a standing surface: a cell column at one height with a solid floor and 3 clear rows over it (`HEAD`), or the sloped surface of a ramp or a stair at the middle of a cell column. Kinds (`rec.t`): ground (plush or the hall floor), floor (a PAD or BULK cell under it: pads, foundations, catwalks, cube plates), ramp, stair, cab. Nodes are made on demand and cached per version, never from a scan of the world. Edges: `walk` (same height, 4 sides, diagonals only across flat floor with both sides open), `step` (one cell, 0.6 m, up or down: a drop of more than that is never an edge, which is the whole edge safety rule), `ramp` and `stair` (grade up to 0.8; onto a slope from something else only when the surface is within 0.36 m above the feet, the player's own lip rule in `walkStep`, so the flank of a ramp is a wall and the end of a stair beside a plate is open), `ladder` (the foot cell and the plate cell the ladder hangs on, both ways), `cabIn` / `ride` / `cabOut` (the elevator), and door cells (a door that has power, or that stands open, is passable at a small cost; a key lock never is).
- **The elevator.** Only a lift with power (`e.pw > 0.05`; a hand crank needs a person in the cab, so no power means the edge is removed), only between stops that `floorsOf` lists, only where `sweepBlocked` says the shaft is clear. The bot stands at the landing cell that has the call panel (`e.sg` row, side and slot), calls the row with `requestFloor`, waits (90 s at most), steps to the middle of the cab, asks for its floor with `requestFloor`, rides with its feet on `e.cy`, and steps off. It never boards a cab that is leaving and never asks a lift without power. Jump pads are not used on purpose: a launch lands where physics says and a bot that misses is on the wrong floor.
- **Doors.** A door is a gap while it is closed and cannot be opened for a bot. A powered door is told to open (`e.tgt = 1`), the bot waits (14 s at most), walks through, and a door without the sensor is closed again behind it. `transit.people()` lists the bots that are walking such a route (`g._botPeople`), so a door never closes on one and a cab never comes down on one. A door that is on the straight way from the bot to its goal makes a bot on the pile floor use the graph too.
- **The search.** A* with a cap of 6000 nodes (`NODE_CAP`), resumable, run by a queue that spends 1 ms a frame across the whole crew (`tick`, called first in `Crew.update`). Results are cached per (start node, goal) and are thrown away when the VERSION changes. When the cap is reached the search is not given up: if the goal's side is a small closed region (a flood from the goal runs dry without touching the start) the answer is none, otherwise the path to the node that came closest is returned as a leg (`partial`) and the bot plans the next leg from there, so a long errand works and a goal that nothing leads to is still reported. The reason for a none is read off the world (`why`: a door with a key lock, a door or lift with no power, else 'No way up').
- **The version** (`version()`, `invalidate(why)`) moves on: a PAD or BULK cell set or removed (`world.setCell` is wrapped once per world; a door's own cells are not news), a ramp, stair or ladder registered or taken down, a lift or door added or removed, and a poll four times a second (`signature`) of every lift's power, stops and cut, every door's lock, power and leaf. Digging plush does not move it; a bot checks the next node before it steps and plans again when the floor is gone.
- **Where the bot is driven.** The old code keeps the pile floor: a bot on the ground whose errand ends on the ground walks the trail, the straight line and the physics exactly as before. `walk` (the waypoint states: return, fwalk, chgwalk, dwalk) takes over when the bot stands on a built surface, or its goal is on one, or a door is on the way, or it has stood against something for 2.5 s; `steer` does the same for idle, low battery waiting, follow and haulgo. While driven, the bot's height follows the surface node by node (kinematic: it never floats and never falls), with a hop on a stair, a lean on a ramp, a climbing animation on a ladder, a call at the panel and a ride in the cab. `move()` skips a bot that was placed this way; any other bot standing on a built floor may not step to a spot whose floor is nearer than its body, so the old fuel code's side step at a machine never takes it off a rim.
- **Failure.** No path: one toast per reason per minute (`<Bot>: No way up`, or the lock, or the lift), the status line and head status read 'No way up', and the old rules apply. A bot on a built surface, or one stopped by a lock or a dead door or lift, holds still (the stuck timer then phases it as it always did); a bot on the pile floor that is merely stuck walks the old way and says nothing.
- **API** (`import * as NAV from './botnav.js'`, positions are `{ x, y, z }` with y the feet, a bot, or a logistics tile `{ i, j, k }`): `pathTo(bot, target, { r, dy, sync, cap })` returns `{ ok, state: 'ok' | 'pending' | 'none', partial, steps, len, why, ver }` (`pending` is finished by the queue; `sync: true` finishes it now); `reachable(from, to, opts)` is true only for a complete path; `stepList(path)` is one entry per node `{ x, y, z, kind, lift?, row?, door? }` with kind `start | walk | step | ramp | stair | ladder | cabIn | ride | cabOut`; `legs(path)` merges them into `{ kind, from, to, len, rise, n, lift?, row? }` (what a call route reads); `version()`, `invalidate()`, `isGround(pos)`, `nearestSafeDown(pos)` (where a phase should land a bot that cannot walk), `graph.nodeAt(pos)`, `graph.neighbors(node)`, `graph.surfacesAt(i, k, ylo, yhi)`, `stats()` (searches, hits, misses, nodes opened, ms), `setEnabled(false)` (every crew.js hook answers 'the old code': what the layer costs, or a bug hunt; `g._navDbg = []` logs each frame's decision). The fuel code may call `pathTo` / `reachable` in `choose` instead of the straight-line `walkClear` and the 2.2 m height rule; nothing in `botfuel.js` was edited for this.
- **Status words** (crew panel V, `statusLine`, `headStatus`, a guest too): Taking the ramp, Taking the stairs, Climbing, Waiting for the lift, Riding the lift, Waiting for the door, Finding a way, No way up. The host puts the code in crew row field 21; a guest builds the words from it.
- **Left alone on purpose.** Bots do not use jump pads. Flags on remains and caches, `crewBelt` laying and the dig itself are the digging bot's own business on the pile floor and are not routed. Machines placed inside plush (a generator in a solid block) have no floor to stand on; the old far-machine phase still handles them.

## 24. Soft slides: the small slide that flows and buries by what lands (user request, API notes)

User request: "soften the hit me off in avalanche, things should slide out from under me and slide me with it and slightly or majorly bury me depending." The old patch slide (used on low slopes, during the 30 s cooldown after a slab, and for players with Climbing Gear 3) hit the player for 12 to 17 hp and knocked them off. It is replaced by a soft slide, `src/softslide.js`, on the machinery of the climbing slab (`avalanche.js`, `start(pos, { soft: true, ... })`).

- **Trigger.** `game.climbRisk` rolls the footing as before (odds `min(0.6, (h - 6) / 45) x (1 - 0.25 climb) x (1 + 0.03 carried)`); the slab gets the first try above 19 m, the soft slide comes next. If nothing can flow (a flat bench, everything held by frames or ropes) the footing only shifts a little, with no damage.
- **Size.** `SoftSlides.size(pos, carry, tan, rnd)`: `e = 0.40 height + 0.16 steepness + 0.28 load + 0.10 luck` (each term 0 to 1: height from 8 to 40 m, slope tan 0.3 to 1.6, load as a share of the bag), `M = 60 x (700 / 60)^e` plush, a slab 3 to 9.6 m wide, 4 to 13 m long and 1 to 3 cells deep (about `2.5 W L D` cells). Small is under 110 cells (the weakest is 50 to 80), medium 110 to 420, large above. Gear (`gearOf`) shortens the flow (`flowT`, 1.6 to 4.2 s) and the travel bound (6 to 24 m) by up to 45 percent; it never changes M or what lands.
- **Ride.** The slab's own cracking and wave release (the crown starts 1.2 m above you, 0.2 s warning moving, 0.4 s standing), bodies driven down the face, frozen on the flat, placed at the travel bound or after `flowT + 4.5 s`. You are carried by the plush around you; a soft slide drives you only while there is plush round you, funnels you toward its centre line and slows you to a stop inside 80 percent of its travel bound, so you end inside the pile you rode. No hp from the slide at any point (`avalanche.shield`): crushing plush, the old "gave way" and the ride-end stop all cost 0; a real fall while carried costs at most 3 per landing and 6 in all (the old 4 and 12 stay for slabs). Half of what you carry drops in a slab; a soft slide drops a quarter to a half.
- **Landing and burial.** `start` records the column heights round the slide (`snap`); when the flow is over and everyone has stopped (at most 2.5 s) `land` counts, for each person in the runout (host player, guest, bots), the plush that really came to rest within 3 cells of them (6 for the one who rode) as `pool`, bounds it by the slide's size (`SS.BOUND`: small `0.08 to 0.19` of its cells, medium 24 to 36.9, large at least 42, scaled down to nothing for a pool under 12) and reads the depth from `SS.DEPTH = [5, 12, 22, 38]`: legs, waist, chest, under a cap. The plush is taken from the top of the landed pile (within 8 cells) and set round the person's cell column by column: a ring of 8 per level (stacking up from the ground on the downhill side), an outer plus ring from chest depth, a cap of two cells when plenty is left. Anything the ground already held beyond the size bound rolls off down the slope (`trim`). Cells plus bodies are conserved. A bot is only counted as buried that deep and lifted to the surface (`freeBots`).
- **Being buried.** `SoftSlides.update` (on every machine, for its own player, only after a soft slide and until you are out) reads the depth from the cells round you (`coverAt`: levels whose four sides are plush, plus a cap) and sets `p.buried` so the trapped system (air from the normal `60 + 30 airTank` s, the buried overlay, Space punches up) runs from chest depth. Walking speed is 60, 35, 15, 5 percent at legs, waist, chest, cap. At legs and waist walking at the way out shoves the cells there loose (0.35 s and 0.65 s a level: about a second); chest and cap need punches.
- **Multiplayer.** The host runs it; the messages are the slab's (`avwarn`, `avrun`, `avend`, `avride`, with `k: 's'` on the first and last) plus the `sslide` command from a guest, which the host judges again (the guest's own `pos` within 8 m and above 7 m, no Rope Anchor, one request per 5 s). The guest's burial is read from the synced cells; it arms its own watch on `avend`.
- **Tests.** `slide_soft_core.js` (`slides.soft-*`), `mp_slide_soft.js` (`mp.slide.soft-*`), with `slide_soft_lib.js`.

## 25. Call routes: a machine calls a bot, the route brings it back down (user request, API notes)

Files: `src/botroutes.js` (all of it), small hooks in `src/crew.js` (the import and install, `sync` marks a load, `chargerFor` asks the module, `update` ticks it, `think` gives it the first word, the status words) and `src/botfuel.js` (a machine on another level that the graph proves nobody can walk to is not offered to a bot), a few more exports in `src/botnav.js` (`noWay`, `drive`, `onNode`, `groundNow`, and `floor` / `ground` goal flags). Tests: `src/tests/botroutes_{flow,stack,charge,perf,soak}.js`, `mp_botroutes.js`, helpers in `botroutes_lib.js`.

- **A call** is the record `{ id, m (machine id), b (bot id), k ('fuel' | 'charge'), ph ('up' | 'down'), g ([x, y, z] of the last ground spot), f ([x, z] of the plush it was farming) }` in `S.botCalls` (a save keeps exactly that). One call per bot, any number per machine (a hopper's reservations in botfuel.js `reserved` bound the fuel calls, `SLOTS` = 2 bots bound the charge calls). A call opens by itself on the bot's next think when a fuel job is set (`b.fuelJob` in a state of `goto farm advance blocked fwalk fgive`) or a charge is on (`b.chg` in `chgwalk recharge`), and it follows the bot's errand: another errand replaces it and inherits `g` and `f`, so the way down is never lost between two errands.
- **The routes** are runtime only, never saved, never sent: `up` (from the bot to the machine) and `back` (from the machine, or once it is on its way down from the bot, to the ground spot and on to the farm spot) are Float64Arrays of cell indices (`(j * NZ + k) * NX + i`, which is 35 bits, so not an Int32Array). They are botnav's own paths read through `stepList`, found by the same budgeted queue and the same cache entry the driver walks, so the line the bot follows and the line on record are one. A pile floor to pile floor errand is a line of two cells. `RT.routeOf(call)` gives plain arrays, `RT.decode(id)` a cell.
- **Laid again** from the bot's own cell (never from the machine) when the version moved, the bot is more than 2 cells off every cell of the route, or the machine's cell changed, and at most every `REPLAN` = 1.5 s per bot and route. A bot in a cab or on a ladder is not asked about. `passed` counts the route cells the bot stood in; it only goes up.
- **The way down.** When the errand ends (full, gone, an order of the player's, a battery too low, a drop) the call turns `down` unless the bot already stands on the pile floor or the hall floor, and then it is removed at once. While `down`, `RT.think` (called first in `Crew.think`) walks the bot with `NAV.drive` to the ground spot and nothing else in the old state machine runs; the status reads Heading back down. At ground (`NAV.groundNow`: a node of kind ground, not a plate, a ramp, a stair or a cab, at rest) the call is removed and the bot is released: a dig goes on with `resumeDig` if the face is still there, else the nearest face, else home; a return re-plans its path from where the bot stands. A bot that cannot find a way down for 24 s is set down at the ground spot with a toast (the old phase home, as the last resort).
- **No way up.** A route that is proven to have no way for 1.2 s releases the call: one toast `<bot>: No way up` (or the lock or lift reason), the machine goes into `b.fuelBad` (or `b.chgBad`) for 60 s, other bots may take it, and the bot goes home or back to its dig. `botfuel.js` asks the graph first for a machine on another level (`farBlocked`), so a sealed catwalk is usually never a call at all. Pile floor to pile floor machines (a post of plush, a pit) keep the old walk and the old phase up.
- **Low battery.** `Crew.chargerFor` is now `RT.chargerFor`: the nearest station within range that holds charge, has a free slot (two bots charge at one station, one at each side, counted from the bots' `chg`), is not held back for this bot, and can be reached through the graph (asked only when the bot or the station is on a built surface). When stations exist but none can be reached the bot says so once a minute (`no way to a charger`) and goes home as before. A bot on a fuel walk that falls under 25%, or whose battery cannot cover the route up and the route back, calls `crew.lowBattery` (the nearest open charger first). A machine that someone else filled while the bot was on its way ends the job at once and the route turns down.
- **Cancel cases** are all one reconcile: the call is alive while the errand it was made for is alive. A machine removed, a bot removed (the call is dropped in `tick`), an order of the player's (`stand`, `follow`, `order`, `sendHome` clear the job), a battery that ran low and a host that leaves all end as `up` to `down`. A save and load keep the records and the bot's own fields; `afterLoad` checks them once the world is up, and a bot that stands on a built surface with no call gets a down call (the way down is owed). A guest never runs any of it.
- **Words.** Called to Generator A (state `fwalk` with a call), Going to charge (`chgwalk`), Heading back down (`down`, leading the line; the movement word follows it). Crew row field 21 carries `CODE.called` 9, `CODE.down` 10 or `CODE.charge` 11 when botnav has no word of its own (down always wins); a guest builds the same words, the machine's name from the fuel job id already in the row.
- **Debug.** `g.botnav.debugRoutes(true)` draws every call's `up` (orange, cyan for a charger) and `back` (green) as lines in the scene group `botRoutes` (depth test off, refreshed 2.5 times a second); `false` removes them; nothing is drawn otherwise. `g.botnav.routes()`, `.calls()`, `.stats()` (frames, ms, worst frame, lays, replans, opened, released, downs, giveups, landed) are for tests.
- **Cost.** A bot with no call and no errand costs one function call (`think`: a lookup and a test). Searches are botnav's (1 ms a frame for the whole crew); the route layer itself measured 0.022 ms a frame (worst frame 0.3 ms) with 30 bots, 10 machines and up to 24 calls at once over three levels, and the whole crew update cost 0.016 ms a frame more than with the layer off (`botroutes.perf`; 68 lays in 30 s for 30 bots). Tests that changed with it: `botnav.sealed`, `botnav.lift` (no power) and `botnav.audit.walk` (a wall across the pad) used to expect that a bot with no way up stands and waits for the stuck timer; the call is now released after 1.2 s and the bot goes home, so they check that instead.
- **Audit fixes.** (1) The player's own charge order (`Crew.intent`) is refused when both slots of a station are taken (it used to put a third bot on it). (2) A ground to ground route is a line of two cells and is not asked `displaced` (it was laid again every 1.5 s on a long floor walk). (3) A bot that is on a built floor in `goto`, `farm` or `advance` with no call (a follower that was then given a dig) gets a `down` call, checked twice a second, instead of standing at the edge for 25 s until the old beam phased it home. (4) `R.said` (the once-a-minute words) is pruned with the bots, and a loaded save never reuses a call id. Known and left: a guest sees `Heading back down` and not the ramp or ladder pose while a bot goes down (field 21 holds one number and the down word wins); a sixty bot crew that cannot reach any of twenty stations pays one 14 ms frame for the first round of reachability questions.

## 26. Charging Station needs a cable (user request: machines always need a cable)
The wired power rewrite had left the Charging Station running with no power (`crew.charger-needs-no-power`). It is now a normal consumer: `DEMAND.charger = 1` kW in `power.js`, always in the solver's consumer list, `pw` 0 until a cable runs to a live grid. `FUEL.chargerPowered(t)` (`botfuel.js`, `pw` over 0.05) is the one test of an open station: `botroutes.chargerFor` skips an unwired or dead one (so no call route and no walk), `thinkRecharge` lets a bot go when the cable is cut mid charge (`dryCharge`), a player order or the panel's Recharge now says `Not wired`, a scooping bot is not sent to fuel it (`starving`), and the readout (`chargerInfo`, plus the cable line from `cables.js`) says NOT WIRED or NO POWER. Plush still go into the hopper and become reserve whether or not it is wired. Tests: `crew.charger-needs-a-cable-from-a-live-grid-and-is-a-one-kw-consumer`, `crew.charger-with-no-cable-...`, `botfuel.a-charging-station-with-no-cable-...`, `botroutes.unwired.`; `liveGrid` in `charger_lib.js` gives a test station its own grid.

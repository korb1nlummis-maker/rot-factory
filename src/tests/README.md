The audit files are `audit_botroutes_{flow,perf,soak}.js` (`botroutes.audit.flow.`, `botroutes.audit.perf.`, `botroutes.audit.soak.`: a dying battery, a sealed platform, orders and removals mid-call, a third bot sent to a full station, a long floor walk that must not be laid again and again, sixty bots and twenty machines timed, three seeded soaks with plush and slot invariants) and `audit_botroutes_mp.js` (`mp.botroutes.audit.`: a guest replays a whole call). `botroutes_lib.js` has no default export: its `kit(ctx)` wraps the botnav kit and adds `callOf(bot)`, `follower()` (counts the frames a bot was not on its route), `dump()` (the call records) and `threeLevels(K)` / `twoLevels(K)` (the stacked bases of botnav.perf and botnav.stack). `RT.routeOf(call)` returns the runtime route arrays of a call as plain arrays, `RT.decode(id)` turns a cell index into `{ i, j, k }`, and `g.botnav.stats()` reads the route layer's milliseconds. The stack, perf and mp tests build their own world (WORLD_TESTS), the others use the build shell.

Wire audit (`audit_wires_core.js`, `audit_wires_aim.js`, `audit_wires_mp.js`; prefix `wires.audit.`, `wires.audit.aim.`, `wires.audit.mp.`): the adversarial pass over wire-only power. Cable loops and forged cable records never double count supply, taking things down in any order refunds each cable once, a factory with 1,400 cables still draws every wire, aiming a wire at a belt in a big factory stays cheap, a collapse that kills 300 machines prunes with one solve, a string of hanging lanterns hung off a switch follows the switch, two belt lines joined after they were wired (and a lantern with a cord to a dead and a live grid) run on the live grid whichever cable came first, taking a frame down returns the cable of the fan hung on it, a richer grid (two generators, a pole, a switch, a lantern string, a belt line) comes back from a save unchanged and forged cable records change nothing, the host sends only the cables whose power changed (`Cables.pwRows`), a cable to a thing whose place is not a number is refused, no player facing text (every source file, `index.html`, `README.md`, scanned as raw text) still teaches a range link, every wide machine (door leaf, jump pad, dock slab, giant arch pillar) can be aimed at with the cable tool because the hammer finds it by shape, and a guest cannot write cables or grid rows into the host, wire things that take no cable, wire with an empty stock or take a far cable down.

Belt feel (`beltfeel_arrows.js`, `beltfeel_plush.js`, `beltfeel_cord.js`, `beltfeel_bends.js`; prefix `beltfeel.`): what a player sees and does with belts and hoses. Arrows: the chevron texture of the bed (read from its canvas, the box UVs and the matrix of every placed bed), the cones of lifts, undergrounds, splitters, mergers and every aim and route preview are compared with each piece's own travel vector, and the pattern slides forward. Plush: every shape, palette, pattern and shiny variant, The One and a decoy, on a belt, a bend, a ramp, a lift and in a hose, is handed to the renderer as one small instance of its own shape and colour (the game's own `feedDynamic`), at any camera distance. Cord: held B and the mouse press-drag-release lay an L, an S and a U with the bends in, as long as the pieces held (nothing bought), carrying on from the open end; the gold end at the SORT bin and at a Depot Beacon, in the preview and when laid. Bends: the corner is a real quarter circle that meets its neighbours edge to edge, a cord round a corner in every facing puts the bend in, and ramps (up and down) meet bends and flat pieces at one height with the plush riding on their deck.
